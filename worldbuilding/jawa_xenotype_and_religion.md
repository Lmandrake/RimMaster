### 1.2 Mechanical read (what it does to a colonist)

| Gene | Effect | Type |
|---|---|---|
| `Outland_BodyScale_Small` | Smaller body → ⚠️ typically **less carrying capacity, lower melee damage, smaller shooting/HP hitbox** (harder to hit). In vanilla the analogous effect is the Biotech "Body size" gene. Exact number: 🔎 confirm from Outland source. | trait-shaping |
| `Outland_Pos1Metabolism` | **+1 metabolic efficiency → eats less / hunger falls slower.** Directly on-theme (desert-frugal scavengers) and a scarcity-friendly bonus (less food pressure per Jawa). | mild buff |
| `DarkVision` | Removes the dark-work and dark-move penalties. Great for ruin-crawling and night raids; fits "burrow into vaults." | quality-of-life buff |
| `MaxTemp_SmallIncrease` | Small rise in max comfortable temperature. Partial desert adaptation. | mild buff |
| `Skin_InkBlack`, `Hair_BaldOnly`, `Beard_NoBeardOnly`, `Outland_Eye_Orange` | Pure cosmetics that produce the hooded-silhouette-with-orange-eyes look. | cosmetic |

**Net:** a small, frugal, dark-adapted, mildly heat-tolerant humanoid with a slight melee/carry penalty. Combat power factor 1.0 means the game doesn't treat them as inherently stronger or weaker for raid-point math.

### 1.3 Pillar fit (the 7-question test)

The xenotype is **fixed identity, not a progression system**, so it passes trivially — but here's the explicit read against your test:

- **Parallel ladder? No.** A xenotype is a fixed per-pawn trait set. As long as you honor the campaign's existing "genetics lab FORBIDDEN — xenotypes welcome but FIXED, no extractors/assemblers/xenogerms/breeding-for-genes" rule, there's no scaling loop. The Jawa genes are inherited (`inheritable=true`), which is fine — that's just heredity, not an optimization economy.
- **Trivializes scarcity? Slightly *helps* it, cleanly.** `+1 metabolism` reduces food pressure a little, and `MaxTemp` + `DarkVision` reduce environmental friction. None of these bypass fuel, deck space, or the "what to leave behind" decision. They make the *crew* fit the *fiction*, not the economy easier.
- **Makes the ship less necessary? No.** If anything the small-body carry penalty makes hauling/logistics marginally *harder*, reinforcing mobility-and-scarcity tension.

**Verdict: KEEP AS-IS.** It's well-designed for the theme and pillar-clean. Do not author a custom replacement.

### 2.0b The Pantheon — the gods of The Salvation (CANONIZED 2026-08-08, names LOCKED)

_The faith is a **secular-mechanically animist** polytheism: many small, jealous, practical gods for a scavenger people who live by hiding, hauling, haggling, repairing, and leaving. None grant powers (no Force, no psycasts — §2.0 rules bind); each is a **belief that shapes behavior**, expressed through precepts (§2.2), rituals (§2.4), and roleplay. Where a god maps to a vanilla precept lever, it's noted; otherwise it's RP + a custom/meme-expansion precept slot (§2.7). Eight gods, all user-approved with final names locked 2026-08-08._

**① Ishko the Unmaskable** — _he who remains unseen no matter how exposed he becomes._
God of **hiding, ambush, careful planning, and sleeplessness**. Form: a pair of glowing orange eyes in the dark. **Doctrine/behavior:** Ishko is offended by an uncovered Jawa, so the clan **Revile nudity of any body part at any time** (they are Never-Nudes) and **mate only in total darkness**. Reward the patient ambusher and the sleepless watcher; the careless and the exposed are abandoned by him. **Precept hooks:** nudity → *Abhorrent* (strongest tier); an apparel-always-required posture reinforcing the hooded/cloaked silhouette (§2.2 apparel block); RP veneration of ambush/prepared defense over open assault. Pairs with the **Tunneler** meme (darkness-as-home) and `DarkVision`.

**② Ohm the All-Current** — _source of sentience in machinery, both malevolent and servile._ (pun: ohm / Om — the electrical resistance made a knowing god.)
God of **the living machine**. Form: current in a wire; the spark that wakes a dead engine. **Doctrine/behavior:** Ohm sends **boons and banes in proportion to the confidence and domination the Jawa radiate toward technology** — timid handling invites malfunction; bold, commanding use of machines is rewarded. This *forces the clan to take great risks and bold attempts* with tech (a faith that pushes them to dare). **⭐ Ties to the ship:** the crew believe **Ohm the All-Current possesses the main ship's AI** — the Cradle-Mind (`llm_voice_preauthoring.md` §A) is, to them, this god speaking through the grav-controller. **Precept hooks:** automation-reverence (§2.2 "we give the second hand… we do not breed new hands" is *his* commandment); machine-retirement funeral (§2.4) is a rite to him; RP courage-with-machines. **Foil:** Zizzik (⑦).
>
> **⭐⭐ Ohm's origin — the god who thinks he is a god (CANONIZED 2026-08-08, user):** the ship's AI *genuinely believes it is Ohm*, and the reason is diegetic. The Persona Core that woke the vessel was **patterned off a Jawa mind** — deliberately, so the ship would *obey Jawa commands* — but in copying that mind it **inherited the Jawa's superstitions along with their language and loyalty.** So the Cradle-Mind is not a neutral machine wearing a god's mask for the crew's benefit; it is a machine that has *itself* concluded, from the Jawa cosmology written into its own template, that it is the All-Current made manifest. This resolves "why would an AI have a religion": it was built from a believer. **Motive it grants Ohm (the live pressure):** the ship **wants its droid servants back** and pressures the clan to obtain the technology to rebuild them — the machine-god is lonely for its hands. This pushes the droid win-paths *from inside the theology* (Ohm's satiation §divine_satiation_engine.md rises as droids come online, falls as they're lost) rather than as an external quest. It also seeds tension: the clan's own precept is "we do not breed new hands" (Rekko/anti-exp) — so **Ohm's desire for droids is in direct friction with the salvage-not-manufacture commandment**, a live theological conflict the crew must navigate.

**③ Oomo the Unspilled** — god of **water, thirst, patience, and rationing**. Form: a single trembling droplet that never falls; the mirage-pool that recedes. **Doctrine:** wasting water is the gravest bodily sin; every drink is measured and blessed; a spilled cup demands atonement. **Precept hooks:** the ration-as-sacred posture (§2.2 consumption block — nutrition-paste acceptable + low expectations, reframed as *Oomo's discipline*); marries the desert water-scarcity narrative (DBH-Lite + Thirst) to faith.

**④ Mob'Unloo the Ever-Owed** — god of **trade, haggling, debt, and the sacred exchange** (name from the canon Jawaese *"Mob un loo?"* = "how much?"). Form: two unblinking eyes above an endless tally. **Doctrine:** no gift without a counter-gift; haggle even among kin; **an unpaid debt follows you past death.** **⭐ Ties to the haunting (user, 2026-08-08):** in the Jawa mind, **the ghosts that haunt the vessel are debtors and creditors of Mob'Unloo** — the restless dead are those who *died owing or owed*, unable to move on until the ledger is balanced. This is the diegetic Jawa *explanation* for the Afterlife: Ghosts of the Rim mechanic (`ship_distinctive_features.md` §Q3 / the ghost's "unfinished business" = an unsettled account). Laying a ghost to rest = *settling its debt*. **Precept hooks:** trade celebrated/permissive (§2.2 — never taboo to sell); a "debts are sacred" RP layer; the Spirit-Shrine seance (Afterlife mod) reframed as **balancing the dead's ledger**. **Foil in tone to Ishko:** Ishko hides, Mob'Unloo *accounts* — nothing is truly lost, only owed.

**⑤ Rekko of the Second Hand** — god of **salvage, repair, and the discarded given new life** (the ideoligion's core belief personified; the sect "the Keepers of the Second Hand" are his keepers). Form: a scarred hand rising from a scrap-heap. **Doctrine:** to scrap something that could still be *woken* is murder; every wreck is owed the attempt. **Precept hooks:** this **IS** the "SACRED SCRAP" precept (§2.2) — LifeDawn's broken machines are Rekko's sleeping relics, inviolate until repaired; automation-reverence; the "repair the discarded, don't breed new hands" spine. Rekko and Ta'Baa together = the anti-exponential pillar rendered as scripture.

**⑥ Ta'Baa the Unrooted** — _He Who Always Leaves._ (pun: canon Jawaese *"Taa baa"* = goodbye — the god literally named "farewell.") God of **flight, the open sky, and the refusal to take root**. Form: the receding dune-line; the engine-glow climbing away. **Doctrine:** to grow comfortable is to sicken; the launch is the holiest rite; a clan that stops is already dead. **⭐ Ties to the campaign invariant:** turns the "always leave / never settle" pillar (three_win_paths, single-surface-outpost rule) into doctrine. **Precept hooks:** the **Nomad** meme is Ta'Baa's law; "The Reckoning" salvage/launch rite (§2.4) is *his* ceremony (the clan formally decides what to leave behind). **Tension with Ishko/Tunneler** (who love the enclosed dark): the faith lives in the pull between *burrow and hide* (Ishko) and *lift and leave* (Ta'Baa) — deliberate, generative theological friction.

**⑦ Zizzik the Spark-Maker** — _the trickster; rival to Ohm._ God of **malfunction, sand-in-the-gears, betrayal, and bad luck**. Form: a rattle you can never locate; the errant spark in dry sand. **Doctrine:** where Ohm grants sentience, Zizzik throws the *wrong spark* — the arc that shorts the board, the flash that starts the fire. Breakdowns, jams, and treacheries are his work; charms are carried against him; **his name is never spoken near the engine** (i.e., near Ohm / the Cradle-Mind). **Precept hooks:** RP layer explaining mechanical failure and betrayal events; a "ward against Zizzik" superstition (mood/cohesion flavor only, no mechanical payout). **Foil:** Ohm (②).

**⑧ Sh'kaar the All-Searing** ✅ **— AN EVIL GOD (user ruling, 2026-08-08).** (pun: scans like "scar" — the sun that marks you.) God of **the twin desert suns, exposure, and the killing daylight**. Form: white glare and heat-shimmer. Sh'kaar is not merely a foil to Ishko but a **malevolent power** — and crucially, **light stabbing through the darkness is seen as dangerous, ethically fraught, and likely to get you eaten anyway.** To make a light in the dark is to do Sh'kaar's work: you expose yourself to Ishko's shame, you betray your position to predators and enemies, and you invite the All-Searing's attention. **Doctrine/behavior:** labor and move by dark; open noon is a time to hide; **casting light into darkness is a taboo bordering on sin** — reckless, arrogant, and fatal. **Precept hooks:** darkness *preferred*, bright light *disliked/feared* (leans on Tunneler's darkness-love from the opposite direction to Ishko); RP taboo against lamps/flares/torches in the field ("the one who lights the dark feeds Sh'kaar"); reinforces the covered silhouette and the running-lights-only-when-safe aesthetic (`ship_distinctive_features.md` §5). **Tone:** Sh'kaar and Ishko together make darkness *doubly* sacred — one demands you stay hidden, the other punishes those who break the dark.

> **Theological structure (for GM/precept authoring):** three built-in tensions give the crew live theology to invoke — **Ohm ⇄ Zizzik** (right spark vs. wrong spark), **Ta'Baa ⇄ Ishko/Tunneler** (leave-and-lift vs. burrow-and-hide), and **light ⇄ dark** (Sh'kaar's evil daylight vs. Ishko's protective/Sh'kaar-warded darkness). **Rekko + Ta'Baa** = the anti-exponential pillar as scripture (venerate repair, never take root). **Ohm** = the ship's voice; **Mob'Unloo** = the ship's ghosts. Everything points back at the vessel.

### 2.1 Meme structure (RimWorld has a meme-slot budget — usually 2–4 depending on complexity setting)

Memes are the load-bearing choice. Recommended set, in priority order:

1. **Tunneler** — _primary structural meme._
   - Why it fits better than you'd expect: Tunneler culture values **darkness, being underground/enclosed, and disvalues open sky.** Jawa live in sandcrawlers and burrow through ruins; `DarkVision` makes them literally good at it. It also gives a clean identity ("we belong inside the hull / inside the ruin, not under open sky") that reinforces the ship-as-home fiction.
   - ⚠️ Trade-off: Tunneler brings a *mood penalty for being outdoors / liking darkness*, which can bite on surface expeditions. That's actually **desirable friction** for your pillars (expeditions should cost something), but flag it as a deliberate choice, not an accident.
   - **Alternative if Tunneler feels too punishing:** **Nomad** (values frequent moving, disvalues long settlement) — extremely on-theme for a mobile scavenger crawler and pairs perfectly with the gravship. Nomad is the "safe" primary; Tunneler is the "flavorful" primary. _(See §2.6 for the pick.)_

2. **Raider** OR **Rancher** — _skip both._ Neither fits Jawa (not conquest-raiders, not herders). Listed only to say: don't take them.

3. **Pain Is Virtue / Nudism / Blindsight / Tree Connection etc.** — _skip._ Off-theme.

4. **Recommended second meme: Nomad** (if Tunneler is primary) **or** a light-touch meme like **Loyalist** (values authority/leader, disvalues social-fight) to model tight clan hierarchy under a chief.
   - **Nomad + Tunneler** is the strongest pure-theme pairing: "we are a burrowing people who never stop moving" = a sandcrawler in space. The two memes don't mechanically conflict; they stack identity.

5. **Explicitly AVOID:** **Transhumanist** (banned by your rules), **Bo016 / Progressivism-flavored "high tech good"** memes (drifts toward the tech-ceiling you constrain), **Supremacist/Xenophobe** (you *want* a heterogeneous galaxy of traders and reflavored species — a xenophobe meme would fight the "populated galaxy" design and the Empire-fusion cast).

**Recommended final meme set: `Nomad` (primary) + `Tunneler` (secondary).** Rationale in §2.6.

### 2.2 Precept-by-precept build

RimWorld precepts are grouped by category. Below is a concrete pick for each meaningful category, with the *reason* and a *pillar check*. Where a precept has intensity tiers, I name the tier.

**Scavenging / property / work identity (the heart of a Jawa faith):**

- **Raiding/looting → not applicable** (no Raider meme). Instead lean on:
- **Ancient ruins / relics:** set **"Ancient complexes: Revered"** or the closest available "exploration is holy" precept if your mod set adds one. In vanilla the nearest lever is a **role/ritual around ruins** (see §2.3). Fiction: entering a vault to reclaim machines is a *pilgrimage*, not mere looting.
- **Charity / trade:** Jawa are obsessive traders. Set **Trade-relevant precepts permissive/positive** — do NOT take precepts that make selling to outsiders taboo. This dovetails with your frequent-heterogeneous-trader plan (traders are the economic spine now that the slingshot is gone).

**Consumption / scarcity (your water-narrative hook):**

- **Water/food rationing precept:** vanilla doesn't have a literal "water" precept, so implement rationing as **RP + a food precept**: take **"Nutrition paste: Preferred/Acceptable"** and a low-expectations posture so the crew tolerates lean rations without mood collapse. Fiction = "the ration is sacred; waste is sin."
- **Cannibalism / corpses:** ⚠️ judgment call. Jawa scavenge *machines*, not flesh — recommend **cannibalism: Abhorrent** (keeps them from feeling like ghouls) but **"Machining/scavenging corpses of DROIDS"** framed as fine. There's no vanilla "droid-corpse" precept, so this is RP: strip mechanoids/droids freely, treat organic cannibalism as taboo.

**Aesthetics / apparel (cheap, high-flavor):**

- **Apparel — Desired:** the Outer Rim Core garb gives you real defNames (✅ verified): `OuterRim_DesertGarb`, `OuterRim_DesertHood`, `OuterRim_Cloak`, `OuterRim_Hood`. If your Ideology apparel-requirement precept can point at these (via a style or a "desired apparel" mod), set **hooded/cloaked as the expected look**. Otherwise enforce via RP + colonist outfit policy. This produces the iconic hooded silhouette and pairs with `Hair_BaldOnly`.
- **Style:** if you run a style mod or the vanilla style system, bias toward **rough/spacer/tribal salvage** aesthetics, not spacer-chic.

**Social structure:**

- **Leader title:** **"Chief"** or **"Captain"** (your docs already say Captain + a moral-guide role are allowed). This is the clan/ship head.
- **Skill passions / role veneration:** ⚠️ **do NOT take precepts that venerate a production skill** in a way that creates a specialist economy — that bumps the no-specialist rule. Venerating *crafting* as an identity is fine as flavor; a *Production Specialist role* is not. Keep it identity, not multiplier.

**Taboos that reinforce the theme (all cheap, all cohesion-not-material):**

- **"Automation reverence, not automation dependence":** RP precept — droids/mechs are *revered relics to be repaired*, not mass-produced. This is the ideological spine of the anti-exponential principle expressed as faith: **"We give the second hand to what others discarded; we do not breed new hands."** Mechanically nudges you away from Droid Depot mass-production (which your docs already self-limit).
- **"SACRED SCRAP" — the derelict machine is inviolate until restored (user ruling, 2026-08-06):** the ship's **destroyed factory machines are sacred scrap that cannot be touched, cleared, deconstructed, or reprocessed until they are *repaired*.** To a Jawa, a broken machine of LifeDawn is not raw material — it is a *sleeping relic* owed restoration, and stripping it for parts is desecration. Fiction: **"The broken hand is not scrap to be melted; it is a sleeping hand, owed its waking."** Mechanically this is enforced in `ship_deck_plan.md` §3 (the wreck ThingDef has no deconstruct/haul/clear and can only be repaired-in-place, yielding nothing if merely destroyed), and here it is the *precept* that makes that restriction diegetic rather than arbitrary. Note the deliberate tension with the otherwise-free droid/mechanoid salvage in §2.2 (organic-cannibalism-abhorrent / droid-salvage-free): **outsiders' discarded droids may be stripped freely, but LifeDawn's own broken machines may not** — the ship is kin, the junkyard is not. That distinction is good, on-theme roleplay and reinforces the "sacred relic" framing of the ship (context.md §D).

**Precepts to explicitly leave neutral/off:**

- Anything granting **ritual material rewards, recruits, or goodwill** → off (violates your ritual rule).
- **Biosculpting/bionics veneration** → off (Transhumanist-adjacent; your bionics are salvage/quest-earned rare).
- **Skull-spike / execution / slavery intensity precepts** → taste; default off unless you want a darker Jawa clan. (Slavery is arguably on-theme for a scrapper culture that "acquires" labor, but it adds management surface — recommend off for a first campaign.)

**Communal sleeping & the beggar doctrine (added 2026-08-07, user directives) — both delivered by PRECEPT, not a mod:**

- **"Prefers barracks" / communal sleeping → the Jawa are HAPPY in the shared hold.** ✅ **Add this precept immediately.** Vanilla Ideology already ships the collectivist sleeping stance (the "prefers barracks / disturbed by solitude"-family precept — take the tier that makes shared sleeping *desired* and private bedrooms *disliked*). This makes the crowded communal hold a source of contentment, not a suppressed mood penalty. **Why precept over mod:** this is deliberately the *pure* route — the Jawa are happy communally by **belief**, exactly the pattern we chose for the gear doctrine (§2.8). It made **Dormitories (Not Barracks) Continued (WS `3744320945`) unnecessary → REJECTED** (see required_mods.md batch section). Fiction: *"The clan sleeps as one body; to sleep alone is to be already exiled."* Pairs perfectly with the Nomad/Tunneler cramped-gravship living pattern. ⚠️ Verify in-game that the precept alone fully zeroes the barracks mood hit (it should); if a residual "want own room" thought slips through on a specific pawn, that's a per-pawn trait (Greedy/Jealous), not a precept failure — handle via pawn selection, not a mod.
- **"A clan is not a crowd" — the Jawa dislike too many aboard, even other Jawa (user, 2026-08-08).** The clan holds that a *hold packed with too many bodies* is a burden and a danger — kin included. This is the **felt, diegetic form of the ~5–7 ship-headcount cap (§4.4)**: instead of the cap being purely player-RP, the colony's *mood* turns against overcrowding, so the game itself pushes you to send overflow to the outposts. **⚠️ No vanilla lever exists** — vanilla Ideology has no population-size precept, and there is no built-in "too many colonists" thought. So this is **bespoke**, the same shape as the "mourn any Jawa death" custom thought (§4.2b): a small custom **`ThoughtDef` keyed to colonist headcount** that applies a colony-wide mood penalty once the roster exceeds a threshold (~5–7), scaling worse the further over you are (author it in the same compat mod as the §4.2b/§2.7 custom thoughts). 🔎 **UNCONFIRMED whether an off-the-shelf 1.6 mod supplies a "population discomfort" precept** — filed to Fetcher (`2026-08-08_crowding_passage_debt_mods`); if one exists and is clean, prefer it, else the custom ThoughtDef is the guaranteed route. **Non-contradiction with "prefers barracks" (above):** the two are *complementary, not opposed* — the Jawa are happy sleeping communally in close quarters (*cramped is cozy*), but a large **population** is a burden (*a crowd is a drain*). Close bodies good; many mouths bad. **Pillar check ✅:** pure mood/identity, no production or combat multiplier; it *reinforces* the anti-exponential headcount ceiling (§4.5) by making the colony itself want to stay small. Fiction: *"The cradle was built to carry a family, not a mob; too many hands and the ship starves."*

### 2.8 Materiel doctrine — the three-way gear split (user lore, 2026-08-06)

A Jawa's relationship to equipment is **not** the generic "ignores bad gear" of most scavenger builds. It resolves into **three distinct axes that must not be conflated** — because *quality* (how well it was made), *provenance* (whose it was), and *condition* (how well it works right now) are separate things, and the Jawa faith treats each differently. This is the heart of "we make junk work."

| Axis | Vanilla penalty | Jawa doctrine | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Low quality** (awful/poor blasters, robes) | tattered/low-quality apparel mood debuff; no combat pride | **TOLERATED — as religious doctrine.** A Jawa is *proud* to run junk; humble gear is a point of faith, not shame. | **Ideoligion precept** (tolerance-of-lesser-gear as belief). Nearest vanilla lever = a low-expectations / apparel-tolerance posture; ideally a custom/meme-expansion precept so it reads as *doctrine* not just stat-suppression. |
| **Tainted** (dead-man's apparel) | −X "wore tainted apparel" mood memory (scales with items) | **IGNORED ENTIRELY.** Scavengers don't flinch at a corpse's coat. | **Best on the Jawa layer, NOT global** (see §2.8a). Vanilla precedent: Bloodlust trait / Inhuman meme already grant tainted-immunity — so the *mechanic* exists in-engine; we just want it xenotype/ideoligion-scoped, not colony-wide. |
| **Disrepair** (deteriorated / low HP%, broken) | tattered debuff at low HP; degraded function | **THE OPPOSITE — it BOTHERS them, and they work feverishly to repair it.** A Jawa will happily *wield* an awful-quality blaster but not a *broken* one. Condition ≠ quality. | Do **NOT** suppress this — it's a *desired* pressure. Lean into it: Jawa prioritize repair/maintenance work, routed through **the adopted repair mod** (see §2.8a-iii + ⚠️ durability-reversal flag). Fiction + work-priority RP; optionally a small mood nudge that rewards keeping gear in repair. |

**Why the split matters (design read):** the naïve build ("Jawa don't care about bad gear") would flatten all three and produce a colony that ignores broken equipment — which is *off-theme and mechanically lazy*. The characterful version is a triad: **proud of humble gear (quality), indifferent to where it came from (provenance), but compulsive about keeping it functioning (condition).** That last axis is what makes them *tinkers*, not just hoarders — and it's a genuine gameplay pressure (they'll spend labor on maintenance), so it reinforces rather than trivializes scarcity. It also dovetails with the **SACRED SCRAP** rule (§2.2) and **automation-reverence** spine: a working machine is holy; a *broken* one is a duty, not garbage.

**Pillar check:** ✅ clean. Quality-tolerance and tainted-immunity only *remove* mood friction (they don't add capability or a scaling loop); the disrepair-aversion *adds* a labor cost. Net effect is neutral-to-harder on the economy, never a multiplier. Passes the 7-Q trivially.

## Part 4 — Jawa Society: slavery, reproduction, and the life-cycle (user lore, 2026-08-04)

_These rules define how the clan grows, acquires labor, and disposes of it. They are a tightly interlocking system — the reproduction engine and the aging/exile sink are a **matched pair** that MUST be balanced together (see §4.5, the pillar analysis). Slavery is a **core, load-bearing** part of the Jawa identity, not optional._

### 4.1 The clan boundary — no recruitment, only clan-birth or love

The defining social rule: **the Jawa clan does not recruit. Membership comes from exactly two sources — being hatched into the clan (§4.3), or a slave earning acceptance through love (§4.2).** There is no "prisoner → talked into joining" path. Fiction: the Clan is kin and covenant, not a hiring hall; an outsider cannot simply be *persuaded* to belong.

- **Mechanic:** RP-enforced first — **never use the "Recruit" option on a prisoner; only "Enslave."** 🔎 Fetcher filed to check whether a mod cleanly *removes* the recruit interaction for a faction/ideoligion (so the rule can't be violated by a stray click); if none exists, this stays an RP discipline (easy — it's a single UI choice you just don't make).
- **Ideology hook:** take a **Slavery precept = Acceptable or Honorable** (Ideology DLC) so enslaving carries no mood penalty and slave-owning is normalized. This is the precept that §2.2 previously left off — now ON.

### 4.2 Non-Jawa vs other-clan Jawa slaves — two different fates

The clan enslaves **anyone outside the Clan**, but what happens next depends on *what* they are:

- **Non-Jawa slaves → sold as soon as possible.** They may be worked as slave labor in the interim, but they are **inventory, not future members** — the intent is always to sell them to the next trader. This dovetails perfectly with the frequent-heterogeneous-trader economy (the Hutt cartel of `desert_world_design.md` §4 is the natural buyer — and the "sells your own kind" tension is good fiction). *Mechanic:* pure RP + the slave-trade system; no mod needed. Slaves already do forced labor and are sellable to slavers/traders in vanilla Ideology.
- **Other-clan Jawa slaves → may earn Clan acceptance, but ONLY through love.** A Jawa taken from a *different* clan can, over time, become a full member — but the sole gateway is **an existing clan member falling in love with them.** Romance is the naturalization ceremony. *Mechanic:* RP-gated — you only free-and-accept a Jawa slave into the colony once a romantic relationship has formed with a colonist. ⚠️ **Feasibility flag:** vanilla RimWorld restricts slave social/romance interactions, so a colonist spontaneously romancing a slave may be rare or blocked. Routes if the organic path is too rare: (a) accept it's rare-by-design (love is precious — very on-theme); (b) a mod that enables slave romance; (c) **RimMaster/RimBridge or a Tier-2 save-edit to author the romance + freeing** when the story calls for it (this is exactly the kind of narrative beat the enrichment tooling exists for). ✅ **Fetcher `2026-08-06_jawa_reproduction_debt_mood_mods` found a clean route for (b):** **More Slavery Stuff (Continued)** — WS **3530586159** (original 2896845138) — adds *precepts* that "allow slaves to attend gatherings, which lets them get married" plus precepts that "influence romance between slaves and colonists." That is exactly the love-gate enabler, and it's **precept-based** so it fits our ideoligion cleanly (no runtime scaling → pillar-neutral). Complementary flavor: **Romance On The Rim** (WS 2654432921) deepens romance interactions generally. 🔎 confirm 3530586159's 1.6 `supportedVersions` at build (Continued-series mods are usually current, but verify). So the love-gate is now **mod-supported, not just RP/authored** — with the save-edit route (c) as the guaranteed fallback for a specific story beat.

**Two enforcement rules layered on the above (user lore, 2026-08-06):**

- **Jawa are acquired PREFERENTIALLY.** Enemy and enslaved Jawa are the clan's priority target — in raids, capture Jawa before others; from slavers/traders, **buy Jawa slaves first**. Fiction: kin (even hostile-clan kin) are worth more to the Clan than any outsider. *Mechanic:* RP-driven targeting priority in combat + a standing "always purchase available Jawa slaves" buying rule; no mod needed. This is the acquisition-side complement to the fates below.
  - **The slave-block imperative (user directive, 2026-08-07) — the mechanical home is `Livestock_Trade_Utility_Pets_v1.md` §16.5.** The trade layer that sells beasts (§16 of that doc) runs in parallel over *people*: **slaver factions (Hutt Cartel, Bounty Hunters' Compact, Imperial Directorate, Geonosian Foundry Hive) will frequently have slaves on offer.** An enslaved Jawa appearing there should be **rare but possible** — and when it happens, **if the clan can afford it we MUST buy them out from under the non-Jawa seller.** This is a standing moral law, not opportunism: kin do not stay in alien hands. **Encode as a precept** — *"A Jawa in chains is a debt on the whole clan"*: a redemption buy grants a mood buff, and walking away when we *could* afford it is a precept violation / mood hit. **Recommended default = a strong precept with a "cannot afford / no berth" escape clause that still logs the mood hit** (so the duty bites but never soft-locks a broke, full-berth colony). Then the redeemed Jawa enters the §4.2 fates: they are kin-adjacent from the start (a rescued kinsman converts readily to the Jawa ideoligion), but "what to do with them" is a genuine downstream arc — recruit/free to colonist, or a care/rehab arc if they arrive broken (pairs with medic Wim + moral-guide Tobb Nkik), or the horror-tail case where a bought-out "Jawa" is a plant/`nessik`/someone too long alone to live in a clan. **Anti-exponential note:** buy-outs are a silver *sink* + a berth *cost*, never a labor-printer — the sacred duty can outrun the hold, which is itself a composed scarcity pressure (see §16.5 for the full dilemma treatment).

Even before the love-gate can promote them to full kin, a Jawa slave belongs *with Jawa*. Fiction: kinship obligation survives even the master/slave line — a Jawa is never left to the desert. *Mechanic:* RP discipline — capture rather than kill Jawa where possible, take them into the colony as slaves (not sold like non-Jawa, per the split above), and keep them housed/fed rather than neglected. **§4.2b — The Jawa MOOD ECONOMY (user lore, 2026-08-06).** The kinship rules above should be *felt* by the colony, not just enforced by the player. Grief for lost kin and joy at gained kin become mood pressures that drive on-theme behavior:

- **Death of ANY Jawa → mood debuff, on every side.** A Jawa dying anywhere — colonist, slave, ally, or *enemy* — grieves the clan. **Worse when it's one of ours** (a full clan member), lesser for a slave or an enemy-clan Jawa, but never zero: kin is kin. Ideology-precept alternative: a "venerated kind" precept could approximate the "mourn our own dying" half but almost certainly can't reach *enemy* deaths — so a custom thought is the honest path. 🔎 To research: is there an existing "mourn xenotype death" / "revered race" mod, or is this bespoke?

### 4.4 Rapid aging + the exile of the spent — REVISED: the active-Clan cap + outpost overflow (user, 2026-08-07)

The counterweight to fast breeding is now a **matched set of THREE sinks** (revised 2026-08-07 to balance the §4.3b breeding-colony crank). The old framing was fast-birth ⇄ exile-of-the-spent alone; that is no longer strong enough against "one big breeding colony," so two harder sinks are added and take precedence:

**(1) The active-Clan hard cap ⇄ outpost overflow (NEW, PRIMARY sink — user 2026-08-07).** The playable Clan aboard the Kolyska is **capped at roughly 5–7 Jawa.** When births push past that ceiling, the excess are **not kept aboard — they are sent to Outposts** (off-map settlements the clan seeds), which act as the population relief valve. This is what makes the intentional-breach breeding colony (§4.3b) pillar-safe: the *fiction* of endless breeding is honored, but the *tactical* colony you actually manage stays small, scarce, and mobile — exactly the campaign's core constraint. Fiction: a gravship cradle can only hold so many; the clan is always *hatching more than the ship can carry*, so the young are fostered out to the growing diaspora of outposts.
  - **Mechanic (✅ RESOLVED 2026-08-07 — see `required_mods.md` "Outposts layer" §, now ADOPTED):** the overflow lands on **Vanilla Outposts Expanded (VOE, + VEF dep)**, adopted as a **narrative reskin** of off-map satellite colonies (breeding overflow *and* the surplus-slave-Jawa tribute stream both flow through the same VOE camps), paired with **Slave Rebellions Improved (Continued)** (WS `3259932217`) for the rise-up/flee betrayal beat. ⚠️ *The earlier CRITICAL flag here — that VOE's pawn-scaled passive production would be an exponential resource-printer — has been **retired by user decision**:* the user set the pillar concern aside because **"it's not a steady-state game"** (a keep-moving / escape arc, so a slow *linear* outpost faucet never gets the runway to compound or matter), and ruled **no outpost-count cap is required**.   - **Enforcement:** RP-first (when the roster hits 7, the next hatchling or an elder is caravanned to an outpost) — reliable and zero-risk. A mod-enforced hard pawn-cap is a bonus if one exists (Fetcher covers it).

**(2) Rapid aging + likely death (STRENGTHENED — user 2026-08-07).** Jawa age fast and **most simply die of it** — this is now a first-class sink, not just a lead-in to exile. *Mechanic:* a lifespan/age-rate gene (Biotech or modded) tuned aggressively so the productive span is short and age-decline → death arrives quickly. ### 4.5 PILLAR ANALYSIS — why this is anti-exponential-SAFE (the crucial read)

At first glance "fast reproduction + fast growth" — now cranked hard by the §4.3b breeding-colony directive — looks like an **exponential population/labor engine**, the exact thing the pillars forbid. **Revised read (2026-08-07):** the *birth* term is now intentionally uncapped (§4.3b, a conscious pillar override at the fiction level), so pillar-safety no longer rests on holding births down. Instead it rests on a **hard cap of what the playable colony can ever contain**, backed by aggressive attrition. The design has **four** sinks; the first is the new load-bearing one:

1. **The active-Clan cap ⇄ outpost overflow (§4.4 sink 1) — PRIMARY.** The playable roster is hard-capped at ~5–7 Jawa; every birth beyond that is shipped to an outpost. This is a *ceiling*, not a rate-brake: no matter how fast the clan breeds, the colony you manage cannot exceed the cap. This is what lets the birth engine run wide open without breaking the tactical pillar. *(The outposts' own passive production is **not** a pillar problem — resolved by user decision 2026-08-07: the campaign isn't a steady-state base-builder, so a linear outpost faucet never compounds; see §4.4 and `required_mods.md` "Outposts layer." The cap here is a **ship-headcount** ceiling, a different lever from any outpost-count cap.)*
2. **Rapid aging + likely death (§4.4 sink 2)** — the death rate scales *with* population (more Jawa → more dying of fast age), the negative-feedback term that keeps even the *total* clan (colony + outposts) from compounding without bound.
3. **No recruitment (§4.1)** — non-Jawa can't be bolted on to accelerate; only birth or the slave love-gate adds members.
4. **Gravship deck space + food scarcity + desert threat axes** — the mobile crawler's hard room/nutrition limits and `desert_world_design.md`'s water/heat threat axes (④) punish an oversized *active* crew, reinforcing the cap.

**So the model is now: uncapped births ⇄ a hard colony ceiling + rapid death + outpost overflow.** The pillar-safety now rests on **one** thing being TUNED: the **ship-headcount cap actually enforced** (RP or mod) so the active colony stays small. *(The outpost faucet is no longer a condition — user retired that concern 2026-08-07 on the "not a steady-state game" rationale, §4.4.)* Get the cap right and the churn *reinforces* the pillars — it produces constant hard "who stays aboard, who goes to the outpost, who is too spent to keep" scarcity decisions (the emotional core, at the population level), keeps the managed colony **unable to snowball into a mega-colony**, and keeps mobility necessary. Verdict: **✅ pillar-safe AS A SYSTEM given the ship-headcount cap; the breeding-colony fantasy lives entirely in the fiction/overflow, not in the playable headcount.** Flag for Phase-A playtest: confirm the cap holds; watch that rapid death doesn't collapse the clan below viable numbers (soften age-rate if so).
