B Unit dots — one mark per item count = number of marks · colour = state RECOMMENDED
RowDecideBuildCheck
SHOWS

Magnitude and composition at once, with no scale to learn — thirteen dots is thirteen items. An empty cell is visibly, honestly empty. One red dot in a field of grey is impossible to miss, which is exactly the thing you currently have to go hunting for.

COSTS

Degrades past roughly 30 marks per cell; today's worst is 22, so there is headroom but not unlimited. Needs a wrap rule and a "+n" overflow chip if a cell ever runs away.

C Proportional stacked bar length = total items · segments = state STRONG SECOND
RowDecideBuildCheck
SHOWS

Magnitude by length and composition by segment, and it scales to any count without breaking. Comparing two rows' total weight is easier here than with dots.

COSTS

Length is relative to the busiest cell, so the whole grid rescales when one cell grows — a row can appear to shrink on a day it did nothing. Small counts become slivers: a 1-item cell is 4px and reads as noise.

D Heat cell + numeral background intensity = open count COMPACT
RowDecideBuildCheck
SHOWS

Where the mass is, from across the room, in the least vertical space of any option. Would let the grid hold all seventeen rows plus the gate rows without scrolling.

COSTS

Composition is gone — you get one number and one intensity, so blocked work is invisible unless it gets its own channel. Intensity is also the least precise encoding here; nobody reads a shade as "seven".

E Bubble — area by count, ring by progress area = total · arc = fraction banked NOT FOR THIS
RowDecideBuildCheck
SHOWS

Magnitude and progress together in a compact, attractive mark. Reads well at a glance for the two or three biggest cells.

COSTS

Area is the encoding humans judge worst — a 22 next to a 13 does not look like 1.7×. It also forces tall rows for the big circles, costing more vertical space than dots while carrying less information. Pretty, and wrong for an operations board.

Recommendation

Option B, unit dots, with option D's compactness held in reserve. Dots are the only encoding here that answers all three questions you actually ask the board — how much work, what state is it in, and is anything red — without a legend or a scale. They also degrade gracefully: the failure mode is a crowded cell, not a misleading one, and today's largest cell is 22 marks against a comfortable ceiling of about 30.

If the grid later grows past what dots can hold, D is the fallback — but only alongside a separate channel for blocked work, because a heat cell cannot show it.

Rendered from real data, infrastructure/state/status_matrix.json and the three queue files. Eight rows chosen to span the range: one genuinely empty, one single-item, the 22-item Factions cell, and rows carrying blocked and in-hand work.
