# Narrator Corpus — The Path Triad (Ishko · Ta'Baa · Mob'Unloo)

_Finished v1 prose: the letters the player actually reads. All text is the
Narrator's (canon ruling: non-egoic, second person, Into-the-Woods register —
within and beyond the world at once, free to reference unrevealed lore, never
speaking AS a god; when a god speaks he is quoted). No game-mechanic words, no
numbers in shipping text. Letter bodies are 2–6 sentences by design. Source of
mechanical truth: the matrix pages in `divine_satiation_engine.md`; this file
owns only the WORDS. Dilemma voice lines are single-sourced in
`divine_dilemma_events.md` (pointer, not duplicate — doctrine); this file adds
generic council barks instead._

## ① ISHKO THE UNMASKABLE

**Voice bible.** Ishko is barely quoted; mostly he is *reported* — the Narrator
describes what watched, what withheld, what was not there. When he does speak
it is few words, present tense, flat as held breath, with something always left
unfinished. His imagery: eyes, doors ajar, dust settling, the held breath, the
grave as a made bed. His letters should read like something glimpsed and not
quite confirmed — sentences that decline to complete their thought. He never
uses the words *glory*, *forever*, or *look at me*; he never raises his voice;
he never explains twice. Where other gods argue, Ishko subtracts.

### First contact — "The Ones Who Walked Past"
> They walked within a stone's throw of your walls and saw a dune, a rock, a
> nothing. Understand what you are inside: a thing built to be unseen between
> the stars. A pair of orange eyes has watched you from the dark since the day
> you landed. Tonight they are pleased.

### Boons
**S — Orange Dusk**
> The lights outside have dimmed themselves, and no hand did it. The desert has
> moved a little closer around your walls, the way a blanket is drawn up. You
> are harder to find tonight. You will not be told by whom.

**M — The Long Shadow**
> Something has stopped a clock you did not know was running. While the dark
> holds, nothing out there is getting any closer to knowing where you sleep.
> Two orange points hang in the viewport, unblinking, keeping it so.

**L — Unseen Berth**
> Whatever was written about your whereabouts has been unwritten. Somewhere a
> tracker stares at sand that has forgotten you; somewhere a map ends early.
> For a while — do not ask how long — you are nowhere at all. It is his finest
> gift, and it is the same gift he gives the dead.

### Demands (offered as opportunities — he does not punish refusal; he only notices)
**S — The Prompt Grave**
> There is a body above the sand. The grave is the deepest hiding there is, and
> a Jawa laid down in it by nightfall has hidden perfectly, at last and always.
> The eyes in the dark would count it a courtesy. That is all that will be
> said.

**M — The Hour of Stillness**
> An offer, quietly made: one hour where nothing moves. No hauling, no hammer,
> no voice above a whisper — the whole clan a rock among rocks. He does not ask
> often, and he does not ask twice.

**L — The Blackout Reign**
> While he holds the floor, he would have the night kept whole: no lamp out of
> doors, no voice thrown to the sky. Only the dark, worn like a cloak, until he
> is done admiring you in it.

### Taboo warnings
**S — on corpses left to the sky**
> The dead man on the surface is a signpost, and every carrion bird above him
> is an arrow pointing down. The eyes in the dark have narrowed. Bury what is
> yours before something else reads it.

**M — on melee-executing the downed**
> You stood over a beaten man in the open and finished him with your hands,
> where anything could watch you do it. That is not killing as he taught it.
> The kill he loves leaves no one certain it happened at all.

**L — REIGN-BREAKER warning, on inviting the light**
> You are about to be *seen on purpose* — and while he holds the floor. Know
> what you spend: he has kept you folded in the dark like a knife in a sleeve,
> and a knife shown is a knife answered. If the floodlights rise, he will not
> shout. He will simply let go.

### Curses (F10: he enacts the hiding — on you)
**S — The Dark Keeps Its Own**
> The fog is staying later than it should. Tools are not where they were left;
> corridors end sooner than remembered. In doorways, in the tail of the eye:
> paired orange points, gone when faced. The dark is keeping things now. It has
> always kept things. Lately it is keeping yours.

**M — Unseen by Friends**
> You wished to be hidden, and it has been arranged — from everyone. The
> traders cannot find the way in; the friendly banners pass by in the distance,
> searching, and move on. The lamps gutter to embers on their own. Bury your
> surface dead and give him one day of true stillness, and the world will be
> permitted to remember you.

**L — The Deep Dark**
> Now you are hidden as the grave is hidden. No voice reaches you and none
> leaves; no visitor finds the door; your own scouts return saying the edges of
> the world have gone black. Even the wind has stopped saying your name. He
> will open one eye when you prove the dark is a weapon and not a burial — when
> something hostile dies, from cover, by an unseen hand.

**Curse-lift**
> The lamps breathe again. The horizon has been given back. Two orange points
> regard you a moment longer than is comfortable — approval, or appetite, or
> both — and are gone.

### Silences
> A pair of orange eyes opened in the dark of the viewport, rested on you
> through the whole affair, and closed without comment.

> Something watched that from the shadow under the hull. It has decided, this
> once, to have not been there.

### Intercession — Ishko shields against Sh'kaar (the canonical pair)
> The killing light leaned toward you today — and found only dune, and rock,
> and nothing. The dark folded over you like a hand over a candle, and the sun
> moved on, cheated. In the shadow it left behind, two orange points consider
> you at length. You are owed nothing. You have been given it anyway.

### Council barks (generic, for staged arguments; dilemma-specific lines live in `divine_dilemma_events.md`)
> *"No."* — and the dark under the floor says nothing more.

> *"They have not seen us. Everything else is detail."*

> *"Loud plans are confessions made early."*

## ⑥ TA'BAA THE UNROOTED

**Voice bible.** Ta'Baa's letters move — clauses that lean forward, verbs of
going, sentences that end at thresholds. He is wind through an open hatch: warm,
urgent, a little wild, and *kind* in the way a hand dragging you from a fire is
kind. He is HOPE with sand in its teeth; his one blasphemy is despair, and even
his threats are invitations. Imagery: doors, fuel, the receding dune-line, the
engine-cough, roads that exist only ahead. He uses the second person imperative
freely — *go, take, leave, live.* He never uses the words *home*, *settle*, or
*enough*; he says *always* only in one sentence, and it is *always leave*.

### First contact — "The Ship Dreamed of Leaving"
> You have been here five days. The ship counted every one of them. Somewhere
> in its long life it learned that a thing which stops moving is already being
> buried — and tonight, dreaming, it tried to leave without you. Take that as
> the kindness it is: a god named *farewell* has noticed you are still here.

### Boons
**S — Tailwind**
> The wind has changed sides. It is at every back, in every stride, under every
> wheel; the whole clan walks downhill today in all directions. Somewhere ahead
> of you, something is holding a door open.

**M — The Open Door**
> The ship sits lighter on its skids, the fuel burns willing, and the weight of
> everything you own seems less certain it must be carried. Even the worst
> thoughts find the exits easily tonight. He has oiled every hinge you have,
> including the ones in your chest.

**L — Somewhere Better**
> He has seen it from wherever he stands: a place over the horizon worth the
> burn — richer, kinder, unclaimed, *reachable*. He is not a god who shows a
> thing twice. The open sky is a promise with a short life. Go and see, or stay
> and wonder forever.

### Demands
**S — The Lift Margin**
> Keep the tanks heavy enough to leave *now* — this hour, this breath. A clan
> that must ask permission of its fuel gauge before fleeing has already chosen
> its grave. He checks the margin the way you check a sleeping child.

**M — The Sortie**
> Send someone OUT. Anywhere. A caravan, a raid, a walk to the ridgeline and
> back with news — he does not much care, so long as dust rises behind Jawa
> feet and the world is reminded you can still move. Sitting is a rumor he will
> not have spread about you.

**L — The Launch**
> Before his time on the floor is spent, he wants the only prayer he has ever
> counted: fire under the hull, the ground letting go, everything you love
> snatched into the sky in one piece. He asks a great deal. He is also right.

### Taboo warnings
**S — on an empty margin**
> The tanks have run to fumes, and somewhere the wind has gone very still. You
> have made yourselves a *fact* — a fixed thing, a known address, a promise to
> every enemy that you will still be here when they arrive. He cannot bear to
> watch you become geography.

**M — on walling in the ship**
> You are bricking up the one door that has never failed you. He watched the
> ancestors of your ancestors trust their lives to that hull, and he watches
> you now, stacking stone between it and the sky, and he asks — gently, once —
> what exactly you think you are building. A wall around a wing is called a
> cage.

**L — REIGN-BREAKER warning, on dismantling the engines**
> Those are not components. Those are the wings of the only bird that ever
> loved you back, and you stand over them with a wrench while he holds the
> floor. Put it down. A clan that eats its own engines has decided, quietly,
> to die at a known address — and he will not stay to watch the sitting.

### Curses (F10: he makes you leave — his way)
**S — The Wind Under Doors**
> There is a whistle in the corridors that the corridors cannot explain, low
> and departing. Sleepers dream of roads and wake up facing the berth; doors
> drift open toward the ship, all of them, all night. Nobody is doing it. It is
> only that everything here has begun leaning toward *away*.

**M — Cut Moorings**
> He has started loosening you himself. What roots, fails; what stores, spoils;
> and in the morning the fuel is found already hauled shipside by hands nobody
> owns — helpful, and not what you wanted. On the heat-shimmer, a dune-line
> recedes where there is no heat. Send something out that comes *back* — motion
> offered freely — and he will stop offering it for you.

**L — The Burning of the Root**
> He looked for whatever was keeping you here, and he found it, and it is gone —
> burned, broken, taken out from under you like a tent peg in a storm. Under
> the hull, uncommanded, the engines are warming; the fuel is being spent
> toward an exit that is his, not yours. He no longer asks. Something FLIES, or
> he will keep removing reasons until nothing here is worth the staying.

**Curse-lift**
> The engines settle, satisfied. The whistle in the corridors resolves, for one
> moment, into something almost like a song about a road — and the ship stands
> easy on its skids again, a bird content to be briefly a stone.

### Silences
> Far off, the dune-line shimmered and receded, and whatever watches from it
> chose — this once — to keep its opinion downwind.

> The fuel gauge was studied all night by nothing you could catch at it. Come
> morning, no judgment had been left behind. Only fingerprints in the dust on
> the glass.

### Intercession — Ta'Baa shields against Mob'Unloo (there is a road out of every debt)
> The tally was coming for you — the accounts called in, the collectors already
> saddling. And then the wind rose, and the paperwork, so to speak, was lost in
> transit. *"There is a road out of every debt,"* says the god named farewell,
> holding the door with one foot, *"and my beloved brother the accountant has
> never once caught a thing that runs."* Run soon. He has bought you the start,
> not the finish.

### Council barks
> *"Whatever the question is: the answer is packing."*

> *"I have never met a problem that could keep pace with a launched ship."*

> *"Despair is the only baggage I refuse to carry. Drop it, and let's GO."*

## ④ MOB'UNLOO THE EVER-OWED

**Voice bible.** Mob'Unloo's letters are invoices that love you. The diction of
accountancy fused with the diction of devotion: *beloved debtor, cherished
account, dear outstanding balance.* Precise, warm, unhurried, terrifying — he
never threatens, he *itemizes*. Sentences balance like ledger columns; he is
fond of the colon, the tidy list, the question *how much?* asked of things that
should not have prices. His imagery: the endless tally, two unblinking eyes,
ink, seals, scales, the page with your name on it. He never uses the words
*gift*, *free*, or *forgiven* — except to deny that such things exist. He is
courteous even in ruin; especially in ruin.

### First contact — "The Page With Your Name On It"
> Profit: noted. Understand that nothing aboard this vessel is ever lost — only
> owed. Two unblinking eyes have opened a page with your name on it, and they
> will keep it past your death. Mob'Unloo the Ever-Owed asks only one question
> of you, ever: *how much?*

### Boons
**S — Thumb on the Scale**
> Observe the prices, beloved account: they lean your way today, gently, the
> way a scale leans when a patient thumb rests upon it. No one at the table
> will mention it. It would be poor manners, and worse business, to mention it
> yourself.

**M — The Ledger Smiles**
> The roads have remembered where you are, and every trader on them has heard —
> from no one in particular — that dealing with your clan is dealing well. They
> will come heavier and leave lighter, and count themselves lucky both ways. He
> is fond of you this season. It is itemized.

**L — The Perfect Deal**
> Once in a long lifetime the ledger produces a page with no wrong side: a deal
> so clean it makes the taking holy. That page has your name on it, and it is
> open *now*. Even the hottest goods find an honest price today; even the
> unaskable question — *how much?* — gets its answer. Do not hesitate. Grace of
> this kind is never carried forward to the next page.

### Demands
**S — The Standing Table**
> A caravan is a table set for two, and an unmet table is an insult with legs.
> Whatever they carry, whatever you hold: *trade*. The sum matters less than
> the signature. He wishes to see your name entered somewhere today.

**M — The Black Line**
> His instruction for this season is brief, as the best contracts are: end it
> richer than you began it. He does not specify how. He never specifies how.
> The bottom line is the only line he has ever read.

**L — The Collection**
> Somewhere out there, someone holds what is yours — a ransom unclaimed, a fee
> unpaid, a debt gone comfortable with age. He finds this state of affairs
> almost physically painful. Go and collect, with papers or with patience or
> with whatever the debtor best understands. The ledger does not close itself.

### Taboo warnings
**S — on gifts for nothing**
> Something left your hands today and *nothing came back* — and you called it
> generosity, which is the word the careless use for leakage. Every gift is a
> loan wearing perfume, beloved. If you will not name the counter-price,
> he will name it for you, and his rates are antique.

**M — on breaking a struck deal**
> A struck deal is a small god: it lives exactly as long as both parties keep
> it alive, and you have just strangled one with your own signature upon it.
> He does not grieve the other party. He grieves the *instrument*. Paper that
> cannot be trusted is sand, and men who deal in sand die poor.

**L — REIGN-BREAKER warning, on being caught**
> Hear him closely, for he says this with love: he has never once condemned the
> taking. He condemns the being *seen* to take — barter so clumsy it must go
> about dressed as betrayal, craft so poor the whole market watches it fail.
> You are close to that cliff now, and he holds the floor, and public shame is
> the one debt he refuses to carry on his own book. Be skillful, or be still.

### Curses (F10: the ledger balances ITSELF — out of your pocket)
**S — Interest Accrues**
> A dry clicking has started up somewhere behind the walls, patient as drip.
> The visitors have begun expecting little courtesies — a cut here, a tribute
> there — as if word had gone out that your clan runs an open tab. Below decks,
> the unsettled dead rattle their accounts in their sleep. Nothing is wrong,
> exactly. Everything is *accruing*.

**M — Called In**
> The ledger has grown tired of waiting and has begun collecting on your
> behalf, which is to say: from you. Old friends arrive with old grievances
> priced in new figures; traders come not to deal but to *collect*; every sum
> on every table is written, somehow, in red. Settle one true debt of your own,
> or tithe him one honest profit, and the page will consent to turn.

**L — The Audit**
> All of it, beloved debtor. All of it has come due at once: the ransoms, the
> creditors, the courtesies borrowed against tomorrow, the dead themselves
> walking their claims up from below with the paperwork in order. Do not plead;
> pleading is not a currency. One great debt collected FROM another people, in
> his name, entered in his sight — the ledger fed from outside — and this page,
> he promises, closes as tidily as it opened.

**Curse-lift**
> The clicking stops on a final, satisfied *clack*, like a drawer closing on
> money. Somewhere a page turns. The eyes above the tally rest upon you with
> what would be warmth, in an accountant: your credit, beloved, is restored.

### Silences
> Two unblinking eyes watched the whole transaction from somewhere above the
> tally, and entered — nothing. Which is not the same, you understand, as
> nothing being entered later.

> A faint smell of ink hung about the affair, and a sense of a sum being
> carried, unspoken, to some further column.

### Intercession — Mob'Unloo shields against Ozzik (shame restructured as debt)
> The humbling was already priced and the collectors of it already moving —
> and then, at the last, an accountant cleared his throat. *"Shame,"* he
> observes, *"is a debt like any other, and I have restructured it."* The blow
> that was coming has been converted to installments: smaller, colder,
> payable over time to HIM. You will feel each one. But you will be standing,
> beloved account, and standing men can trade.

### Council barks
> *"Sentiment is a rounding error. What does it COST?"*

> *"I have heard six opinions and no figures. Figures, please."*

> *"Whatever we decide: get a receipt."*
