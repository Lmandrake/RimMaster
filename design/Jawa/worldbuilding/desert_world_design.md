## 1. The core design thesis (the thing to internalize)

**Landing should be a decision under scarcity, not tourism.** In most RimWorld play, biome choice is aesthetic ("I wonder what ice sheet feels like") because every biome is a *complete* self-sufficient colony site — you can grow food, mine steel, and settle anywhere. That flatness is what makes destinations interchangeable.

Our world breaks that flatness deliberately: **no single tile is self-sufficient.** Each terrain is *rich in something you need and hostile in a way that stops you staying.* The gravship exists precisely to let you take what a place offers and leave before its risk compounds. That converts the map into a **supply-run economy** — you land at the volcanic field *because* you're out of components, knowing the ground itself is trying to kill you, grab what you came for, and jump. That is the campaign's central loop, and it's the diegetic engine behind the anti-exponential pillar: **you can't snowball because you can't safely stay anywhere long enough to build an exponential base.**

**Design rule for every terrain:** it must answer two questions in one breath — *"What does this place give me that I can't get elsewhere?"* and *"What is it doing to me the whole time I'm here?"* If a terrain has only an upside, it's tourism; if it has only a downside, it's just a bad tile nobody picks. The pairing is the whole game. §2A below turns this rule into a **hard four-axis schema every terrain must fill** — one Abundant resource, one Scarce/missing resource, one Exotic resource, and one major Threat — so the pairing is enforced by construction rather than left to taste.

**Pillar alignment:** this is `Custom_World.md`'s "constraint as engine" applied at the *tile* level instead of the *world* level. It raises no player power ceiling — it *constrains* where power can be exercised — so it passes the 7-question test trivially. The risk side also directly feeds the §19 "qualitative danger" thesis: terrain hazards are a new *capability* of the environment, not stat inflation.

## 2. The terrain identity table (the four axes — the heart of it)

**The single framing for every terrain is the four-axis schema.** Each biome/terrain must define exactly four things: **① Abundant** (the surplus resource that's your reason to come and the thing you stockpile before leaving), **② Scarce/missing** (the resource this tile flatly denies you, forcing dependence elsewhere — this is what guarantees no tile is self-sufficient and creates your *next* need), **③ Exotic** (the rare, located, covetable resource you can only meaningfully get here — a terrain treasure, gem vein, alien biomass, salvage cache; the "spice" that makes a tile memorable and drives trade), and **④ Major threat** (one characteristic §19 *qualitative* danger — a new capability of the place — that also serves as the timer forcing you out). §2A below is the enforceable spec and rationale for why these four; this table is the applied version with implementation routes. **How to read this table for play:** the *sea* is deep desert (you're always crossing it). Oases, volcanic fields, rivers, coasts and anomaly patches are the **islands of purpose** scattered across it. You plot a course between them by *need* — low on water → oasis (but expect a fight); low on components → volcano (but beat the eruption clock); need to move fast/far → river corridor (but you'll meet everyone using it). The four axes make that loop mechanical: axis ① fills a need, axis ② creates the next one, axis ③ tempts you to linger for wealth, axis ④ forces you out before you can settle.

## 2A. The four-axis schema — the enforceable spec behind §2

§2's table is the applied version; this is the **enforceable spec and rationale**. Every terrain in the world — the anchors in §2 and every expansion biome in §2B — must define exactly **four axes**. If a terrain can't fill all four, it isn't finished being designed.

1. **Abundant** — the one resource this terrain hands you in surplus. This is the *reason to come* and the thing you stockpile before leaving.
2. **Scarce / missing** — the one resource this terrain flatly *denies* you, forcing dependence on somewhere else. This is what guarantees no tile is self-sufficient and keeps you flying; each landing therefore creates a *future* need that pulls you to the next tile.
3. **Exotic** — one *special, located* resource you can only meaningfully get here: a terrain treasure (§3B), a gemstone vein, alien biomass, a salvage cache. Distinct from Abundant: Abundant is *bulk you live on*; Exotic is *rare wealth you trade or covet*. (This is the "spice" layer — the thing that makes a specific tile memorable rather than generic.)
4. **Threat** — one *major, characteristic* danger, ideally a §19 *qualitative* one (a new capability of the place: killer plants, eruptions, sandstorms, a territorial guardian) rather than just "more raiders." This is both the standing cost of being there and the timer that forces you out.

**Why four axes:** a bare upside/downside pairing is necessary but coarse. Splitting the upside into **Abundant** (bulk survival good) vs **Exotic** (rare covetable) is what prevents the world from collapsing into "every tile just has a different ore" — the Exotic axis is where personality and trade tension live. And naming the **Scarce** axis explicitly is what mechanically enforces the no-self-sufficiency rule: each terrain must *deny* something. The four axes are a **closed loop**: Abundant fills a need, Scarce creates the next need, Exotic tempts you to linger, Threat forces you out before you can.

**Anti-exponential note:** none of the four axes is a *ladder*. Abundant/Exotic are finite located windfalls (you carry them off, you don't build a factory that makes more); Scarce is a pure constraint; Threat is enemy-side. The schema **raises no player power ceiling** — it shapes *where* and *how long* power can be exercised. Passes the 7-question test by construction. The one guardrail: an Abundant or Exotic resource must never be paired with a *player-buildable* infinite generator of it on that tile (that would convert a windfall into a ladder) — this is exactly why the extraction engines in §3B are **non-buildable and tile-bound**.

**Reading the Exotic axis (③):** this is where the **Gemstone mod** lives as a recurring flavor (river placer gems, salt-flat crystalline evaporites, volcanic gem veins) — see §3B/§3C. It's deliberately *not* an Abundant resource anywhere: gems are covetable trade/relic material, never a survival staple, which is exactly the "harmless, better-than-jade" role you flagged.

## 2D. World-shape & biome-placement directives — REGIONALIZED, not scattered (decision session, 2026-08-04)

The default RimWorld worldgen sprinkles biomes by latitude/rainfall/temperature noise, which would salt-and-pepper volcanic tiles and rare biomes evenly across the globe. **User ruling: the planet is regionalized instead.** The world should read as *distinct territories*, not uniform noise. Concrete directives:

- **Volcanic concentration.** MANY volcanic mountain ranges clustered in **one part of the globe** (a "fire coast" / hellrim region) — Pyroclastic Conflagration + Volcano + Lava Fields + Tar Pits packed together there — while the rest of the planet stays comparatively quiet. This makes "going to the workshop" a real *journey to a region*, not a hop to the nearest scattered tile.
- **Vast unbroken desert elsewhere.** Away from the fire region, **large contiguous tracts of Extreme Desert / Desert / Badlands** with few interruptions — the true "sea you cross." Oases and rivers stay *rare* points within this expanse (they already are, per §2C).
- **Mechanoid "Shipyards" — a SINGLE cluster.** `AB_MechanoidIntrusion` (prime steel source) is placed as **one localized cluster of tiles**, not scattered seeds. Lore: an ancient ship-building mega-project — hence "the Shipyards." **(f) Fecund world / the overgrowth tax — plants grow crazily *everywhere*, and the water gradient falls out for free (user, 2026-08-07; approach revised 2026-08-07).** The mirror image of (a): where §(a) makes the *dry* sea lethal by absence, this makes the living world lethal by **excess**. Wild vegetation grows *freakishly* fast across the whole world, and that speed is its aggression:

- **Wild vegetation grows freakishly fast, and that speed is its aggression.** Jungle, vines, and scrub reclaim cleared ground continuously; the player must **mow it down constantly just to hold paths and safe zones.** This is not decorative lushness — it is a standing maintenance *tax* on operating on this world, and it stacks with the (c) hostile-flora hazards (Agarilux Prime spore-fields, mycotic fungi): the fast regrowth both *conceals* threats and *re-conceals* them after you've cleared a lane, so no ground stays "handled." A path cut yesterday is overgrown today.
- **The water tiles are the *worst* of it — automatically, at no extra cost.** This is the key realization behind the revised approach: **a global growth boost reproduces the water gradient for free**, because RimWorld multiplies a plant's base grow-speed by *terrain fertility* (source-confirmed: growth = `growDays` × fertility, scaled by each plant's `fertilitySensitivity`, typically ~0.5). Wet/marshy/rich soil carries a high fertility multiplier natively, so a plant growing 4× faster *everywhere* still grows fastest in absolute terms on the fertile wet cells. "Crazy everywhere" therefore means "craziest near water" — the oasis/river/coast shorelines become the densest, most-overgrown, most-ambush-friendly ground on the map **without any per-cell water-adjacency logic at all.** The gradient is emergent, not authored.
- **Animal abundance near water then follows for free too.** Because the wet tiles carry the densest, fastest-regrowing forage, normal foraging concentrates grazers there — and predators follow the grazers. So the "shoreline crawls with tameable stock and ④-threat predators" effect (Kaadu, mucklurker catfish, megakrill, polluted-water fauna) largely emerges from the vegetation gradient rather than needing a separate fauna-spawn rule. Biome-level `animalDensity` tuning on the wet biomes can *reinforce* it, but is no longer load-bearing.

*Anti-exponential check — clean, and it does real pillar work.* This adds no player buildable or economy; it's an environment rule, and a growth-*speed* boost is a *tax*, not a yield (you still pay the labor to harvest or mow — it's gated by handling/work, never a free-wealth printer). Its *shape* reinforces the pillar: the richest capture grounds (abundant tameable animals, the water-clade species) are also the **most dangerous and most labor-intensive to hold** (crowded, overgrown, low-visibility, predator-dense), so wild-taming can never quietly out-scale the honest creature trade — you pay for abundance in mowing-labor and blood.

*Two side effects to accept on purpose:* (i) **cultivated crops also grow faster** — a mild food-economy easing; probably fine (arguably welcome) on a survival-hostile desert, but it's a deliberate choice, not a surprise. If it ever feels too generous, it can be clawed back via the §(a) no-free-water rule (crops still need water). (ii) **Cover regrows fast everywhere** — faster-regrowing brush helps raiders approach and re-hides threats after you clear sightlines. On-theme (the world fights you), but a real combat consequence, not just flavor.

*Route — this REPLACES the earlier per-cell runtime plan; the flora Tier-C runtime dependency is retired.* The whole effect is now **one global plant-growth-*rate* multiplier**, delivered by one of three routes (⚠ pending the 2026-08-07 Fetcher search for a clean 1.6 mod):
1. **Broad XML patch** setting `growDays` low across all `ThingDef`s with a `<plant>` block. Assembly-free and simple, but a `PatchOperationReplace` *sets* rather than *multiplies*, so it flattens every plant to the same speed (loses relative pacing between grass and trees). Acceptable but blunt.

## 3A. The resource-partition scheme (the "purpose map" — buildable now)

**The principle:** enumerate every resource a RimWorld map normally hands you *everywhere*, then deliberately **partition them across terrains so no single tile carries them all.** In vanilla you get food + stone + steel + components + wood + medicine everywhere; that flatness is the disease. We cut the deck so each terrain owns a *slice*, and the slices only add up if you *keep moving and collecting them* — which is the campaign.

**First, the inventory of "typical map resources" (what we're partitioning):**
- **Food capacity:** arable/fertile soil (growing), wild game (hunting), foraged plants.
- **Water:** the scarce master-resource (§3b, §(a)) — a first-class *common* resource each terrain rates, exactly like food or ore. Three physical forms, and which one a tile offers is what its rating means: **surface water** (river/oasis/coast — pumpable via `ElectricPump`/`WindPump`), **groundwater** (aquifer — reached by `PrimitiveWell`/`DeepWells`, then hauled to a tub), and **none** (deep desert / salt flat — you arrive with a full Water Butt and ration it). The rating below is therefore also a statement about *what fill route works there*.
- **Construction bulk:** stone (chunks/blocks), wood.
- **Metals:** steel (from ore/slag), rarer components & advanced components.
- **Energy:** geothermal (geysers), solar (open sky), wind, chemfuel/astrofuel feedstock.
- **Salvage / tech:** ancient ruins loot, mechanoid/droid scrap, working machinery, rare artifacts.
- **Exotics:** jade, gold, uranium, plasteel, luciferium/medicine reagents, biome-unique alien materials.

**The partition (each terrain is rich in a FEW, poor in the rest):**

| Resource | Deep desert | Oasis | Volcanic | Coast | River valley | Salt flat |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arable food** | ✗ none | ✅✅ the food tile | ✗ | ⚠️ biomass but hostile | ✅ fertile strip | ✗ |
| **Water** | ✗ none — carry & ration onboard Butt | ✅✅ the water tile: surface + shallow aquifer (pump or well) | ⚠️ no surface; deep aquifer only (`DeepWells`, slow) | ✅ surface, but saline — pump then desalinate/haul | ✅✅ fresh surface (pump freely) | ✗ none — dry crust, no aquifer |
| **Stone / bulk** | ⚠️ some | ✗ | ✅ obsidian/rock | ✗ | ⚠️ | ✅ salt/mineral crust |
| **Steel / ore** | ⚠️ surface scatter | ✗ | ✅✅ the metal tile | ✗ | ⚠️ placer deposits | ⚠️ crust minerals |
| **Components / advanced** | ✅ (from ruins/wrecks) | ✗ | ✅ deep-drill | ⚠️ | ✗ | ✗ |
| **Energy** | ☀️ solar (brutal but strong) | ⚠️ | ✅✅ geothermal | 🌊 (tidal, if modded) | 💨 | ☀️ solar + wind |
| **Salvage / tech** | ✅✅ the ruins tile | ✗ | ⚠️ (droid-held) | ⚠️ wrecks | ⚠️ corridor debris | ⚠️ |
| **Exotics** | ⚠️ buried | ✗ | ✅ rare veins | ✅✅ alien biomass/organics | ✗ | ⚠️ evaporite salts |

**Reading the Water row specifically (the master-resource):** ✅✅ = pumpable surface water, refill the onboard Butt freely (oasis, river); ✅ = water present but needs work (coast = saline, desalinate; groundwater = well + haul to tub); ⚠️ = deep aquifer only, slow and power-hungry (`DeepWells`); ✗ = no source at all, so your dwell time on that tile is bounded by whatever's in the Water Butt when you arrive. That last case is the whole engine — a ✗-water tile physically cannot be camped, which is what times your exit from the deep desert and salt flats.

**How to read it:** the **oasis** feeds you but has nothing industrial; the **volcanic field** is the workshop of the world (metal + components + power) but starves you and burns you; the **deep desert** is the salvage-and-solar sea with no food/water; the **coast** is exotic wealth guarded by killer vegetation; the **river** is the only place that's *balanced* (food + water + travel) which is exactly why it's a contested highway; the **salt flat** is a staging pad, never a home. **No column is self-sufficient** — that's the win condition of the design.

**Implementation (this is the buildable-now layer):** Map Designer per-biome ore/deep-resource commonality + ruins density, Choose Biome Commonality for the mix, Geological Landforms for rivers/geysers. Set volcanic biomes: ore↑↑, deep-drill-resource↑↑, geyser↑, ruins↓, fertility→barren. Deep desert: ruins↑↑, ore→surface-only, fertility→0. Oasis (author as a landmark): fertile patch + water, ruins→0. Etc. **Water specifically** is set two ways: *surface* water is a landform/terrain question (Geological Landforms rivers/pools + placing water terrain on oasis/coast landmarks), while *groundwater* (whether `PrimitiveWell`/`DeepWells` can draw at all) 🔎 needs an in-game check of DBH-Lite's well placement rules — if the well works on any tile regardless of biome, that would let the deep desert cheat its ✗-water rating, so we'd want to confirm it's gated (or gate it ourselves) so dry tiles stay dry. **None of this needs a new mod** — it's the director-mod config, now with a *scheme* instead of guesswork. This is the concrete next artifact to lock (per-biome Map Designer value table).

## 3B. TERRAIN TREASURES — pre-placed, workable, NON-buildable extraction sites (your quarry insight, generalized)

**This is the strongest idea in the whole design.** Your quarry observation generalizes into a *category* that solves a problem the partition scheme alone doesn't: how do you make a terrain's Exotic axis (③) feel like a **discovered, worked treasure** rather than just "higher ore commonality"? Answer: **place the extraction engine on the map already-built, workable, but impossible for the player to construct.** You can *operate* the quarry/well/drill you find; you can never *build* one. So the resource is bound to the *place*, not to the *colony* — and since the pursuing Empire eventually forces you off the tile, you extract what you can and leave. **The pursuit IS the balance mechanism** — it's the threat axis (④) acting as the timer that does the anti-exponential work, exactly as you said.

**Why this is anti-exponential-clean (the key insight):** a *player-buildable* quarry is an exponential violation — infinite stone/metal anywhere, forever, snowballing. But a *pre-placed, non-buildable, tile-bound* quarry is the **opposite**: it's a finite, located, time-limited windfall you must travel to and abandon. It can't snowball because you can't replicate it and you can't stay. This flips a classic "cheaty" mod (buildable quarries) into a *scarcity engine*. The 7-question test: no parallel ladder (you don't build them), doesn't scale (bound to tiles you must leave), doesn't trivialize the ship (it's the *reason* to fly). ✅ clean — **provided the "non-buildable" enforcement holds** (see technique below).

**The technique (how you make a mod's building non-buildable):** most quarry/extraction mods ship a *constructible* building. To convert it into a terrain treasure you (a) **remove its `<designationCategory>`** so it's not in the architect menu (a one-line PatchOperation in the `Jawa_Patches` mod — same pattern as the Slingshot patch, but *inverted*: we're *removing* buildability instead of adding it), and (b) **pre-place instances at map-gen or via authoring** (Map Designer scatter, a custom `GenStep`, or RimBridge/save-edit placement per landing). The pre-placed instance stays fully functional; the player just can't make more. 🔎 Confirmed feasible in principle (it's the reverse of the buildability patch we already drafted); per-mod specifics need the source once Fetcher confirms which quarry/extraction mods are 1.6.

## 3C. The Gemstone axis (your "better-than-jade" exotic)

That's the "harmless" property you intuited, stated in pillar terms: an Exotic that can't be eaten, burned, or built-with can't become an exponential engine; it can only enrich trade tension (which *feeds* the Hutt-cartel and trader-heavy Jawa fiction).

Candidates (both 🔎 1.6-verify before adopt): **Tilled Soil** (rebalanced-for-1.6 version exists; buildable ~120–200% fertility floor, has a research gate) or **Fertile Fields** (`WS 3225843229`, 1.6; compost→fertilizer→rich-soil chain — more involved, more thematic). Vanilla **Tilled Soil is even available with zero mods** as a fallback.
  - **Pillar fit:** a buildable fertility floor is a *global* capability, but it's a **minor** concern — food isn't a sanctioned exponential tree, nomad dwell-time caps its value, and (unlike VFE sprinklers) it adds no waterless growth engine. Clean.

  **Route:** MOD (fertility-floor mechanic: Tilled Soil / Fertile Fields, 🔎 1.6-verify) or vanilla Tilled Soil (no dep) + AUTHOR/RimBridge (canal flavor terrain + placement). **🔎 open:** pick Tilled Soil vs Fertile Fields (or vanilla), and author the decorative canal water-terrain. *Rejected:* VFE sprinklers (waterless = breaks the water theme), DBH sprinkler (needs forbidden plumbing).

### 3B(5). Helixien gas pockets — ✅ ADOPTED 2026-08-10 (Exotic ③, volcanic + deep desert)

**Mod:** Vanilla Helixien Gas Expanded, Workshop `2877699803` (`VanillaExpanded.HelixienGas`). Adoption + full gate list in `required_mods.md`.

A **found** gas pocket is a near-perfect §3B treasure: located, workable, high-value, carried off as fuel, and gone when it's gone. It gives volcanic and deep-desert tiles an Exotic axis that isn't another ore, and it feeds the Jawa trade fiction — bottled fuel is exactly the sort of thing a scavenger caravan sells.

**⛔ It does not ship pillar-clean. One thing must be stripped.** The mod grants *"a single, small but infinite pocket of Helixien gas from game start."* That is a permanent, tile-independent, infinite source — the literal case §2A's guardrail names, and it directly contradicts the reason we adopted it ("we must always keep moving"). An infinite pocket removes the *timer*: you would never need to leave for fuel again.

**Required at install:**
1. **Remove the infinite starting pocket.** Keep only the finite scanner-found pockets, which the mod itself states "won't be infinite."
2. **Make the extraction plant non-buildable** if it is constructible — the §3B `<designationCategory>` technique above — so gas is *found*, never *manufactured*.

With both done it collapses into the same profile as the oasis well (§2A ①) and the fossil/amber deposits (§3D(4)): a windfall you travel to, drain, and abandon. Without them it is a ladder, and would fail the 7-question test on question 2.

## 3E. THE 15-TILE SET-PIECE TABLE — one signature discovery per terrain (◇ authored spec, review-only; promoted from autonomy sweep 2026-08-05)

This is the Tier-1/Tier-2 set-piece model (`context.md` 2026-08-05, GREENLIT) rendered as a concrete per-terrain draft. **Nothing here is placed yet** — it's the authoring blueprint. One signature discovery per terrain, each tied to that tile's four-axis identity and each dropping **one breadcrumb** toward the 3-act Empire arc (marked → *arc beat*). Codes match `resource_terrain_matrix.html`.

**Delivery mechanism (preference order, from batch 05c/05d):** (1) **native Odyssey Landmarks** (✅ wiki-verified — generates abandoned colonies / ancient garrisons / city ruins; let a Landmark make the tile *type*, then populate it); (2) **Ancient Urban Ruins + Custom Quest Framework** quest-map authoring (build each set-piece as a reusable explorable map — see `required_mods.md`; **what CQF can and cannot author, read from its source and keyed to this section, is `vendor/wisdom/cqf_quest_types_explainer.md`** — read it before designing a set-piece around CQF); (3) **the save-authoring pipeline** (`skills/rimworld-savegame/SKILL.md`; hand-place, the reliable fallback, passes 7-Q Q6 "single authored exception"); (4) Go Explore!-style event content *only if* a 1.6 build is ever confirmed (its "Lost City" + "capture a spaceship" events are near-turnkey, but 1.6 is unconfirmed — optional garnish, not backbone).

*Desert sea (the default ocean you cross):*
- **Deep desert (DD):** a **caravan ossuary** — sand-scoured pack-animal skeletons and toppled cargo around a dead pilot clutching a working nav-beacon. Loot = the beacon (a fixed waypoint hint). → *arc beat: the beacon's last ping points toward the launch corridor — the first thread of a destination.*
- **Arid shrubland (AR):** a **derelict moisture-farm homestead**, vaporators long dry, with a scratched wall-log of a family who "left when the soldiers came through." Low-danger, high-flavor. → *arc beat: earliest civilian rumor the Empire patrols this region.*
- **Forsaken crags (FC):** a **crashed escape pod** wedged in the rocks, occupant gone but distress-log intact — coordinates and a name. → *arc beat: introduces a recurring NPC the crew might later meet (or find dead) — a personal stake in the pursuit.*
- **Salt flat (SF):** a wrecked **Imperial gravbarge** half-buried in salt — the first hard proof the Empire came this way. → *arc beat: Act I → II hinge, the "they're real and they're here" reveal.*

*Water (the scarce lifeline tiles):*
- **Oasis (OA):** a **shrine-well tended by absent hands** — fresh water, offerings, a warning totem in an unreadable script (later translatable). Peaceful now; someone maintains it. → *arc beat: hints at a hidden local faction that could become ally or informant.*
- **River (RV):** a **half-sunk ferry barge** loaded with someone else's evacuation cargo, snagged midstream — salvage-rich but the current (and whatever fouled the barge) is the threat. → *arc beat: the evacuees fled the same pressure you're about to feel.*

*Volcanic (the punishing high-yield tiles):*
- **Volcanic (VO):** an **abandoned Imperial mining rig** over a lava vent, still auto-pumping, crew gone in a hurry (tools dropped, doors open). High-value extraction, high heat/eruption risk. → *arc beat: proof the Empire is resource-stripping this world too — you're competing for the same tiles.* (Pairs with the §3D(8) CoreDrill terrain-treasure.)
- **Tar pits (TP):** a **tar-preserved battlefield** — two forces died here fighting, one clearly Imperial, weapons/armor pristine under the pitch. → *arc beat: someone already resisted the Empire here and lost — a cautionary mirror + a §19.5-safe gear cache (audit loadout).*

*Coast & jungle (the lush-but-lethal tiles):*
- **Coast (CO):** a **beached hulk of a larger starship**, tide-flooded decks, a colony of scavengers (hostile fauna or a rival salvage crew) already picking it over. → *arc beat: introduces a rival scavenger faction — a non-Imperial pressure competing for salvage.*
- **Feralisk jungle (FJ):** a **silk-wrapped explorer's cache** in the canopy — a prior expedition strung supplies up to escape the ground threat, then didn't come back down. → *arc beat: another crew that came this way and vanished; foreshadows the stakes.*
- **Mycotic jungle (MJ):** a **spore-choked research station** studying the fungus, logs describing a specimen that "shouldn't have grown that fast." Exotic biotech loot; contamination threat. → *arc beat: seeds a possible late environmental hazard the Empire may weaponize.*

*Dark (the low-visibility tiles — now also the orbital-timer refuge, §3(e)/§4-Orbital):*
- **Glowforest (GF):** a **bioluminescent grove around a downed comms-relay** whose glow masks a predator lair — the relay still broadcasts a loop. → *arc beat: tapping the relay reveals Imperial traffic (raises Heat if you linger to decode it — deliberate risk/reward).*
- **Ocular forest (OC):** a **previous expedition's camp, intact but empty** — everyone gone, nothing taken. → *arc beat: a direct mirror of your crew's possible fate; maximal dread, minimal exposition.*

*Faction-held (the two authored powers):*
- **Wasteland / android (WA):** a **derelict android foundry** the inhabitants treat as sacred ground — enter and you're tolerated only if you touch nothing. → *arc beat: establishes the androids as the "moat" faction (the Empire avoids their tiles), making WA a Heat-relief sanctuary with its own etiquette cost.*
- **Shipyards / Mechanoid (SH):** the mechanoid cluster is **building something gravship-shaped**. → *arc beat: the reveal you're not the only crashed Factory ship — the endgame's thematic climax.*

**Design guardrails.** (a) Every row is a *single authored exception* (7-Q Q6 ✓) — none adds a repeatable system or a player ladder. ### 4-Orbital. ⭐ ORBITAL SUPREMACY & THE PLANET-BOUND LEASH (user, 2026-08-05) — the mechanical *why* behind the pursuit timer

> **↔ Settlement-count reconciliation (2026-08-12).** `faction_roster_v2.md` gives the Galactic Empire **10** settlements, which read as a flat contradiction of "one or two" for six days. It is not one: **10 is the fiction total across both layers — 2–3 surface, ~7–8 orbital.** The surface seats are the Sector Galactic Empire's aristocratic household, clustered near the large spaceport. **The worldgen `Target settlements` field is a surface number and is set to 3**, because ten ground bases would invert this whole section. Full note, and the one open mechanism question, at `faction_roster_v2.md` §"Strategic balance" †.

*This is the spatial doctrine that grounds the whole pursuit fiction and explains why the campaign is surface-locked for now.* The Empire's power is **vertical, not horizontal**: on the *surface* they hold only **perhaps one or two settlements** — a light footprint — but **in orbit they own nearly everything**. The orbital layer is Imperial-dominated: platforms, asteroid holdings, the space lanes. **The one exception is the Hutts**, who run a **"pirate"-type base** (a base type *anyone* can approach and trade with regardless of faction standing) — the neutral-but-treacherous market node, now given a *location* (orbital / edge-of-system) rather than just a trader-visit abstraction.

**Consequence 1 — the player's factory ship is PLANET-BOUND for now.** Going *up* — to an asteroid, an orbital platform, anywhere off the surface — means entering Imperial-controlled space, where **droids/troops attack almost immediately**. The gravship *can* fly, but the sky is a hostile ceiling. This is a clean, diegetic reason the campaign stays on the planetary surface without an artificial "you can't leave" rule: the Empire owns the exits. It also gives the *endgame* its teeth — the whole point of the 3-act arc (launch corridor / gravcore vault, `context.md` 2026-08-05) is to punch *through* that orbital blockade, so orbital supremacy is exactly the wall the arc is built to breach.

**Consequence 2 — the PRIMARY pursuit timer on most tiles comes from the Empire "from orbit," NOT from territory (user's sharpening 2026-08-05).** Because the Empire watches from above, the baseline "you've lingered too long, they've found you" pressure on *any* surface tile is an **orbital-detection timer** — it applies everywhere the sky is open, independent of whose ground you're standing on. This is the main engine that drives the Imperial Heat gauge and forces the keep-moving loop. **Faction Territories & Vassalage's in-turf ambushes are a SECONDARY, ADDITIVE layer on top** — extra danger for operating *inside* the Empire's (or an android/Hutt faction's) actual territory, stacked on the ever-present orbital timer. So the two pressures compose: orbital timer = the floor everywhere; territorial ambush = a surcharge on held ground.

**⭐ DARK TILES PAUSE THE ORBITAL TIMER — LOCKED DESIGN MECHANIC (user, 2026-08-05).** The one place the sky *can't* see you is under cover: **pitch-dark biomes, caverns, and overhead-canopy tiles PAUSE (halt / heavily suppress) the Empire's orbital "must-leave" detection timer.** This is now a committed mechanic, not just a flavor note — it's the intuitive, diegetic counter to orbital supremacy (you go dark by going *under*), and it makes the Glowforest / Ocular Forest / Biomes!-Caverns / CaveBiome tiles (§3(e), `biome_terrain_palette.md`) tactically load-bearing rather than atmospheric detours. **Crucially, it does NOT make dark tiles a safe haven — it trades one pressure for others.** A dark tile stops the orbital clock but imposes, in its place: **(1) its own territorial issues** — these are exactly the tiles rogue androids favor / the deep places where §4 territorial threats live (a dark cavern is somebody's ground); **(2) per-tile challenges** — the §3(e) vision-scarcity stack (LOS-fog, sight-shrinks-in-dark, unseen spore/hazard set-pieces §3(c)), which makes *fighting and working* there hard even though the sky is blind; **(3) resource scarcity** — dark tiles are poor in the open-sky staples (no solar, limited/absent surface water and sun-grown food per the water master-resource §3A and the §2 four-axis ② Scarce column), so you can *hide* but you can't *thrive* — supplies drain while you shelter. **Net loop:** the dark tile is the "catch your breath / let Heat cool" refuge, but the ticking cost shifts from *the Empire finds you* to *you run out of light, water, and food while something in the dark contests the ground* — so you're still pushed back out into the open eventually. This is the same anti-exponential discipline as everywhere else (no tile lets you camp forever; the *nature* of the pressure just changes), and it turns "run and hide" into a real tactical choice with its own distinct threat profile rather than a get-out-of-jail button. *Implementation:* the pause/suppression is owned by the RimBridge GM layer that runs the orbital timer + Imperial Heat (it already reads tile type) — a dark/covered tile flag zeroes or steeply discounts the orbital-detection accrual while that colony sits there; the compensating threats are already in the stack (§3(c) hazards, §4 android territory, §3A/§2 scarcity columns). *Anti-exponential check:* pure threat/geography trade — raises no player ceiling. Clean.

**Implementation notes (evidence-tiered).** *Established:* Odyssey ships a genuine orbital/space layer (space maps, asteroids, orbital platforms) and the "pirate" tradeable base type is a vanilla faction-base concept — both are real hooks, not inventions. *Reasoned inference / to-confirm at build:* the cleanest builds are (a) Empire = the singular pursuer faction with a heavy roster of *orbital* settlements + 1–2 surface ones (settlement placement is tunable via Faction Territories & Vassalage scan-range + worldgen seeding, `required_mods.md`); (b) Hutt "pirate" base = reflavored vanilla pirate/outlander base with the universal-trade flag; (c) the **orbital-detection timer itself is most naturally owned by the RimBridge GM layer** driving Imperial Heat (it already owns the gauge) — it raises Heat while a colony sits on an open-sky tile and triggers Imperial responses at thresholds; a pure-vanilla approximation is a storyteller/quest cadence via CQF, but the GM-layer version is what makes it *feel* like being watched. *Open design question (not a blocker):* whether "go up and get attacked immediately" is a scripted CQF/bridge response or leans on Odyssey's native orbital-threat behavior — confirm what Odyssey does on an orbital/asteroid map with a hostile-dominant system before authoring. **Anti-exponential check:** all of this is threat/geography — it raises no player ceiling, and it actually *reinforces* the pillar (the sky-ceiling is another wall that stops you snowballing in place). **Recommendation: ADOPT Vehicle Framework + Vanilla Vehicles Expanded (player caravan mobility) and reflavor a pack animal as the Bantha; add Giddy-Up 2 if we want ridable mounts.** This dovetails with the **Nomad-primary ideoligion** — mobility *is* the identity — and with the trade-heavy Jawa fiction (a skimmer caravan rolling into a Hutt market is exactly the intended texture). **Pillar guardrail (important):** vehicles/animals are for **mobility and cargo only** — reject any vehicle that acts as a mobile *production/manufacturing* platform (an on-board factory would be an exponential vector and a mobile base that defeats the "can't safely stay anywhere" loop). Cargo capacity and speed = fine; on-board benches that let you snowball while parked = not fine. *Route:* MOD (VF + VVE, share source already in hand) + AUTHOR (Bantha reskin). **✔️1.6 CONFIRMED FROM SOURCE (Fetcher `2026-08-04h`):** VF About.xml `1.4/1.5/1.6`, VVE `1.4/1.5/1.6` (deps VF + VEF Core, both in stack). **🔎 Remaining:** whether world-map vehicle caravans interact cleanly with RimWorld Exploration Mode's fog-of-war (both touch world travel — in-game test).

### 4C. Landing a gravship in town peacefully + nomad travel events (resolution, 2026-08-04)

Two connected questions closed here: *"no way to land a gravship in a town in a friendly manner to go chat?"* and *"some nomadic event generators while we're trucking things around in our skimmers."* **Findings (Fetcher `2026-08-04f`, 6/6 delivered.)**

**(a) Peaceful town visits — vanilla is better than expected; the gravship parks nearby, the *visit* happens on foot/shuttle.** RimWorld Wiki (Gravship page) states plainly: *"the passenger shuttle... can visit settlements directly to trade — the gravship can only land nearby."* The gravship never touches down *on* a friendly town (same tile-destruction reason it wrecks its own launch pad). So the intended peaceful loop is: **park the gravship on the outskirts → either send the passenger shuttle in to trade directly, or form a caravan off the landed ship and walk the last tile**, where a native "Fulfill trade / Trade" button appears at the settlement. Three mods graduate this, ordered by how much they bend the vanilla rule:
  - **Visit Settlements** (WS `3535955435`, confirmed 1.6/Odyssey) — **the on-theme pick.** Adds a gizmo to *enter* a settlement without attacking, a dedicated interaction menu (rent beds, trade supplies), reform-caravan-from-inside, plus **penalties for theft, mining, encroaching, and vandalism** on their property. That penalty structure is *pillar-clean*: the peaceful visit becomes a genuine social-consequence surface (§19 qualitative), not a free lunch.
  - **Gravship Settlement Trade** (WS) — trade with a nearby settlement via **comms console** when the ship is within ~5 tiles, with **configurable goodwill/proximity penalties**; built explicitly for solo gravship runs. Closest literal answer to "land near town and go chat," and it keeps the friction. **⚠️ 1.6 UNCONFIRMED** (Fetcher `2026-08-04h` found no clean version page) — optional, not a blocker.
  - **Settlement-proximity bypass mod** (unnamed in snippet) — removes the "can't settle/land near factions" limit. **⚠️ Pillar-risky:** stripping proximity friction cuts *against* §19 qualitative threat. Skip unless specifically wanted.

  **Recommendation: ADOPT Visit Settlements; treat vanilla shuttle-trade as the baseline; consider Gravship Settlement Trade only if solo-ship runs feel too caravan-heavy.** Answer to the literal question: *no*, you can't set the gravship down inside a friendly town — but you were never really blocked from peaceful town interaction; the shuttle/caravan path already does it, and Visit Settlements makes it a real, consequence-bearing social scene.

**(b) Nomad travel-event generators — the pillar-clean set for a mobility campaign.** Strongest candidates: **Caravan Adventures** (WS `2558957509`, 1.6, GitHub `iforgotmysocks/CaravanAdventures`) is the anchor — it replaces caravan micromanagement with adventure features and a "sense of purpose"; ~25% is a Royalty-gated storyline that's cleanly ignorable. **Better Ambushes Continued** (WS `3422946483`, 1.6) improves the **enemy-side** world-map ambushes against *your* caravans (configurable) — the consequence layer for your own trade runs, squarely §19 qualitative threat, no power creep. **Vanilla Events Expanded** (WS `1938420742`, VE org, 1.6) adds events *and* — governance-useful — lets you tune or disable **any** event, including modded ones. Optional flavor: **Caravan Ambush** (WS `3654247407`) and **Mo'Events** — Caravan Ambush lets *you* raid passing caravans (player-initiated aggression, fits a skimmer-raider streak if wanted). **Recommendation: ADOPT Caravan Adventures (travel purpose) + Better Ambushes Continued (consequence on your runs); add Visit Settlements from (a) for the peaceful pole.** All qualitative-threat, none scalable-production — consistent with the anti-exponential pillar. **✔️1.6 CONFIRMED (Fetcher `2026-08-04h`):** Caravan Adventures About.xml `1.2–1.6`; Better Ambushes GitHub has a dedicated `/1.6/` folder; VEE About.xml `1.4/1.5/1.6`. Visit Settlements ✔️1.6; Gravship Settlement Trade ⚠️1.6-unconfirmed (optional, not a blocker). **🔎 Remaining:** Better Ambushes × CAI-5000 raid-AI compat (in-game test, same as §4A).

## 5. Implementation routes — how this actually gets built

Ordered by how much is already in hand:

1. **CONFIG (in hand today):** the resource-distribution scheme (Map Designer per-biome ore/ruins/density), biome mix (Choose Biome Commonality — amplify desert, rarefy lush), faction interest per tile, river landforms (Geological Landforms). This alone delivers "different tiles hold different things." **This is the layer we can spec fully now** and is the backbone of §2's Abundant/Exotic axes (①/③).
2. **MOD (biome + treasure side now Fetcher-confirmed; hazard/faction side still pending):** *Confirmed candidates* (IDs in §2B/§3B/§7) — Alpha Biomes, Advanced Biomes (Continued), the Quarry/Deep-Drill/Salvaging/Scavenging extraction family, Vanilla Chemfuel Expanded, Atmospheric Water Collection, Ancient_Structures. *Still pending* — thirst/water need, heat/sandstorm weather, dangerous vegetation, Hutt & rogue-android factions, the Gemstone mod, mineable boulders, VVE derelicts (Fetcher `..._gemstone_and_biome_1p6_confirm` + `..._boulders_vehicles_ruins` filed). Each must be 1.6-confirmed and pillar-checked before adoption.
3. **AUTHOR (save-edit / Tier-2, and RimBridge / Tier-2b):** placing specific landmarks (a named oasis, a rogue-droid-held ruin), and — the powerful one — **live per-map enrichment via RimBridge**: seeding a coastal map's aggressive-plant spread, dropping a resource cache in a volcanic field, spawning the territorial droids when you land. This is where "consequential landing" gets *hand-crafted per destination* instead of left to RNG, and it's exactly the Tier-2b use case `rimbridge.md` was set up for.

**The synthesis:** CONFIG makes the *rules* (tiles differ by design), MOD supplies the *hazard vocabulary* (thirst, heat, killer plants), and AUTHOR/RimBridge lets us *hand-place the memorable ones* (this oasis, that droid-held volcano). The campaign doesn't need all three before it's playable — CONFIG + a couple of MOD confirmations already delivers the core loop; AUTHOR is the enrichment polish.
