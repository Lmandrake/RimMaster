## 1. The premise as physics — a press, not a stomach

`deep_desert.md` §4 hands the sarlacc its whole biology before it is named:

> Energy is free and water is money. · Nothing eats for calories out here. **The prize is a
> full animal**, and a dried one is worthless. · Nothing rots. Bodies desiccate almost
> instantly. · Predators appraise before they act, and usually decline. · The head is a still.
> · Mass IS the water balance sheet.

Apply those to a creature that has stopped moving:

- 🔑 **It does not digest. It wrings.** The mouth is a press. What enters is squeezed — over
  years for a beast, over centuries for the whole intake — and the water goes *down* into the
  reservoir while the dry residue goes *out*. There is no dissolving, no acid, no nutrition
  in the ordinary sense. The sarlacc is a still turned inside out: the biome's animals condense
  their own breath to keep water in; the sarlacc condenses *everything else's* to take it.
- 🔑 **It is a well because everything must eventually come to water** (the item's own line).
  A predator that appraises before striking spends almost nothing if it simply *is* the water.
  So a rooted sarlacc strikes rarely and only to tithe — a drink at its rim costs a death now
  and then, and the ecosystem pays because the alternative is 20° of arc to the next drink.
- 🔑 **Mass is the balance sheet, so the size ladder IS the water ladder.** A swimmer is
  small because it carries only what it was born with. A cistern is enormous because it holds
  centuries of intake. "Really huge" and "semi-permanent" are the same fact seen twice — a
  thing holding hundreds of tons of water cannot move, and a thing that cannot move must be a
  well or die.
- ⭐ **It is the animal the silverbole is the plant of.** The sheet's tree is *inverted* — no
  leaves, no photosynthesis, grows cell by cell from a hint of moisture, nurses the seep it
  grows beside, stands among the bones of the giants that died there. The sarlacc is the same
  inversion in flesh: it grows in place from a seep, nurses that seep until it *becomes* it,
  and the bones ring it. **The two are one system** (§3), and the biome already said so about
  the tree.

⚠️ **What is deliberately NOT here:** the digested-memories intelligence (cut), any sense of
malice or hunting, any "boss" framing. A cistern is not hostile. It is a place, with a price.

## 2. The three stages

Working names for the sitting. The dead state is **the throat** — `wasteland.md`'s word for a
dead sarlacc ("a pre-dug descending throat") and already canon; a stage name that survives
death should not be invented twice.

| stage | what it is | mobile? | scale (mass = water) | lasts | engine shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I — the swimmer** | a sub-sand animal carrying its birth-water, looking for a seep | yes | large animal, solitary | decades | a pawn |
| **II — the anchored** | rooted on a seep; mouth at the surface; reservoir filling | no (semi-permanent by size) | a small pit, few tentacles | a century or three | a building + tentacle pawns |
| **III — the cistern** | the well: hundreds of tons held; the local water table; an ecosystem | no | a landmark, a dungeon | until breached; "ancient" | landmark + portal + pocket maps |
| *(end) — the throat* | dead; drained; a dry descending gullet | — | as III, empty | forever (nothing decays) | the husk delve (`sarlacc_spec.md` phase 1) |

### 2.1 Stage I — the swimmer

**What it is.** The sand is a medium, not a surface (`deep_desert.md` §4), and the swimmer is
the largest thing that swims it. It is a mouth with a body behind it — the radial beak and the
tentacle ring are already there, small, because the icon carve-out protects the sarlacc's
recognizability outright (`README_BIOME_GRAMMAR.md`) and the mouth is what a player must be able
to name at every size. Under sand it reads as a moving dune-swell; surfaced, as a pit that was
not there a moment ago.

**How it lives.** Exactly as the biome's predators do, because it is one: it hunts by
vibration, **appraises** by water, and usually declines. It never chases. It drinks a kill
where it falls — nobody caches out here. 🔑 **It does not strike a droid** — a droid carries no
water and is invisible to the food web; that is the biome's rule and the swimmer obeys it.

**Its budget.** A swimmer is born with a fixed water reserve — its egg was a canteen, like every
large egg in this biome — and every strike, every surfacing, every metre of sand spends it.
Kills top it up; misses do not. **A swimmer is therefore a countdown**, and the countdown ends
one of two ways.

**Transition trigger (I → II) — it roots, and rooting is not optional.** Two triggers, either
sufficient:
1. **It finds a seep.** A buried moisture source — a brine seep, a fossil lens, the buried
   corpse of something vast, *a silverbole's own root* (see §3 for what that means). It sinks,
   anchors, and opens the mouth at the surface. This is a *moment* — hours, a visible event —
   not a duration.
2. **Its reserve runs out.** A swimmer that has not found a seep by the time its birth-water is
   spent roots *where it is*, on nothing, because a still animal spends less than a moving one
   and rooting is the only move left. 🔑 **These are the throats.** Most swimmers root badly;
   a sarlacc rooted on dry ground is a small dead pit within the century, and the four
   `sw_DeadSarlacc` landmarks and the wasteland's throats are what that looks like a thousand
   years later. Failure is the common outcome, and the map already shows it.

**Time.** Decades mobile — order of 20–60 years. ⚠️ Placeholder; §7 asks whether any transition
happens inside a campaign at all.

### 2.2 Stage II — the anchored

**What it is.** A sarlacc that has stopped. The body has sunk and spread into the ground around
the seep; the mouth stays at the surface as a pit that **breaks the grain** — in a biome where
every yardang points the same way, a circle is the one shape that does not belong, and it is
visible for exactly that reason. The pit never fills in, whatever the sandstorms do to the
dunes around it (`sarlacc_deep_design.md` §1's dune-resistance, carried) — the tell that
something under it is keeping it clear.

**What it is doing.** Filling. Everything that comes to the seep's trace of moisture — and in
the deep desert, everything eventually does — meets the press. The reservoir grows by what is
wrung from what came; the seep itself is a trickle, and 🔑 **the sarlacc's intake outgrows
its seep long before it is a cistern.** From roughly here on it is a net *taker* of water from
the surface, not a channel for water from below.

**Surface read.** A small pit with a damp lip — the only dark sand in the biome, damp not
shiny (the mineralization ban holds: powder-glazed flesh, never wet-looking outside the mouth
itself). A few tentacles that mostly lie still. A first ring of bones. And the first
**silverbole** seedlings on the pit's downwind side, where the exhaled moisture settles — the
beginning of §3.

**Semi-permanent.** The owner's word, honoured literally: an anchored sarlacc is not
immovable — it has not yet grown into the rock — but uprooting costs the reserve it has
built, so it does so only if the seep fails outright, and it does so *as a swimmer with a
head start*, not as an anchored thing that walks. ⚠️ §7 fork: whether uprooting exists at all.

**Transition trigger (II → III) — it becomes the water table.** The threshold is hydrological,
not a birthday: the reservoir has grown until **the sarlacc holds more water than the ground
under it ever did**, and from that point the seep it rooted on is irrelevant — the sarlacc *is*
the seep. Two things are true at once from that moment: (a) it has grown into the rock and can
never move again, and (b) the surface around it has an ecosystem, because a permanent source
in a biome of absence gathers one whether or not anything intends it. The stage is named for
what it holds, not what it does.

**Time.** A century to three, set by the *traffic* — a pit on a route fills fast; a pit
nowhere never fills and dies anchored (a throat again). ⚠️ Placeholder.

### 2.3 Stage III — the cistern

**What it is.** The owner's sentence. A reservoir the size of a building, grown into the
bedrock, holding hundreds of tons of water behind flesh, with a mouth at the top and roots
that reach the caverns (crosslink ruled *yes*). It is **continuous with the ground**: there is
no seam where sarlacc ends and rock begins, because it grew into the rock the way the
silverbole grows into a bone-field, cell by cell over spans that are geological. A player who
digs at the rim finds flesh under stone under flesh.

**What it looks like from the surface** — the biome's own artistic theme, with one circle in it:

- **The pit**, wide, at the centre of a landscape of parallel lines. The mouth at the bottom is
  the *only wet surface in the biome* — glistening in a place that bans shine — and that
  contrast is the whole art direction. Everything else about the creature is pale and
  powdered like the biome's other life.
- 🔑 **The dew ring.** The reservoir breathes. Wet air rises from the mouth, and where it
  meets the biome's sterilising light it condenses on the first cool thing — the lip of the
  pit — exactly as `desert.md` describes dew forming at a shade line. So the rim of a cistern
  carries a ring of dark damp sand a few metres wide. **That is what the ecosystem drinks.**
  Nothing drinks from the reservoir; they lick its breath at the rim, which is precisely where
  the mouth can reach them. The well's toll is built into its geometry.
- **The silverbole ring** beyond the dew ring — bone-white shields, the tallest stand in the
  region, planted by the sarlacc's own exhalation and nursing it. Between the dew ring and the
  silverboles: **the apron**, the bones and wreckage of everything the press has finished with
  (§2.4).
- **Stillness.** Tentacles that do not rise for a walker. A cistern almost never strikes; it
  tithes. `deep_desert.md`: *a predator that watches you walk past is doing arithmetic* — a
  cistern's arithmetic says everything comes back.

**What it holds.** The dungeon (§4). Trapped living things. The pearls. And the roots — the
"underground tendrils surfacing across the map" of the encounter doc, given a reason: they are
the plumbing, and they reach the caverns, and a cistern can surface one wherever it has a root,
which is how a cistern that never moves can still reach you at range.

**Reproduction — the swimmer's egg.** Sky-seeding is cut, and a thing that never moves must
still make swimmers. The press voids the dry into the apron; **among the dry, occasionally, is
a clutch** — eggs the cistern has invested water in, laid out in the one place every scavenger
on the planet will walk to. 🔑 `deep_desert.md`'s birth-trap eggs are the model verbatim:
*"Approached, they hatch violently, and the newborn tries to drink the intruder as fast as it
can."* A sarlacc egg is a canteen a Jawa would cross a desert for, and some of them are
swimmers waiting for their first drink. ⚠️ §7 fork: whether these ARE the biome's birth-traps
or one species of them.

**Time.** Centuries to millennia. Ends only by breach (§4.4) or by the seep-and-traffic
starving it so slowly that it dries into a throat with the reservoir still half full — which
is what a throat with a wet floor would be, if the owner wants one (§7).

### 2.4 The press, the apron, and why the archaeology is on the surface

The press must void what it has finished with, or it fills with dry. So:

- **The apron is residue.** The bone-and-wreckage ring is not what died at the rim; it is what
  went *in* and came back out dry, pressed flat, in the order it was taken — 🔑 **a timeline in
  strata, oldest furthest out**, readable by anyone who stops and looks. This is
  `sarlacc_deep_design.md` §1's "timeline made of debris" with the mechanism supplied.
- **The interior holds the in-progress and the stuck.** Things mid-wring (years), things too
  large to void, things lodged. And one class it can never void: 🔑 **droids.** The press
  cannot finish with what it cannot start on — a droid holds no water, so the sarlacc never
  registers it as done, and it stays. **A cistern is full of the one thing it could not
  drink**, and for a Jawa clan that is the richest ground on the planet inside the richest
  ground on the planet.
- **Nothing rots**, so nothing in either place has decayed. Every stratum of the apron and
  every chamber of the interior is intact, mummified, in whatever state of technology it
  arrived in — `deep_desert.md` §7's preservation rule, concentrated at one site.

## 3. The ecosystem it builds

The biome allows exactly three shade sources — silverboles, wreckage, caverns — and bans green
in the open and large herds. A cistern must build an ecosystem **without adding a fourth shade
source and without adding green.** It does, because the biome already has the parts:

| part | what it gives | what it takes |
|---|---|---|
| **the cistern** | the dew ring (water), the apron (shade under wreckage, when it has any) | a tithe of drinkers; the tenants' waste and dead |
| **the silverbole ring** | the shade — rented, per the sheet; the region's tallest stand | the cistern's breath; the tenants' rent |
| **the tenants** | the ring's rent; the cistern's tithe; the fliers' prey | shade from the ring, water from the rim, and their turn at the mouth |
| **the fliers** | the biome's only organic import — guano, dropped prey | a drink at the rim on their way through; a nest the ground cannot reach |
| **the Deep Desert Tribes** | the rite (§3.1); a holding, never near water — but *this* water is not on the ban's list, see §5 | a drink; the apron's scavenge; a place to forget things |

- **Predators at a cistern do not hunt.** They own a shadow and wait — the desert's rule, moved
  one biome deeper. The cistern's own toll keeps their numbers honest.
- **Nothing large competes near it.** `sarlacc_deep_design.md` §5's "safer a short walk out"
  holds: the ring of ground *between* the silverboles and open desert is the quietest ground in
  the region, and that is where a clan camps.
- 🔴 **Killing the cistern kills all of this.** Not as a flavour line — §4.4 says what actually
  happens, in order.

### 3.1 The rite of offering and forgetting

The owner's reading of evidence-feeding, honoured without the memory-compile: the tribes throw
into the mouth what they want *gone* — a body, a weapon, a name, a debt — and the sarlacc takes
it. The water in it joins the reservoir; the thing itself is wrung, and **a century later the
press voids it, dry, into the apron.** What the tribe forgot is what a Jawa digs up. The rite
is sincere, the forgetting is real, and the sarlacc keeps the receipt where the next scavenger
will find it. This is the ownership fabric's dead-end without a single line of mind-fiction:
geology files the serial numbers off, slowly, and hands them back.

Sits beside the tribes' Sun-Debt (`FACTION_SPEC.md`) as its natural partner — fire is what they
cannot afford; water is what they owe.

## 4. The dungeon module

Two dungeons, one layout. **The cistern** is the live one; **the throat** is the same layout
drained, and a cistern the player breaches *becomes* a throat — so every dungeon a player
finishes is still on the map afterwards, dry, as the record of what they did. This is the
dead/live ladder `sarlacc_spec.md` §1 already wanted, with the conversion made literal.

### 4.1 Layout — the three chambers, top to bottom

The wringing premise sets the vertical order and the spec's stacked pocket maps
(`sarlacc_spec.md` §3, nesting measured legal) carry it:

| level | name | what it is | what a clan meets |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **the press** | the throat behind the mouth: a ring of muscle that is still squeezing. Walls with things pinned in them, recent arrivals still wet | the tentacle pawns (`sarlacc_spec.md`'s lord-anchored creature) and the press itself — a floor that *takes* what stands on it too long |
| **2** | **the gallery** | the strata: centuries of the pressed and the stuck, in layers, oldest deepest. Dry. Silent | the loot layer. The stuck droids (§2.4). Beasts, machines, people, in any state of technology, mummified. The wringing of something huge can be walked through as a room |
| **3** | **the reservoir** | the cistern proper: standing water in the dark, hundreds of tons, the only wet, cool, dark place for 20° of arc. A shore | the trapped living (§4.2); the pearls on the floor (§4.3); the roots leaving through the walls toward the caverns; and **the wall itself**, which is the kill (§4.4) |

**Two entrances** (crosslink ruled *yes*): the mouth, down through the press; and the roots,
up from the cavern layer into the reservoir — which skips the press entirely and is how a
cavern expedition finds a cistern by accident from below, standing on a shore before it knows
what it is in.

**Being swallowed is a route in** — carried from the spec unchanged. A pawn taken at the rim
lands on level 1, in the press, and the press is slow: a strong pawn climbs out of it into the
gallery. That is the only reason level 3 has a shore population.

### 4.2 The trapped living

The reservoir shore holds what climbed out of the press alive and could not climb further:
survivors of a caravan, a hermit, adapted droids that walked down through the press
untouched and have no reason to leave. They live because the reservoir is the one place in the
biome with water and dark and no radiation — **the sarlacc is keeping them alive with its own
water, and it does not mind, because everything comes back to the mouth eventually.** They
drink, and are drunk. A survivor here is the escape quest's target, drawn from the encounter
doc's list, never invented fresh.

### 4.3 The pearls

The owner's word for castings. In a press, a pearl is what the water *cannot carry*: the
mineral and salt of everything the cistern has ever wrung, packed by centuries of pressure into
a concretion on the reservoir floor — a kidney stone the size of a fist, made of the whole
apron's worth of intake. 🔑 They are the only mineralized thing the sarlacc makes, and they are
inside it, where the biome's no-mineral rule does not reach the eye. Quality rule carried from
the proposal: a pearl taken from a breached, drained cistern is cracked by the flood; one lifted
from a living shore, by a crew that went down and came back up, is whole. With many cisterns
there are many pearls; the spec's "ONE ever" is a casualty of the 09-02 ruling (§6).

### 4.4 Killing one — the breach, and what actually happens

**You cannot kill a cistern from the surface.** Fire routs, and a thing grown into bedrock
cannot rout; the mouth closes and the tentacles regrow. There is no hit-point bar on a
landmark. 🔑 **The kill is the breach: cut the reservoir wall from inside, and the water goes.**
Which means the deed is done standing on the shore of level 3, with a charge, by a crew that
has already seen the trapped living and the pearls and the droids — the choice is made *in the
room*, not from a menu.

Then, in order:

1. **The levels flood upward.** `sarlacc_spec.md` §3's collapse is repointed: the ~25000-tick
   timer, the staged rumble, the roof-drops — but what rises is water, and the map dies by
   drowning everyone left. Level 3 first, then the gallery, then the press. The escape climb is
   the third act, ahead of the water.
2. **The surface floods.** The pit overflows; a radius of sand becomes mud and shallow water.
   🔑 **This is the "struck a well" moment and it lasts exactly as long as the biome says a
   flood lasts**: *"the ground blooms within hours and is dead again within days."* The
   anhydrobiotic sand wakes — a bloom of fast plants and grain-scale life around the pit — and
   desiccates on the biome's schedule. **The well you struck is exactly as big as the tanks
   you brought.** A clan with storage fills it; a clan without has spent an ecosystem for a
   three-day garden. The instrument exists in the live list — `dubwise.dubsbadhygiene.thirst`
   and `.lite` are active in `ModsConfig.xml` (read 2026-09-06) — but ⚠️ `water_doctrine.md`
   holds that the v1 engine has *no* water layer; §7 asks which is true at launch.
3. **The ecosystem dies.** The dew ring is gone the moment the reservoir is. Tenants die of
   thirst over days or scatter into the open and are sterilised. The fliers stop coming. The
   silverbole ring does not die — it is geological — but it stops, and the tribes know what a
   ring of silverboles with no circle inside it means.
4. **The landmark converts.** `sw_Sarlacc` → the dead landmark at the same tile; the portal
   becomes the husk portal; the pocket maps regenerate as the *same layout drained* — the
   gallery's strata still there, the shore now a dry floor with a cracked pearl on it and the
   drowned. The player's dungeon becomes the player's monument.
5. **The region answers.** The Deep Desert Tribe holdings that drank here lose their water; a
   faction that tithes to a well does not forgive its killer — goodwill, and a holding that
   must now take water from someone, which is you. Neighbouring tiles' rosters lose whatever
   the cistern's fliers and tenants fed. ⚠️ Scope of "region" is a §7 fork.

**Poison as a slower kill** — the Glowing Throat proves a *dead* sarlacc accepts a millennium of
casks; whether a *live* one can be poisoned through the rite (offer it something that fouls the
reservoir over years) is a §7 fork. If yes, it is the only kill that does not require going
down, and it is the coward's kill: the ecosystem dies just the same and there is no flood to
catch.

### 4.5 Returning changed — a menu, not a rule

Ruled: *"Returning from 'that place' leaves you permanently changed... many such explanations,
all disturbing and interesting."* Each below is a hediff-shaped idea grounded in the press, the
brine or the dark, not in memory. Owner picks any number; none is chosen here.

| name | what it is | the gift | the cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the wrung** | the press took water the body will never hold again | thirst need runs slower — the body learned to hold | less blood, slower healing, a gauntness that never leaves |
| **salt-eyed** | weeks of the reservoir's brine in the eyes | immune to the biome's glare | sight permanently clouded |
| **the stillness** | learned the predator's arithmetic on the shore | never panics; no fear-driven break | cannot run — a hard cap on move speed, forever |
| **the tithe** | the body carries the dew ring's scent | — | every appraising predator in the biome reads the pawn as *full* and strikes it first; a cistern's roots surface nearer to it. It wants them back |
| **the bloom** | anhydrobiotic spores lodged in the skin | — | whenever the pawn is wet, something briefly grows on them — a cosmetic tell that no one who sees it forgets |
| **pressed** | a limb or a ribcage the press flattened and released | pain in that part is gone — nothing left to hurt | breathing or manipulation reduced, permanently |
| **taken-and-returned** | the tribes' word for one who came back | sacred to the Sun-Debt tribes | or unclean to them — the sitting's call, and it could be both by holding |

## 5. The standing-water carve-out — exact wording

`deep_desert.md` §6 ban 5 currently reads: *"No green in the open, and no standing surface
water except brine seeps."* The wrong fix is to add "and sarlaccs" — that opens every pit and
every wet thing to argument. The right fix scopes the exception by what the water **is**, so
that nothing else can qualify:

> 5. 🔴 **No green in the open, and no standing surface water except brine seeps.** A living
>    cistern-stage sarlacc holds water that is **neither standing nor surface**: it is inside a
>    body, behind flesh, reachable only through the mouth or by breaching the reservoir. It
>    never exists as a terrain cell in the open. It reaches the surface in exactly two forms,
>    both permitted: the **dew ring** (damp sand at the pit's lip — damp, never pooled, never
>    potable by walking) and the **breach flood** (transient, on the biome's own bloom clock —
>    hours to wake, days to die — after which the ground is dry desert and the sarlacc is a
>    throat). A swimmer, an anchored sarlacc and a throat hold no water this rule need name.

Three tests, any one failing disqualifies:
1. **Contained in a living Stage III.** Not a pit, not a pool, not a "sarlacc-fed oasis", not a
   throat with a damp floor. The water is the animal's.
2. **Never a terrain cell in the open.** Inside the pocket map, yes; on the surface, only as damp
   sand (dew ring) or as the timed flood. A generator that paints a water tile next to a cistern
   has broken the biome exactly as much as one that paints a pond.
3. **Not potable by walking.** Reaching it costs the mouth. This is what keeps
   `water_doctrine.md` ruling 2 honest (*potable water is defended, always*) — the defender is
   the well itself, and it is the reason the tribes are there.

**And the green ban is untouched.** A cistern adds water without adding green: its ring is
silverboles (leafless, white), its tenants are animals, and the only green it ever produces is
the three-day bloom of its own death, which is the biome's existing flood exception. **No
fourth shade source** either — the shade is the ring's, and the ring is one of the three.

## 7. Forks for the owner — two defensible directions each, none picked

1. **Do transitions happen in play?** (a) *Authored only* — every swimmer, anchored pit and
   cistern is placed; nothing changes stage during a campaign; the timescales are lore. Cheapest,
   frozen-map-clean. (b) *Swimmers root in play* — a swimmer that finds a seep or runs dry
   anchors on the map, visibly, creating a new Stage II the player watched happen; II → III
   never happens in play. This is creature behaviour on a map, not worldgen, but it puts new
   pits on a frozen world and the owner should say whether that is wanted.
2. **How many cisterns, and where?** (a) *Two to four, deep desert only* — Glare, Long Sand,
   Dry Marches; every one campaign-scale; move or demote the Dew Belt one. (b) *A ladder* —
   keep the Dew Belt cistern as the one a clan can breach *without* ending a region (water is
   near; the tribes there do not need it): the pearl-hunter's sarlacc, the one that teaches the
   layout; the deep-desert cisterns are the ones with a world attached. (b) is the more
   generous design and the more expensive one.
3. **Can an anchored sarlacc uproot?** (a) *No* — anchoring is one-way; "semi-permanent" means
   killable, not mobile. Simpler, and it makes the swimmer's choice of seep the whole drama of
   Stage I. (b) *Yes, once, at ruinous cost* — a Stage II whose seep fails becomes a swimmer
   again with what it has saved, which is why some swimmers are enormous.
4. **Are the sarlacc's eggs THE birth-trap eggs?** (a) *Yes* — the deep desert sheet's
   "some eggs are birth traps" line is the sarlacc's clutch and nothing else's; one mechanism,
   one creature, the biome's most valuable egg and its most dangerous are the same object. (b)
   *One species of several* — the sheet's line stays general and the sarlacc clutch is the
   apex case. (a) is tighter; (b) keeps the biome's roster free.
5. **Poison as a kill route?** (a) *No* — the only kill is the breach, from the shore, in
   person; the deed cannot be delegated. (b) *Yes, slow* — the rite can be turned: years of
   offering something that fouls the reservoir, until the cistern dies without a flood. The
   coward's kill, with all of the ecosystem cost and none of the water.
6. **What the water is worth at launch.** `water_doctrine.md`: v1 engine = no water layer.
   `ModsConfig.xml` today: DBH thirst + lite are active. (a) *v1 ships the breach as spectacle
   and ecosystem-death only*; the "struck a well" payoff waits for the v2 water layer and is
   expressed then in bottles. (b) *If DBH thirst is in the shipped list, the flood is
   fillable at launch* and the design should say so in the item. This is a fact question first
   and a ruling second — the owner knows which list ships.
7. **Scope of "campaign-scale."** (a) *The tile and its tribe* — the cistern's map, its
   landmark, one faction's goodwill and the nearest holding. (b) *The arc* — every tile within
   N° loses its cistern-dependent roster entries; the fliers' nests empty; a named region's
   character changes. (b) is what the item's language promises; (a) is what can be built and
   verified first.
8. **Which of §4.5's changes ship, and how many a survivor carries.** One per survivor, rolled?
   All that apply to how they were taken (pressed for the press, salt-eyed for the shore)? The
   table is a menu; the rule is the owner's.
9. **Stage names.** *Swimmer / anchored / cistern / throat* is this doc's working set, in the
   sheets' plain-noun register. The tribes will have their own words and the owner may want
   those to be the shipped labels.
