# The Terminator Sea — biome definition sheet

_First pass, 2026-09-05, to the grammar in `README_BIOME_GRAMMAR.md`._

## 🔑 The lush rule, in three parts (owner, 2026-09-05) — and this biome is not in it

> *"The terminator is solitary not the only place where lush exists. In fact it's not
> lush there. The rivers and coasts are the only lush areas on the dayside. And then
> there will be a very different definition of lush using very alien life forms on the
> night side."*

1. **Dayside:** lush exists **only along rivers and coasts** — a thin riparian and
   littoral LINE, never a region, drawn against the vast empty dune.
2. **Terminator:** 🔴 **not lush. SOLITARY.** Sparse, singular, lonely.
3. **Nightside:** a **different definition of lush** entirely, built from very alien
   life forms — dense, but not green and not plants. (See `nightside_ice.md` §10.)

⇒ This sheet's job is therefore the opposite of what a coastal biome usually does. It
has open water, mild temperature and permanent light — every ingredient abundance
normally needs — and it must explain **why abundance does not happen here anyway.**
The answer is salt, wind and isolation, and all three are already in the canon.

## 1. What it is

A white salt pan under a permanent horizontal wind, with a dying sea on one side and
a single black sail standing on it, throwing a shadow that runs to the horizon.

The star sits on the rim of the world and does not move, so the light comes in flat
and reddish from one bearing forever. Anything that stands up out of the ground casts
a shadow a kilometre long — and there is so little standing up that you can count the
shadows. One blade of dark tissue here. Nothing for twenty minutes' walk. Another
one. Each is enormous, each is alone, and the ground between them is salt crust that
crunches and shows your footprints for years.

The wind never stops and never changes direction. It comes off the nightside, cold
and dry, crosses the water, and goes on dayward, and it takes a little more of the
sea with it every year. You can see where the sea used to be: **terraces**, ring after
ring of stranded shoreline stepping down toward water that has retreated from all of
them. Salt works built on a beach sit two hours' walk inland now, with nothing between
them and the water but their own older selves.

And the sea itself is **one thing**. Not a community — a single organism, or near
enough, a mat that covers it from shore to shore, and in the whole body of water there
is almost nothing else alive. Something very large moves under it, occasionally, and
it is the only one for a hundred kilometres, and it is the last of the only lineage
this particular sea ever produced.

## 2. Planetary position

**Terminator band (θ ≈ 63-117°), around +14 °C at θ 90**, on and around open water —
the **Twilight Sea** (θ 91, bearing 170, *moldy*) and the **Grey Sea** (θ 92,
bearing 8, *salt-encrusted, shrinking*). Both are hypersaline; both are terminal
sinks with no outflow.

- **Energy regime:** permanent low-angle light. It never stops, but at a grazing
  incidence the flux per unit ground is a fraction of the dayside's, and there is no
  season and no noon to make up the difference.
- **Temperature:** mild and stable — ordinary carbon-and-water biochemistry runs here
  and nowhere else on the planet. That is real and it is not enough.
- **Anomaly:** ⭐ **standing hypersaline water on the day→night convection line** —
  a sea that is being evaporated by a wind that never turns, in a basin with no exit.

### Why the terminator's coast is salt and the Scald's coast is jungle 🔴
The owner's rule says *rivers and coasts* are the dayside's lush lines, so the word
"coast" needs disambiguating or this sheet will be read wrong:

| | **the Scald and the dayside rivers** | **the terminator seas** |
|---|---|---|
| water | fed by rain condensed at altitude, moving | terminal, no outflow, going nowhere |
| salinity | usable at the inflow | **saturated brine** |
| light | +38 to +58 °C, overhead, fierce | grazing, weak, from one bearing |
| result | 🌿 **a fierce green line** — the planet's only jungle | 🧂 **salt crust and solitude** |

⇒ Lushness on Ash'karr is not bought by water. It is bought by **moving fresh water
plus a real photon budget**, and the terminator has neither. It has standing brine
and a low sun. Those make a *habitable* place, not an abundant one.

### Why the wind is one-way, and why the seas are dying 🔴
The global circulation is a single cell: air rises at the substellar point, travels
aloft to the nightside, sinks at the antistellar point, and returns **along the
surface from night to day**. So the terminator's surface wind is **cold, dry
nightside air moving dayward**, permanently, from a fixed bearing.

- It crosses the water, loads with vapour, and **carries it dayward, where it warms
  and never condenses.** The sea's water is being pumped onto the dayside and lost.
- Nothing brings it back: there are no meridian rivers (canon — *"no meridian river
  exists"*), and rain only condenses at altitude on the dayside.
- ⇒ **The terminator seas are evaporating and will not be refilled.** Canon already
  says the Grey Sea is shrinking; this is why, and the Twilight Sea is on the same
  road more slowly. Every year the brine gets stronger and the shore steps back.

⇒ 🔑 **The salinity is the biome's engine, and it is still rising.** This is a habitat
that is getting harder to live in on a timescale a colony can notice.

### Boundary with the Poison Forest ⚠️
Both biomes sit in the same θ band and must not blur. They are separated by
**anomaly, not position**: the poison forest is terminator + **cold gas vents** —
chemosynthetic, crowded, busy, wet-black and encrusted. The terminator sea is
terminator + **standing brine** — photosynthetic, empty, white, windswept and dry to
the touch. 🔑 The poison forest is *quiet and full*. The terminator sea is *loud and
empty*. They interleave along the band and a tile is one or the other.

## 3. Driving forces

**Weak permanent light from one fixed bearing, a wind that only ever blows one way,
and a terminal sea concentrating toward saturation with no way out.** Life here is
limited not by energy and not by water but by **osmosis** — and osmotic limits do not
reduce the amount of life, they reduce the **number of kinds**.

## 4. How the biology adapted

**Huge biomass, almost no diversity, enormous spacing. That is what solitary means
here, and it is a measurable claim, not a mood.**

- ⭐ **A saturated brine admits about three solutions to the same problem, so about
  three lineages exist.** Osmoregulating against saturated salt is expensive and
  there are very few ways to do it; whichever organism finds one first takes
  *everything*, because there is no second niche to be beaten into. ⇒ The Twilight
  Sea is *moldy* (canon) because it is **one mat**, shore to shore, uncontested for
  a geological age. The Grey Sea holds a different one. Each sea is a monoculture at
  planetary scale.
- ⭐ **Each sea is an island, so each sea is endemic and alone.** Three waters on the
  planet, torn, hundreds of kilometres apart, separated by lethal desert in one
  direction and lethal cold in the other. Nothing crosses. ⇒ **Every large organism
  in a terminator sea exists in that sea and nowhere else in the universe**, has no
  relatives, and cannot be replaced if it is fished out. Solitude is not just spacing
  — it is *taxonomic*. A player who kills the last one has killed the last one.
- **The shore flora is sparse, huge, and individually spaced.** With the star fixed
  on one bearing, the surface that intercepts most light is a **vertical plate facing
  it** — height buys nothing, tracking is impossible, and a crown of leaves would sit
  edge-on to the only light there is. But the salt allows very few individuals, so
  the result is not a palisade: it is **isolated blades, tens of metres tall, spaced
  hundreds of metres apart**, each one centuries old and each one alone.
  ⇒ ⚠️ **This is the correction the owner's ruling forced, and it is a better image
  than the one it replaced:** not a striped forest, but *one black sail on a white pan
  with a shadow running out of frame*.
- **The blades are dark inside and white outside.** They must absorb hard in weak
  light, so the tissue is black-green — but they must also dump salt, and the cheapest
  way is to excrete it onto the outer surface. ⇒ **A rime of white crystal over dark
  tissue**, flaking off in the wind and rebuilding, so the ground downwind of every
  blade is a salt drift shaped like the blade. Distinct on sight from the poison
  forest's metal-weeping trunks, and derived from a different waste product.
- **The permanent shadow is a habitat.** A kilometre-long shadow that has never moved
  is the only shade in the biome and the only ground where condensate survives.
  ⇒ Everything that is not the blade lives **in the blade's shadow lane** — one
  narrow line of association per plant, running dayward, and empty ground either side.
  Gameplay: the lanes are the cover, the water and the ambush ground, and they are
  isolated from each other. Travelling here means crossing open salt between islands
  of shade.
- **Fog is the only drink on Ash'karr, and there is not much of it.** Cold night air
  over warmer water makes thin advection fog on the nightward shore — real, but
  intermittent and shallow, and nowhere near enough to grow anything on. What it is
  enough for is **condensation on a surface**: the blades run it down grooved faces
  to their roots, animals lick it off them, and people string nets. ⇒ 🔑 The seas are
  not drinkable and never will be; **fog condensate is the planet's only free potable
  water**, and it arrives a cupful at a time. That is exactly why canon puts 33
  Homestead Defense League sites on this margin — *stores water, has no source.*
  They are scraping, not farming.
- **Giants, for a reason unique to this biome: osmotic surface area.** Everything
  here is fighting to keep water inside a body surrounded by saturated brine, and the
  loss runs across a *surface* while the reserve is a *volume*. Bigger is
  categorically better, and there is no predation pressure worth speaking of to
  offset it. ⇒ **The terminator's animals are enormous, extremely long-lived, very
  few, and very far apart.** A single individual may be the entire visible fauna of a
  day's travel — which is the biome's whole character in one encounter.
- **Nothing here schools, flocks, herds or packs.** Grouping is a strategy for
  places with many individuals and many predators. This place has neither.
  ⇒ 🔴 **Solitary is a checkable property**: fauna defs here are single-spawn,
  low-density, long-lived and high-mass. A herd def is a violation of this sheet.
- ⚠️ **Colour signalling barely gets off the ground.** Constant light means a colour
  *would* be reliable — but display signalling only pays when there is somebody to
  signal to, and here there usually is not. ⇒ What little colour exists is **mate-
  finding across enormous distance** — a single high-contrast mark visible from
  kilometres, on an otherwise white-and-black animal. Not a chromatic biome. One
  bright mark on a lonely thing.

## 5. Always true

- The star is **on the horizon, on one fixed bearing**; every shadow is long,
  parallel and permanent.
- **The wind blows, always, from the nightside toward the dayside.** There is no calm
  and no reversal.
- **The seas are hypersaline, terminal and retreating**, and the stranded shorelines
  are visible as terraces.
- **The ground is salt crust**, white, and it takes a footprint.
- **Diversity is minimal and biomass is not.** Few kinds, large individuals, huge
  gaps.
- **The sea is not potable; thin fog condensate is** — and it is the only free water
  on Ash'karr.
- **Every large organism here is endemic to its own sea** and has no population
  anywhere else.

## 6. Never true 🔴 (hard bans — checkable)

- ⛔ **No lush flora, no dense vegetation, no closed cover, no jungle, no forest.**
  🔴 The terminator is **not** a lush band. Checkable: no tile in this biome may
  exceed a low plant-density ceiling, and lush plant defs are barred outright.
  *(Dayside lush is confined to river and coast lines; nightside "lush" is a different
  thing entirely — see `nightside_ice.md` §10.)*
- ⛔ **No trunk-and-crown trees, no broadleaf canopy, no overhead canopy layer.**
  Flora is planar, vertical, and widely spaced.
- ⛔ **No heliotropism, no sun-tracking, no plants that open and close.**
- ⛔ **No deciduous, annual, seasonal, dormant or flowering-season behaviour.** No
  growth season, harvest season or seasonal graphic — there are no seasons.
- ⛔ **No potable sea water, no freshwater lake, no freshwater organisms.**
- ⛔ **No rain, no thunderstorms, no snow, no ice.** At +14 °C with no lift, the only
  water delivery is thin fog.
- ⛔ **No calm.** The wind is a permanent condition of the biome, not a weather event.
- ⛔ **No herds, flocks, schools, packs or swarms.** 🔴 **Solitary is enforceable:**
  fauna are single-spawn and low-density by def.
- ⛔ **No high species count.** Checkable: the terminator sea's roster is capped at a
  handful of large organisms, and a rich fauna list is a violation of the sheet's
  central claim.
- ⛔ **No nocturnal or diurnal fauna** — there is still no night here.
- ⛔ **No cold gas vents, no metal-plating trunks, no toxic-by-default meat.** Those
  belong to the poison forest.
- ⛔ **No instantly-nameable Earth organisms** (standing recognizability ban) — 🔴 and
  the giants make it strictest here, per the amendment: a recognisable *large* animal
  is the worst admission this sheet could make.

## 7. Uniquely available

- ⭐ **Water you can drink**, condensed from fog — the only free source on the planet,
  in small quantities, and therefore the reason every faction on Ash'karr has a stake
  in this band.
- **Salt, brine chemistry and evaporites** at industrial scale, from a resource that
  is *increasing in concentration* as the seas retreat. The Grey Sea is the planet's
  chemical works.
- **Mild temperature** — the only outdoor band where an unprotected human is
  comfortable, and the reason the Homesteaders exist at all.
- **Single enormous carcasses.** One animal is a season's provisioning: mass, oil,
  plate, gut chemistry. ⚠️ And it may genuinely be the last one.
- **Structural blade-sheet** — the only large workable non-metal building material on
  the planet, from a plant it takes a century to replace.
- ⚠️ **Not abundance, not farmland, not cover, not company.** Anyone here is far from
  anyone else, on purpose or otherwise.

## 8. Inhabited objects

Everything built here is built to **catch water, hold salt, or stand in the wind**,
and everything is oriented to the same fixed bearing because the wind and the light
share it.

- **Fog nets and condenser walls**, strung across the wind, sometimes for kilometres.
  A working net is wealth and a cut net is an act of war.
- **Cisterns, and fortifications around cisterns.** ⛔ Not wells — the water is in the
  air, not the ground.
- **Salt works, and older salt works stranded inland** by the retreat: a legible
  timeline of the sea dying, and free surveying for anyone who can read it.
- **Homestead holdings** built in the lee of a single blade, using it as one wall —
  🔑 a settlement per plant, which is itself an image of the biome's spacing.
- **Ruins here are salt-buried and wind-scoured**, white-crusted and structurally
  intact — a third ruin-language distinct from the dayside's clean burial and the
  poison forest's corrosion.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"One black sail on a white pan, in the light of a sunset that is not going
anywhere, with the wind never stopping."**

Palette: **salt white and pale grey** ground, dominant and everywhere · **black-green
and near-black** in the few standing plants · **low warm amber** on every starward
face, because grazing light is reddened light · **dull steel and bone** in the water,
which is never blue · and one small **saturated accent** per animal, used once. The
palette is deliberately **bleached** — this is a bright, pale, high-key biome, and it
is the poison forest's exact opposite in value as well as in mood.

Light: **one hard bearing, near-horizontal, warm; long parallel shadows; a
white-hot rim on every starward edge and darkness on every other face.** ⭐ The
defining shot is **a single blade with a shadow leaving the frame, and nothing else
in it.** If a terminator-sea render contains three interesting things, it is wrong.

Silhouette language: **one vertical in a wide horizontal.** Blades, sails and fins —
clean-edged, huge, salt-rimed, isolated. Animals are **massive, smooth and slow**,
half-submerged or standing in the shallows, and drawn so that the empty space around
them is most of the picture. Nothing is ragged; the poison forest owns ragged.
Nothing is crowded; nothing here has ever been crowded.

Sound, because it matters as much as the palette: **constant wind, salt hiss, and
one animal noise a long way off.**

## Roster consequences (the sheet is the admission test)

**Wanted:** a very small number of very large organisms — planar salt-rimed blade
flora, spaced hundreds of metres apart · a single mat/film organism per sea · one or
two enormous, ancient, **solitary** brine animals per sea, endemic to it · shadow-lane
detritivores and condensate-drinkers · shore scavengers on stranded carcasses.
**Barred on sight:** anything lush or densely vegetated · trunk-and-crown trees ·
anything that herds, flocks, schools or swarms · anything seasonal or deciduous ·
sun-trackers · freshwater life · anything imported from the poison forest's chemistry
· anything instantly nameable.
🔴 **The population target is the point of the sheet.** A rich terminator roster is a
*failure*, not a success — this biome is defined by how few kinds it has and how far
apart they stand. Where another sheet asks "does this creature fit?", this one also
asks **"do we already have one?"** — and usually the answer that keeps the biome
right is *no more*.
