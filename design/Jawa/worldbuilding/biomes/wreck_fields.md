# The Wreck Fields — biome definition sheet

# 🔴 UNRULED — BENCH'S PROPOSAL, FOR THE OWNER TO ACCEPT OR REJECT

_BENCH, 2026-09-05. **Nothing in this sheet is canon.** The other three sheets
written today describe places the map already has; this one proposes a place the map
does not have yet. It exists because `README_BIOME_GRAMMAR.md` lists "ancient wreck
fields" among the ring-breaking anomalies, and because the owner's world is a
scavenger world — but an anomaly named in a list is not an anomaly that works. This
sheet's job is to **argue the case honestly and let the owner kill it.**_

> ⚖️ **BENCH's verdict up front: it works, but only on one premise.** The wreck
> fields are a biome **if and only if some of the wrecks are still faintly powered.**
> Cold wreckage is a landmark set — scenery, salvage, a map decoration. A wreck that
> is still putting out two hundred watts of waste heat at −25 °C is a **point energy
> anomaly with an ecology around it**, which is the same category of thing as a vent
> or a sea, and that is what makes it a biome. ⇒ **If the owner rejects live power
> sources, he should reject this biome with them** and demote wreck fields to
> landmarks. There is no half version worth building. See §10.

## 1. What it is

A cold grey plain, scattered with the intact hulls of ships that fell out of the sky,
and every hull that is still warm has a garden around it.

The wrecks are not rusty. That is the first thing that is wrong with the place. It is
too cold and too dry for metal to corrode, so a hull that came down two thousand years
ago is still **bright** — scoured, dented, split open, but silver, and it throws hard
reflections across a landscape that has nothing else shiny in it. From a distance a
wreck field reads as a scatter of mirrors on slate.

Most of them are dead, and around a dead one there is nothing at all — bare frozen
ground right up to the plating. But a few are still running something, deep inside,
badly, and around each of those is a **circle of green-black life a few hundred
metres across**, sharply edged, warm enough to melt frost, and utterly unlike the
next one along. The organisms in one circle are titanium-white; in the next they are
verdigris; in the third they are dark and heavy and make the counter tick. They are
built from what is leaching out of the hull they grew on, and **each patch is the
colour of its own ship.**

Between the circles: nothing, frozen, and the tracks of things crossing.

## 2. Planetary position (proposed)

**The nightward flank of the terminator, θ ≈ 100-135°, roughly −5 °C to −30 °C.**
This is where canon already puts the Junkers — *"past the terminator on the warm
downwind flank, plus the old mining fields"* (owner, 2026-08-18) — so the proposal
does not move anybody; it explains them.

- **Energy regime:** effectively none from the sky. Twilight fading to dark.
- **Temperature:** cold enough that **water is not liquid and metal cannot corrode**,
  warm enough that a few hundred watts of waste heat can make a puddle. That narrow
  window is doing all the work and it is only a few tens of degrees wide.
- **Anomaly:** ⭐ **crashed ancient tech — as a substrate, a shelter, and, in the
  living cases, a heat source.**

### Why wrecks CONCENTRATE here — two independent mechanisms 🔴
A scatter of wrecks is not a biome. A biome needs the wrecks to be **dense**, and
they are dense here for two unrelated reasons that happen to agree:

1. **Atmospheric.** The dayside atmosphere is hot and inflated — a large scale
   height, thin at altitude, so an object entering over the dayside begins
   decelerating high, heats for longer, breaks up, and arrives as **fragments and
   dust**. The nightside atmosphere is cold and collapsed — dense, but low — so an
   object crossing into it **stays fast much further down**, meets the thick air
   late, and arrives **largely intact**. ⇒ Dayside arrivals are scattered debris;
   nightward arrivals are recognisable ships. The distinction is a real one and it
   is checkable in the art.
2. **Behavioural.** The terminator is the only habitable band on the planet, so it is
   where every ship for ten thousand years has tried to arrive. Failed approaches
   fall **short of** the thing they were aiming at — and short, on a night-to-day
   approach, is nightward. ⇒ Ten millennia of bad landings pile up on the same lane.

⇒ Physics and history point at the same strip. That is why this biome cannot simply
be moved: dayward of the terminator the wrecks are dust and there is no cold; deep
nightward the approach lane never went and there is no heat window.

### Why the substrate PERSISTS 🔴
Corrosion needs an electrolyte. There is no liquid water here and no rain, so a hull
does not rust away — it lasts on a **geological** timescale, which is the difference
between a biome and a ruin. But then nothing leaches either, and leaching is what the
ecology needs. The resolution is the nightside's own chemistry: **ammonia frost
migrating up from the cold trap is an electrolyte.** Where it contacts alloy it
corrodes it hard and locally, and the products run downslope. ⇒ Each hull grows a
**metal-salt apron** below it, sharply bounded, with a composition set by that ship's
alloys. ⛔ Not a general contamination of the region — a **patchwork at the scale of
a single vehicle.**

## 3. Driving forces

**A cold, dry, non-corroding plain where ten thousand years of intact wreckage has
concentrated, where ammonia frost leaches each hull into its own private soil, and
where a handful of machines are still warm.** Everything else follows.

## 4. How the biology adapted

- ⭐ **Each live wreck is an oasis with no geology behind it.** A decayed pile or an
  old RTG at a few hundred watts is nothing to an engineer and everything to an
  ecosystem: at −25 °C it holds a pocket of ground above freezing, and around it
  there is **liquid water** — the only liquid water for a thousand kilometres in the
  nightward direction. So the biome is an **archipelago**: dozens of hectare-scale
  ecosystems, each one complete, each one isolated, with dead ground between.
- 🔴 **Every organism is built from its host hull, so the wildlife is colour-coded by
  the ship it grew on.** Life on a metal-salt apron must handle the metals, and the
  cheapest way is to precipitate them **outward into a shell or plating** — but each
  apron offers a *different* metal, so the shells differ: white and matte on a
  titanium hull, blue-green on copper alloys, grey and dense and faintly warm on the
  ones you should not stand near. ⇒ ⚠️ **This must not be drawn like the poison
  forest.** There, plating is *metabolic waste from vent fluid*, uniform across the
  whole biome, and the forms are ragged and encrusted. Here it is *sequestration from
  a point source*, **different in every patch**, and the forms are hard-edged and
  geometric because they are crystallising a single alloy rather than a soup.
  Two biomes with metal in the tissue and no visual overlap at all.
- **Everything lives inside something.** A hull is the largest cavity in a landscape
  that has no trees and no caves — a windbreak, a radiation shield, and a roof.
  ⇒ The fauna is **cavity-nesting to a fault**: nothing here builds, everything
  occupies. Compartments, ducts, tanks, holds. ⇒ Gameplay: this is the only outdoor
  biome on Ash'karr with genuine **interiors and verticality** — a firefight in a
  wreck field is fought inside things.
- ⭐ **Oases die, and that is the biome's whole clock.** Every power source has a
  half-life. Some patches are already cold and their ecologies are fossils. Others
  are dimming inside a human lifetime. And a colony that taps a wreck for power is
  **taking it from the thing living on it**. ⇒ Gameplay: a renewable-looking resource
  that is not renewable, that you can visibly extinguish, and that something else is
  already using. That tension is not available anywhere else in the game and it is
  the strongest argument in this sheet.
- **Therefore things migrate, and the crossings are the danger.** When a patch cools,
  whatever can walk must reach the next one across dead frozen ground with no food,
  no cover and no warmth. ⇒ The fauna is bimodal in a different way from the dune
  sea: **sedentary specialists** inside the oases and **lean, desperate,
  cold-tolerant crossers** between them. The open ground between wrecks is empty of
  resident life and full of transients, which is exactly backwards from every other
  biome and should feel that way.
- **The apex animal's size is set by the thickness of hull plate.** The richest food
  in the biome — cabling, polymer, organics sealed in a hold, the soft interior of a
  patch that has been growing undisturbed for centuries — is **behind armour**. So
  the top predator is not fast or clever, it is **big enough and strong enough to
  open a ship**, and it must be, because nothing smaller can get in. ⇒ Giants here
  are justified by a material property, not by mood. A wreck-breaker is the size it
  is because six millimetres of alloy says so.
- **Sensing: metal, heat and echo.** Live wrecks are electrically noisy, thermally
  bright and radio-loud. ⇒ Fauna homes on **EM and warmth** — which means a colony's
  own generators, comms and heaters read to the wildlife as *an unusually attractive
  wreck.* ⛔ Not the nightside's pure thermal sense: here the signal is
  electromagnetic and the animals are drawn *toward* machines, not merely toward warm
  things. Building a base in a wreck field means becoming the best oasis in it.

## 5. Always true

- **Exposed metal is bright, not brown.** Nothing rusts here.
- **Life is in circles around live wrecks, and nowhere else.** Sharp edges, dead
  ground between.
- **Each patch is chemically and chromatically distinct**, and its colour names its
  host hull.
- **There is liquid water only inside an oasis**, and it is metallic and needs
  treating.
- **Every wreck is a shelter**, and every shelter is occupied.
- **The power is finite and measurable.** How long a patch has left is a knowable
  number, and it is the most interesting number in the biome.

## 6. Never true 🔴 (hard bans — checkable)

- ⛔ **No rust, no brown corrosion, no oxidised patina on exposed metal** except
  within an ammonia apron. A rusty junkyard palette is the failure mode of this
  biome and must be banned by name.
- ⛔ **No continuous vegetation.** Flora may only be placed within a bounded radius
  of a powered wreck; a plant def on open wreck-field ground is a violation.
- ⛔ **No ecology on a cold wreck.** Dead wrecks are sterile. Checkable: patch life
  requires a power source in the same patch.
- ⛔ **No vents, geysers, hot springs, geothermal or volcanism.** 🔴 Every warm spot
  in this biome is **artificial**, and that is the entire premise. A natural heat
  source here destroys the biome.
- ⛔ **No mechanoids, no active machines, no functioning AI, no mechanoid intrusion
  content.** ⚠️ **This is the boundary with the Rust Cathedral** (`AB_MechanoidIntrusion`,
  substellar, hot, inhabited, at war). Wreck fields are **cold, nightward, and the
  tech is dead** — a trickle of waste heat, not an occupant. If a wreck fights back,
  it is Cathedral content and belongs at θ 0.
- ⛔ **No organism whose colouring is independent of its host hull.** Patch identity
  is the art rule.
- ⛔ **No sand burial and no overgrowth.** Wrecks sit on the surface, exposed,
  permanently; this is a fourth ruin-language and it must not borrow the other three.
- ⛔ **No lush flora.** 🔴 Under the owner's three-part lush rule this band is neither
  a dayside river line nor the nightside, so nothing here qualifies: an oasis is
  **fierce, small and hard-edged**, a few hundred metres of dense crystalline life
  around one hull, and it is never green and never soft.
- ⛔ **No instantly-nameable Earth organisms** (standing recognizability ban).
- ⛔ **A "wreck field" tile requires multiple wrecks.** Checkable: below a minimum
  density it is a landmark, not a biome tile, and must not carry the biome.

## 7. Uniquely available

- ⭐ **Pre-fall salvage in bulk and in place** — hull plate, structural alloy,
  components, and the occasional sealed compartment nobody has opened. On a scavenger
  world this is the reason the faction exists, and it is the most on-theme resource
  in the entire design.
- **Trickle power you can tap** — with the cost written into the biome: you are
  killing the patch, and it will not come back.
- **Alloy-specific bio-concentrates** — the patch organisms have already done the
  refining, so a titanium patch yields titanium and a hot patch yields something you
  should handle with tongs. ⇒ Prospecting is by **colour**, at a glance, which is a
  lovely and rare thing for a game to be able to offer.
- **Ready-made structures** — the only biome where the shelter is already built and
  the problem is who is living in it.
- ⚠️ **Not safety, not clean water, and not a renewable base.** Every wreck field
  settlement is on a clock.

## 8. Inhabited objects

The Junkers are canon here (5 seats), and this sheet says why they and nobody else.

- **Junker holds built into large hulls** — not beside them, *in* them, using the
  wreck's own power and shielding, which is the only way to live in this band.
- **Cut faces and stripped skeletons** — a picked-over wreck is a distinctive object
  in its own right: ribs, no plating, and a cold dead patch around it where an
  ecology used to be. ⇒ **Strip-mining a wreck is visible from the air for centuries.**
- **The old mining fields** (canon) — the other half of the Junker territory, and the
  same logic: abandoned industrial plant, still cold, still bright, still standing.
- **Cairns and route markers** across the dead ground between patches, because
  crossing is the dangerous part and everybody who crosses marks the way.
- ⛔ **No roads.** Nothing here is worth a road; the patches move (die) faster than a
  road amortises.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"A field of mirrors on slate, and every mirror that is still warm has a garden."**

Palette: **cold grey-blue** ground and sky · **bright bare metal** — silver, gunmetal,
white-hot specular highlights, the only strong speculars on the planet · and then
**one saturated hue per patch**, and never the same hue twice in a shot: verdigris
here, titanium white there, a bad yellow-green somewhere you should not go. ⛔ **No
rust orange anywhere.** The instinct to paint a junkyard brown is exactly the instinct
this sheet exists to forbid.

Light: **twilight ambient, plus artificial point sources that should not still be
working.** A wreck field's lighting is *practical*: a strip that still glows inside a
hull, a running light on a stub of wing, and the deep-red thermal glow of something
under a deck plate. The oases are lit from *inside their own wreck*, which makes each
one look like a lamp in a dark field. ⭐ The defining shot is **a lit hull at
half a kilometre with a ring of dark growth around its feet, and nothing else in
frame.**

Silhouette language: **big broken geometry.** Straight lines, cylinders, wing roots,
frames — machine shapes at building scale, tilted, snapped, half-collapsed. Organic
forms are **small, hard-edged and crystalline**, clustered at the base of the machine
shapes like barnacles on a pier leg. The scale contrast between a hundred-metre hull
and a knee-high organism is the composition.

## 10. 🔴 The honest verdict, for the owner

**It works — as a biome, not merely as a place — and here is the load-bearing test it
passes:** *could this be moved somewhere else on Ash'karr?* No. It needs the crash
lane (which requires the terminator to be the only destination and the nightside
atmosphere to be the only thing that delivers ships intact), the no-corrosion window
(which requires cold and dry), the ammonia electrolyte (which requires the nightside
cold trap upwind), and enough residual power to make a heat island (which requires
cold ambient — the same reactor on the dayside would be invisible). **Four conditions,
one strip.**

**Three ways it could fail, stated plainly:**

1. ⚠️ **The premise is the whole thing.** If the owner rules that no ancient power
   source is still running, there are no oases, no ecology, and no biome — just a
   good-looking landmark set. **Reject the sheet with the premise; do not build a
   dead version.**
2. ⚠️ **It is close to the Rust Cathedral and the Shipyards.** Three tech-substrate
   places is possibly one too many. The distinction I have drawn is sharp — hot /
   inhabited / at war versus cold / dead / merely leaking — but the owner may
   reasonably say the planet only needs one crashed-technology idea, in which case
   this is the one to cut, because the Cathedral is canon and this is not.
3. ⚠️ **It is the most "science-fiction" of the four sheets** and the least
   *planetary*. The other three are things a world does to itself. This one is a
   thing that was done to a world. That is either the most on-theme biome in a
   scavenger campaign or the one that reads as set dressing, and that judgement is
   the owner's, not mine.

**What I would not do:** water it down. A wreck field with no live power, or with
mechanoids in it, is either the Rust Cathedral or a rubble texture. It is worth
building as written or not at all.

## Roster consequences (the sheet is the admission test)

**Wanted:** crystalline metal-sequestering flora and sessile fauna, **one palette per
patch** · cavity-nesting and compartment-dwelling forms · lean cold-crossing
transients · EM- and heat-homing hunters · and one **hull-breaking giant**, sized by
plate thickness, as the biome's apex.
**Barred on sight:** anything rusty · anything living on open or unpowered ground ·
anything mechanical or mechanoid · anything that reads as the poison forest's plated
chemosynthetic life · anything lush · anything instantly nameable.
⚠️ **And nothing may be committed to this roster until the sheet is ruled.** If the
owner rejects the premise, this admission test is void along with it.
