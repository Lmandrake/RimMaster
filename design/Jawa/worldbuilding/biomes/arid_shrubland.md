# The Arid Shrubland — definition sheet

_Owner + BENCH, 2026-09-05, written in conversation over three passes. The last damp ground
before the stormwall, and the planet's power country. Thematic handle: **the hush** — and its
gesture: **everything leans toward the light.**_

🔑 **Read against `desert.md`.** Desert is the shade economy; this is where the shade economy
*ends* — the sun drops to 16% of overhead and the terrain flattens, so at the same stroke the
land loses its long sightlines and loses the need to hide. Concealment becomes universal and
free, and the whole ecology reorganizes around that fact.

## 1. What it is

Knee-high silver-green fuzz at eerily even spacing, running unbroken to every horizon, all of
it leaning the same way, all of it moving — because the wind never, ever stops. No rain falls;
the only water is fog carried in on the ground-wind from the stormwall, thinning with every
sunward metre. It is mild here — the first ground on the dayside that is not trying to kill
you — and that is the trap: the moment the environment stops being the danger, the danger
becomes each other, and everything you cannot see. Sightlines are gone. Cover is everywhere,
for everything, all the time.

Big things ignore the fuzz and ignore you. Small things live under the fuzz and are never
seen. An intelligent biped is precisely the wrong size for this place — too large to hide in
it, too small to be safe over it — and the biome treats you accordingly: with indifference
from above and venom from below. Not a welcoming place; arguably harsher than the desert for a
farmer, and the locals farm it anyway, because the wind is worth it.

## 2. Planetary position

**Terminator approach (core arc 70–95°) × the anomaly of FOG.** And the fog is not a band —
🔑 **each shrubland is the plume of the sea upwind of it.** The Hadley return flow picks up
clean evaporate crossing the Torn Seas, and the damp tongue reaches sunward from each coast,
pinching out where mountains comb the wind dry, surging through gaps as fog-corridors. The
current core already obeys: Damp, Grey Sea and Twilight Sea are the biggest surviving regions,
all on drained seabed and coast. **Plumes, not rings** — this is the anti-bullseye mechanism
and the mending instrument in one sentence (owner's ruling: maintain longitude-based
differences; never revert the band to a bullseye).

## 3. Driving forces

**The planet is one huge Hadley cell.** Air rises at the substellar point, races high toward
the terminators carrying scarce moisture, collapses and falls through the stormwall, and races
back along the ground toward the sun. So the surface wind here blows darkward → sunward,
perpetually, laden with traces of fog that lose condensability with every metre of travel.
The toxins of the two atmospheres' collision precipitated out at the terminator — **this
water is clean.** The flow is steady; mid-latitude cyclones driven by the extreme temperature
gradients can stall it or lash it to a gale, but a still day is nearly unknown.

**And the two resources arrive as anti-parallel winds.** Light streams in from the sunward
horizon at 9°; water drifts in on the ground-wind from the darkward horizon. Every organism
must choose its point on one axis: **more moisture darkward, more light sunward.** That
tension in space is the biome's engine.

## 4. How the biology adapted

### The two shadows, and the quincunx

Every shrub casts **two shadows in opposite directions**: a light-shadow stretching darkward
(6× its height at this sun), and a **fog-shadow stretching sunward** — the wake of dried air
behind a plant that has already combed the wind. A bush starves its darkward neighbour of
light and its sunward neighbour of water.

⇒ **The only stable arrangement is even, staggered spacing** — each plant offset from both of
its neighbours' wakes. A quincunx; an orchard nobody planted; creosote-country regularity,
not perfect but too much for comfort. There is not enough water for anything denser: bushes
spaced apart, short grasses struggling between. Thickets are physically impossible (a clump
dies of its own fog-shadow) — **the openness is not an absence of structure, it IS the
structure.** One exception refuses the law: venomvine (below).

**Sand is gone** — held down by roots and cemented with dead vegetation. The ground is
root-bound crust, and the first true soil on the dayside forms here: damp, mild and dim is
where rot finally works, and the things doing the digesting are the first cousins of the
mycotic biomes, humble and clean, before the wall's chemistry teaches them stranger things.

### The sweetline, and the gradient that reverses

Travel the moisture-light axis and the fuzz thickens steadily — then reverses. Sunward of the
optimum the flora is **water-starved** (sparse, waxy, armored); darkward it is
**light-starved** (fog-fat, silvery, dim); between them runs the **sweetline**, the contour
where the trade balances, the bushes stand densest, and the biome's only trees grow. You can
tell how far you are from the stormwall by the colour of the fuzz at your feet, and the
sweetline wanders with the seas and the mountains, never a circle.

### The two floors

The fuzz is a canopy at knee height, dividing the biome into two worlds that cannot see each
other:

- **Above the fuzz — the giants' floor.** To anything huge the vegetation is a low fuzz it
  cares nothing about: it consumes it in great mouthfuls and **grows huge to be safe** — the
  only sightlines in the biome belong to a grazer's raised head.
- **Below the fuzz — the runway floor.** To anything small the same vegetation is safety
  itself, never to be left: worn tunnel-runs under the canopy, a labyrinth invisible from
  above, navigated by smell and whisker. Freeze in place or run fast, depending; born, fed,
  bred and died without once standing in open light.

**The floors never meet — except violently.** Every predation strategy is an attack on the
interface, by the three routes only: **ambush where a runway is forced to surface** (the
fog-shadow barrens — ground too dry to grow cover — are this biome's deadly crossings);
**run the tunnels yourself** (long, thin, terrible things shaped like the corridors — the
runway's own diameter tells you what hunts it); or **come from the sky and dive**.
Prairie-style adaptations, alien key.

### 🔴 The size ladder (owner's ruling, ratified)

**Small, medium, then NOTHING large — until huge.** The large band is a void, populated by
exactly one thing: **the young of the huge**, walking the valley of death behind their
parents. Being a predator here is difficult by design; nothing resident occupies large.

- **Small** — the runway nations: fuzz-grazers, comb-lickers, thornhold nesters. They forbid
  rather than flee or fight: venom-armed, lashing from hidden places.
- **Medium** — the interface killers: snake-analogs in the tunnels, edge-pouncers at the
  barrens, bird-analogs above. Also, uncomfortably, *us*.
- **Large** — giants' children only, and 🔴 **approach is attack: the parent enrages if you
  even get near the young.** No warning is given.
- **Huge** — the giants (below).

### ⭐ The ripple — the wind is the smallfloor's camouflage

The flier hunts in 16% light over a closed canopy, so **it reads the canopy, not the prey**:
anything moving in the runways shakes the fuzz above it. The roof that hides you reports you.
And the steady wind is the counter — a constant breeze keeps the whole canopy shivering
uniformly, one great noise-floor that swallows the twitch of every small body under it.

- 🔴 **The Stall (rare).** A cyclone cancels the Hadley flow, the wind dies, the canopy goes
  still — and every step under the fuzz is a signal flare. Fliers rise, the runway floor
  freezes solid, giants alone keep grazing. Hours of held breath. Any movement is dangerous.
- **The Gale (more common).** The canopy whipped to white noise no eye can read — and the
  smallfloor erupts: dispersal, migration, courtship, everything too dangerous on an ordinary
  day, spent in one roaring window.

The desert's calendar was smoke; **the shrubland's calendar is the wind gauge** — and both
are set by storms the biome never sees.

### The giants

- **The fuzz is graze-adapted**: growth crown buried below the soil line, leaves expendable.
  The lawn and the mowers are one equilibrium — the giants maintain the very openness that
  makes them safe.
- **They are the biome's involuntary sentinels** — the only eyes above the fuzz. When the
  giants stop chewing and lift their heads, the whole plain reads it and the runways go quiet.
- 🔴 **They are indifferent to the intelligent races.** A giant will walk through your wall on
  its grazing line, ignore your rage, and not know what it did. The desert's whale wanted your
  roof; the shrubland's giant *has not noticed your species.*
- 🔴 **They despise fire.** They charge it and stamp it dead at all costs — and then stamp out
  its **source**.

### Venomvine — the fortress flora

Dense thickets that sometimes manage to colonize: nearly impossible to cut down, and a scratch
carries venom with serious results. **Small creatures pass through easily; larger ones simply
cannot.** Desert lineage — they resist fire greatly and burn only grudgingly.

- 🔑 **A mature thicket is a dungeon.** Sparser stands let a Jawa slowly thread them — no
  larger race can — but inside it is a dangerous, cave-like maze whose residents move through
  the walls to their advantage. Sneaking in and out is lucrative; fighting through is
  perilous, venomous, and debatably worthwhile.
- ⭐ **The scrap nests.** The bird-analogs are scavengers too: they line their nests in the
  vine with glittering scrap — wire, lens-glass, chips of hull — beautiful creations, and a
  treasure worth seeking for a Jawa. Snake-analogs feed on the birds and their eggs and are
  replete here: the treasure has its serpent, coiled in the one corridor you both fit.
  *(Candidate: the birds also steal from player bases.)*
- **The smother-craft.** Locals kill a thicket by throwing blankets over it — starving it of
  light — then burn it after it desiccates for years. The blanket-weave is a trade good; a
  blanketed thicket is a banked claim; and dead venomvine is the finest fuel in the region —
  wood that spent its life refusing to burn and, dry at last, burns long and hot.
- **Settlements grow their fortifications**: transplanted venomvine is the local curtain
  wall — which a Jawa can sometimes walk through.

### The sweetline trees

🔴 **Huge, ancient, powerful things** (owner's ruling — never small trees). Each grows only on
the optimum, so each is a living surveyor's mark, visible for a day's walk; roads follow them
and every one has a name. Giants rub against them, so the bark snags **shed giant-wool — a
rare hanging harvest** for whoever dares the traffic. *(Candidate, owner's suggestion: unique
animals that dwell near the trees and defend them from human-sized things.)*
⚠️ Tree art is our own problem to solve at our own scales — see `TREE_GRAPHICS_OWNERSHIP_1`;
no stack of tree mods gets to rescale these.

### Fire, folly, and the stamping of it

There is hardly ever lightning or violent weather beyond windstorms — so **a fire almost
always means someone lit it.** Smoke on the horizon is a person.

1. **The deep desert tribes' pyroculture is at its most terrible here** — stand upwind, light
   a moving wall of flame, harvest what is left behind. But this ecosystem is NOT friendly to
   fire: the fuzz burns grudgingly, crown safe underground, so the fire-wall yields no
   forage — **only the burned corpses of animals that could not flee. And of your enemies.**
   Here, pyroculture is pure weapon.
2. ⭐ **A firewall can pay anyway**: where it forces a venomvine thicket, it can reveal the
   scrap nests inside — treasure in the ashes.
3. 🔴 **And the giants come.** Arson summons the biome's titans onto the arsonist: they stamp
   the fire, then its source. Every raider knows the wall of flame is also a beacon.
4. ⭐ **The calling-pyre.** In desperation, defenders light the shrubs themselves and bring the
   wrath down upon ALL who remain — attacker, defender, farm and field. Very Pyrrhic; spoken
   of the way sailors speak of running aground on purpose.

## 5. Always true

- **There is always wind.** The Hadley ground-flow is the biome's pulse; a still hour is an
  event (the Stall), never a day.
- The only water is fog on that wind — clean, its toxins already dropped at the terminator —
  thinning sunward every metre. It does not rain; it does not sprinkle.
- Vegetation is continuous but SPACED: the quincunx, with struggling short grass between.
  Venomvine thickets are the sole exception.
- Sand is gone; the ground is root-bound, vegetation-cemented crust. First true soil; rot
  works here.
- Cover is universal and free — and sightlines do not exist below a giant's head height.
- The size ladder holds: small · medium · void · huge, with huge-young as the void's only
  residents.
- The wild is quiet. Danger announces itself by posture, never by voice; the noisiest things
  on the plain are the settlements.
- Mild, by this planet's standards: the environment is not the killer here. Everything else is.

## 6. Never true — 🔴 HARD BANS (linter-checkable)

1. 🔴 **No rain** (planet-wide, R-H1). Fog only.
2. 🔴 **No natural ignition.** No lightning, no dry storms, no fire weather. Any fire def or
   incident that starts without an agent is a violation: **fire implies folly.**
3. 🔴 **No day/night cycle, and no content depending on one** (as the desert; the clock here
   is the wind, not the sun).
4. 🔴 **No resident creature in the LARGE band.** Large is legal only as the juvenile stage of
   a huge species — a standalone large creature def in this biome is a violation.
5. 🔴 **No still-air baseline.** Weather defs for this biome carry wind; calm exists only as
   the Stall event. A windless ordinary day is a violation.
6. 🔴 **No dense flora except venomvine.** No thickets, no forests, no clustered vegetation
   defs — the quincunx law. The lush rule applies in full (terminator-adjacent: SOLITARY,
   never abundant).
7. 🔴 **No open sand terrain.** The ground is vegetated crust; a sand terrain def on core
   shrubland tiles is a violation.
8. 🔴 **No unprotected outdoor food crops.** Edible plants simply don't grow well here outside
   protection (owner's ruling) — farming happens under cover and growlight.
9. 🔴 **No flammable living flora.** The native plants resist fire greatly and burn only
   grudgingly; a quick-burning living plant def here is a violation. (Dead, desiccated
   venomvine burns hot — that is the exception and the industry.)
10. 🔴 **The recognizability rule applies**; the Star Wars icon carve-out still protects icons.

## 7. Uniquely available

- 🔑 **Wind power, effectively unlimited** — the steadiest, strongest resource on the planet.
  Fantastic for power; the turbines never stop.
- **Fog water, anywhere** — a moisture farm works wherever you stand, no cistern to find and
  hold. But the harvest is *meager*: this is the biome of thin, reliable margins.
- 🔴 **The moisture-farmer's trade, stated once (owner's ruling):** the shrubland offers
  nearly the same deal wherever you wish — steady wind power plus more moisture — but the
  16% sun means **the wind must also power the growlights**, cancelling part of the water
  utility. The desert offers less moisture and unpredictable wind, but its permanent strong
  sun does **double duty** — power *and* free crop light — anchored to cisterns and water
  points that must be found, protected and nurtured. Freedom and a meager living here;
  richness chained to a defended point there.
- ⭐ **Scrap nests** — salvage, eggs and venom-thorns behind a wall only a Jawa can thread.
- **Giant-wool**, snagged on sweetline-tree bark.
- **Dead venomvine** — premium fuel, years in the making; and the **smother-blanket** craft
  and trade that produces it.
- **Living venomvine** — transplantable fortification.
- **Reliable protein at the interface** — snake- and bird-analog hunting for those who learn
  the crossings.

## 8. Inhabited objects

- **Moisture farmsteads** — vaporator rows set comb-wise to the wind. 🔴 **Every farm wears a
  V-shaped blight**: vaporators rob the moisture and desertify downwind, so each farm sits in
  a pale barrens wake of its own making — automatic firebreak, automatic sightline moat,
  visible from the air, and an ecological grievance (the farm drinks the runways dry; the
  wild raids the vaporators like an oasis).
- **Hedge-forts** — settlements walled in living venomvine, food grown inside under light.
- **The sweetline tree-roads** — routes running tree to named tree along the optimum.
- **The wind gauge on every wall** — the most-watched instrument in the biome: 🔴 **raids ride
  the gales**, when power surges and shorts from overload, when hearing fails, when the
  canopy is white noise and nothing can be read.
- **Deep Desert Tribe raiding grounds** — no holdings here; they come with the wind and the
  fire-wall, and leave with the burned.
- **Blanketed thickets** — banked venomvine claims, inherited; "my grandmother darkened this
  stand."
- **Calling-pyre scars** — the rare burned farmstead that chose the Pyrrhic lever.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"Everything leans toward the light, and the wind agrees."**

- **Gesture:** the ground-wind blows darkward-to-sunward — the same direction the
  light-hunger pulls — so every bush, grass tuft and great tree leans sunward, permanently,
  pushed and yearning in the same direction. One biome-wide lean.
- **Motion is the signature.** The desert was stillness-then-sprint; the shrubland never
  stops moving — canopy ripple horizon to horizon, forever. The Stall reads as horror
  precisely because it is the one image of this biome standing still.
- **Palette:** silver-green fuzz banding to grey fog-country darkward and waxy gold sunward;
  pale barrens wakes behind farms and shrubs; venomvine as near-black islands; the sweetline
  trees as lone ancient verticals; giants as walking hills with lit flanks.
- **Light:** dimmer and cooler than the desert's golden hour — the last ten minutes of a
  sunset that never ends, fog-softened toward the dark side.
- **Sound:** the eternal hiss of wind through a million bushes, and under it, nothing. The
  wild has no voice, only posture. Bells, turbines and voices — the noise is always yours.
