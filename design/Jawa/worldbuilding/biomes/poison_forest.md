# The Poison Forest — biome definition sheet

_First sheet written to the grammar (`README_BIOME_GRAMMAR.md`). Owner, 2026-09-05:
"I know the poison forest needs help. Its trees looked a wee bit too ordinary."_

**The diagnosis:** it had no driving force. "Poison" was a label, not a mechanism —
so its trees defaulted to tree-shaped, and a tree with no reason to be strange
comes out looking like an oak with a bad palette.

## 1. What it is

A low, wet-black forest standing over ground that breathes. Nothing here is green.
The trunks are pale and slick and weep a grey metallic sweat that hardens on the
bark in scabs and crystal fans, so a mature trunk looks part-tree, part-slag. There
is no canopy in the ordinary sense — instead each trunk carries a crown of fine
chemical fronds held out like filters, and they are always moving slightly, because
the ground exhales. The air shimmers. Everything is faintly, chemically luminous:
not the warm glow of fungus, but the sick, even light of a struck match held too
long.

## 2. Planetary position

**Deep nightside to inner nightside (θ ≈ 130-165°), on a vent field.**
Energy regime: eternal dark — there is no photosynthetic budget at all.
Anomaly: **chemistry.** A field of sulfide and heavy-metal vents keeps the ground
warm and chemically active where everything around it is frozen.

## 3. Driving forces

**The ground exhales sulfides and dissolved heavy metals, and there is no light.**
That single fact does all the work: an energy source that is chemical rather than
solar, and a toxin load that has to go somewhere.

## 4. How the biology adapted

The trees **chemosynthesize** — they metabolise vent chemistry, drawing metal-rich
fluid up from the ground. They cannot excrete it into soil they depend on, so they
**excrete it outward, into their own bark**. The poison is not a weapon. It is
**metabolic waste**, and the tree is slowly plating itself in its own tailings.

Consequences that follow without further invention:
- **No leaves.** Leaves are solar collectors and there is no sun. The crowns are
  *filter fronds* — high surface area for absorbing vent gas, held into the updraft.
- **Trunks thicken with age as ore, not as wood** — old growth is genuinely hard to
  cut and worth cutting *for the metal*.
- **Animals here are chemically armoured or they are dead.** Anything living is
  either sealed (plated, waxed, shelled) or it processes the load itself and is
  therefore poisonous to eat.
- **Nothing is fast.** A chemosynthetic energy budget is thin; life here is slow,
  patient, and heavy.

## 5. Always true

- The ground is warm. Snow does not lie here even though the nightside is frozen.
- Everything luminesces faintly, and the light is cold-toned and even, never warm.
- Every plant surface is either wet or crystalline — nothing here is dry and matte.
- Vent gas moves the air constantly; there is no stillness.
- Ore is present *in the organisms themselves*, not only in the rock.

## 6. Never true 🔴 (hard bans — checkable)

- ⛔ **No green.** No `foliage`/leaf-green in any plant art here. Nothing photosynthesizes.
- ⛔ **No leaves, needles or broadleaf canopies** — no plant whose art reads as a
  conventional tree crown.
- ⛔ **No sunlight cues** — no dappled light, no cast shadows implying a sun, no
  heliotropic forms.
- ⛔ **No lush flora** (standing ban: lush belongs only in the water-high biomes).
- ⛔ **No instantly-nameable Earth organisms** (standing recognizability ban).
- ⛔ **No grazing herbivore body plans** — there is no grass and no fodder here.

## 7. Uniquely available

- **Bio-accumulated metal**: harvesting old trunks yields refined-adjacent metal no
  ore field gives, at the cost of toxin exposure. This is the biome's reason to exist
  for a scavenger clan.
- **Vent heat**: free geothermal on the nightside, where heat is otherwise the
  binding constraint on settlement.
- **Toxin stock** for weapons, preservation and medicine.

## 8. Inhabited objects

Whoever came before came for the metal and the heat, not the land. Expect
**wellhead scaffolds and capped vents**, **condenser stacks** furred with crystal,
**tap-lines** run from tree to tank, and **sealed shelters** — everything here is
built to keep the air *out*. Ruins are corroded rather than sand-blasted, which
makes them read differently from every dayside wreck on the planet. Abandoned
suits and filters are the characteristic small find.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"A forest that is slowly turning into a smelter."**
Palette: wet black and slate ground · bone-pale trunks streaked with grey metal
tears · crystal in bruised blues and sulfur yellows · the only warmth is the dull
red of an open vent. Light is **ambient and sourceless** — cold luminescence with no
sun direction, so nothing casts a proper shadow. Silhouette language: **vertical,
ragged, encrusted** — trunks that widen downward into scab and slag, crowns that are
feathery rather than massed. Against the ochre emptiness of the dayside, this biome
should read as the planet's exact opposite: wet, dark, chemical, and busy.

## Roster consequences (the sheet is the admission test)

**Wanted:** chemosynthetic trunk-forms, filter-frond plants, crystal/mineral growths,
plated or shelled slow animals, sealed scavengers.
**Barred on sight:** anything green, any conventional tree, any grazer, any
instantly-nameable animal, any lush water-loving plant.
Alpha Biomes' chemical and fungal flora are the natural donor pool here — but each
candidate must pass §6 before it is admitted.
