# The Deep Desert — definition sheet

_Owner + BENCH, 2026-09-05, in conversation. Canon anchor: `hydrology_and_fire_ecology.md`
R-H6d, which names the deep desert **the dayside terminus** and the exact conjugate of the
forsaken crags — there the danger is presence, here it is **absence**; there the fear is
that you are not alone, here it is that **you will run out**._

## 0. Where it is

The far ring of dayside ground, past the Pyrelands and past the tar pits those Pyrelands
manufacture as their own margin (R-H9). Measured as degrees of arc from the nearest water:

| region | from water | tiles | arc | temp | reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cracklands** | 39.6° | 138 | 67° | 31 °C | the terminus — furthest dayside ground from water on the planet |
| **Thornbelt** | 38.7° | 263 | 69° | 30 °C | |
| **Dry Marches** | 28.9° | 345 | 68° | 29 °C | |
| **Long Sand** | 26.1° | 532 | 67° | 32 °C | |
| **Glare** | 25.9° | 879 | 50° | 43 °C | the deep — hottest of the far ring |
| **Kiln** | 19.4° | 878 | 51° | 43 °C | the margin, where the Pyrelands let go |

**≈3,035 tiles.** 🔑 **Glare and Kiln are not twins.** They match on arc and temperature but
Glare is **six degrees deeper into the absence**, and the biomes agree: Glare runs 628
`ExtremeDesert` to 239 `Desert`, Kiln an even 418/412. **Kiln is the transition; Glare is
the deep; Cracklands is the end.**

⛔ **The Anvil is NOT this biome.** It is 6.5° from water, which is why it carries grassland
and jungle — it is the fire ecology at maximum energy, not the terminus.

## 1. What it is

The ground past the last thing that burns. Everywhere else on the dayside, freakish growth
makes fuel and the fuel makes fire; out here there is not enough water to grow anything to
burn, so **the fire cycle simply stops**. No ash, no cinder, no black rain, no regrowth.
Where the Pyrelands are loud and fast and renew themselves violently, this is silent, slow,
and **never renews at all**. What is destroyed here stays destroyed, and what died here is
still lying where it fell.

It looks empty. It is not empty — most of it is **asleep**, and it is waiting for water.

## 2. Planetary position

**Deep-to-mid dayside (arc 50–69°) × the anomaly of DISTANCE.** Its defining feature is not
heat and not altitude — it is being 19–40° of arc from any water on a planet where every
drop is stolen from a mountain (R-H1). The anomaly is *absence itself*, which is why it is
the terminus rather than another flavour of desert.

## 3. Driving forces

**Energy is free and water is money.** Every structure, behaviour and body plan here is a
water-accounting decision. And because the sun never moves, **shade is not weather** — it is
permanent geography, and it shelters against **merciless radiation**, not merely heat. An
animal caught in the open is being sterilised, not just cooked.

## 4. How the biology adapted

### The sand is a medium, not a surface
Things **swim** beneath it. But surfacing spends water, so a predator that feels your
footfalls performs an **appraisal — how much water is that worth?** Attack is an economic
decision, never a reflex.

- 🔑 **A droid is invisible to the food web.** It carries no water, so nothing under the
  sand will spend a strike on it. **A Jawa clan that fields droids can cross ground nothing
  alive can cross.** This is the campaign-defining consequence of the whole biome.
- 🔴 **Predators do not chase, and they often do not move even once seen.** The stillness is
  not lethargy from hunger — it is **disinterest in spending water on a pursuit that might
  not pay.** A creature that watches you walk past is doing arithmetic, and this reads as far
  more alien than aggression.

### Water is taken from bodies, because there is nowhere else
Predation is hydrological. Nothing eats for calories out here; calories are cheap and useless
when you cannot sweat. **The prize is a full animal**, and a dried one is worthless.

- 🔴 **Nothing rots. Bodies desiccate almost instantly.** There is no decay because decay
  needs water. A kill must be drunk where it falls; nobody caches.

### Water accounting written into the body
- **The head is a still.** Breathing is the largest loss, so faces are mostly condensing
  organ — nasal labyrinths reclaiming exhaled vapour. The face reads wrong because it is not
  a face, it is plumbing.
- 🔑 **Mass IS the water balance sheet.** A large animal here is large because it is
  *holding* water, not because it eats well. (Binds to `creature_normalization_doctrine.md`:
  bodySize as mass means size carries a specific, readable meaning in this biome that it does
  not carry elsewhere.)
- **Anhydrobiosis.** Some things dry to dust and wait — not dormant, *desiccated*, and they
  rehydrate. The sand is therefore full of sleeping life, **a sandstorm is an aerosol of
  sleeping organisms**, and when a rare flood reaches this far the ground blooms within hours
  and is dead again within days.

### The ground lies to you
Predators hunt by vibration and appraise by water, so **both sides evolved to falsify the
signal**: prey that **drums dry** and goes still, and predators with **lures that drum
juicy** — an angler's decoy that sounds like a fat animal. This is a hunting ground where the
ground itself is dishonest.

### Flight is a solved problem
Fly, drink somewhere else, return. Fliers nest here **because nothing can reach the nest** —
ground predators cannot afford the crossing, making the deep desert the safest nursery on the
planet if you have wings. Their guano, dropped prey and dead chicks are the biome's **only
meaningful organic import** — the mirror of the Fall Line, where carbon falls from orbit
instead of flying in.

### Eggs are canteens — and some are traps
A large egg is portable water in a rigid shell, which is exactly why a Jawa prizes one. It is
not food, it is **drink you can carry**.

🔴 **Some eggs are birth traps.** Approached, they hatch violently, and the newborn tries to
drink the intruder as fast as it can with **sharp beaks and needle claws**. The clutch is
bait; the mother invested water in a weapon, not only in offspring.

## 4b. The Silverbole — the tree that grows to cast shade

_(Working name; `Silverpan` and `Silverbole` both fit. Owner asked that **silver** be in the
name, for how reflective they are.)_

The one plant that matters, and an **inverted plant**: it is not photosynthetic, it has **no
leaves**, and its product is **shadow**.

- It accretes **cell by cell from the tiniest hint of moisture**, over spans that make its
  growth effectively geological. A stand of them is older than any ruin.
- It stands as **bone-white shields and flat-topped truncates rising out of the sand**,
  brilliantly reflective — which is both why it survives the radiation and why it is visible
  from an impossible distance.
- It grows **among the true bones of vast creatures that also died out here long ago**, and
  it is **nursing the very source of water that might sustain it** — the seep, the corpse,
  the buried moisture. The bones and the tree are one system, not scenery beside each other.
- Its shade is **rented**: tenants pay in waste and water, so a silverbole is a small
  ecosystem standing alone in open ground.

🔑 **Silverbole wood is a material with a unique stat signature:** near-total immunity to
**heat and flame damage** — one of the only such materials in the game — and correspondingly
**helpless against sharp and blunt.** It is an ingredient in many rare recipes. That trade-off
is the point: armour and structure that laughs at fire and folds to a hammer.

## 5. Always true

- Water is currency; energy is worthless.
- **Shade is rare** and always significant. Its sources are exactly three: **silverboles**,
  **fallen wreckage**, and **caverns / permanently shaded canyons**. Nothing else.
- Predators appraise before they act, and usually decline.
- Bodies desiccate; nothing rots; nothing decays.
- The fire cycle does not reach here.
- Most of what lives here is asleep at any given moment.

## 6. Never true — 🔴 HARD BANS (linter-checkable)

1. 🔴 **No fire ecology.** No ash, char, cinder, ember, burn-scar or fire-regrowth content.
   The Pyrelands' whole vocabulary is banned here.
2. 🔴 **No rot or decay.** Corpses desiccate instantly. Any content assuming putrefaction,
   maggots, or wet decomposition is a violation.
3. 🔴 **Creatures must NOT look mineralized.** Pale and dusty — a **powder glaze, never shiny
   glass**, never crystal, never a salt crust that reads as gemstone or armour plate.
4. 🔴 **No fast growth.** The planet's freakish-growth fact does NOT apply here — the same
   deliberate exception the terminator gets (R-H2b). A global growth multiplier must not
   flatten it.
5. 🔴 **No green in the open**, and no standing surface water except brine seeps.
6. 🔴 **No abundant shade or structure.** Shade is rare by definition; a generator that
   scatters cover across these tiles has broken the biome.
7. 🔴 **No large surface herds.** The water budget cannot carry them.
8. 🔴 **The recognizability rule applies** (`creature_recognizability_rule.md`). The Star
   Wars icon carve-out still protects icons.
9. 🔴 **No lush**, under all three parts of the lush rule.

## 7. Uniquely available

- **Silverbole wood** — heat/flame-immune, sharp/blunt-vulnerable. Nothing else like it.
- **Eggs as portable water**, at the risk that a clutch is a trap.
- **Brine and mineral salts** from the cavern seeps — a chemistry input available nowhere
  else.
- 🔑 **Preservation.** Nothing decays, so **everything that ever died out here is still
  here, intact and mummified** — beasts, machines, people. The tar pits preserve wet; the
  deep desert preserves **dry**. For a scavenger clan this is the richest ground on the
  planet, and it is earned by the same physics as everything else.
- **Ground a droid can cross that a person cannot.**
- **Yardang stone** — wind-polished, grain-aligned.

## 8. Inhabited objects

- **Wreckage-shelters.** 🔑 Where debris falls it makes shade, and shade makes a **small
  living biome right beside it**. These are the treasured sites of the deep desert, and they
  are the reason anything settles here at all. (Related to but distinct from the Fall Line:
  there the fall is constant and the whole region is defined by it; here a wreck is a rare,
  precious island.)
- **Caverns and permanently shaded canyons — the real ecosystems.** Tiny upwellings of
  mineral brine allow scant, twisted growth and a genuine food web. **Here calories and
  self-defence matter again**, and competition is vicious *because* the territory is metres
  wide: plants and animals alike **strike out of the water like traps** to kill whatever
  threatens their patch. ⭐ **Serious authoring effort belongs here** — reference the
  Mandalorian cave-beast: a huge shaggy thing deep in a cavern, with massive eggs a Jawa
  would cross a desert for.
- **Deep Desert Tribe holdings** — canyons, caves, isolated ridges, never near water, and
  **no roads** (canon, `ASHKARR_WORLD_DEFINITION.md` §7).
- **Silverbole stands** among giant bone-fields.
- **Mummified fields** — intact dead things, at any age, in any state of technology.

### 🔴 Fire is the tribes' weapon, and the scarcity loop is the point

Nothing here knows fire or would ever want *more heat*, so fire causes **rout, not damage** —
it drives off things nothing else will move, including very large beasts. But **there is no
fuel here**, so every scrap is imported and a tribe's fire is its treasure. **The weapon that
wins is the one you can barely afford to use.** That is why fire-keeping is sacred to the
Deep Desert Tribes, and it sits naturally beside their existing **Sun-Debt**
(`FACTION_SPEC.md` §4).

### Sandstorms

Driven by the atmospheric heat engine. They **break walls, tear roofs off, and carry pawns
away permanently.** They also **seed** (the aerosol of dried life) and **redistribute the
real estate** — burying one silverbole's tenants, uncovering new rock, a cavern mouth, a
ruin, or something older.

## 9. Artistic theme

**"Everything that ever died here is still here, and most of it is only asleep."**

- **Palette:** bleached bone, pale dust, wind-glazed dark stone, salt-white. No green
  anywhere in the open, and — the distinguishing absence — **no black char**, because nothing
  has ever burned here. The only strong verticals are **bone-white silverboles** and the
  actual bones of vast animals.
- **Surface quality:** everything alive is **pale and powdery**, a dusty bloom rather than a
  glaze. ⛔ Nothing shiny, nothing crystalline, nothing that reads as mineral.
- **Silhouette language:** 🔑 **the map has a GRAIN.** Yardangs — long, smooth, wind-carved
  ridges — all aligned to the one superrotating wind, so every shape points the same way and
  a player can navigate by the landscape's direction. **This belongs in the map-modification
  routine**, not just in art.
- **Light:** flat, vertical, merciless, and unchanging. ⭐ Because the sun is **fixed**, real
  rendered shadows are correct here in a way they are nowhere else — the shade under a
  silverbole or a wreck is a hard-edged permanent shape, and the player can *see* why that
  shape is worth owning.
- **Motion:** almost none. Heat shimmer, blowing dust, and a predator that watches you go by
  without bothering to rise.
