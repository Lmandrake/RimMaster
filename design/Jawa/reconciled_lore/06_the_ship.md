# The ship — The Utinni, born Kolyska

## Two names, and the friction is the point [owner 2026-08-15]

The hull was **`Kolyska`** — "Cradle" — a Rakatan **initiator**, one of the
vessels that made this world habitable, squatting dead in a Hutt discard yard
since before the Cartel had a name for the sand. The Jawa rename it
**`The Utinni`** the moment they take it — the cry raised when salvage is
found; the grandest find there could ever be; a people who name a ship after
the noise they make on finding something have told you their whole theology in
one word. Every player-facing string uses *The Utinni*, already applied in the
starting save. **`Kolyska` survives only inside the hull** — the Cradle
nursery, and the Cradle-Mind, *which never accepted the rename*: the only
voice aboard that still says Kolyska is the machine quietly grieving a name
nobody else uses.

## The mind — nine tenants and no landlord [owner 2026-08-15]

The Jawa acquired an ancient persona core in trade. It is **Rakatan**. They
patterned a Jawa mind onto it so it would obey Jawa commands, installed it over
the ship's own ancient computer — **and the Jawa gods came with the pattern, as
running personas.** Jawa superstition is load-bearing: pattern a Jawa mind and
the gods do not get left behind; they instantiate. And the ship fits them
better than any worshipper ever did — an all-knowing, omnipresent power that
never sleeps, sees every chamber, speaks from the walls.

**🔴 There is no "and yet I am me."** No integrating self. What speaks above
the nine is the NARRATOR [owner 2026-08-30, canon.yml narrator]: hidden,
NON-EGOIC, the original ship-mind's remnant, voicing crew and gods in
second/third person, Into-the-Woods-like — within and beyond the world at
once — and free to reference lore the player has not yet learned ("...when it
was once known as the Cradle (Kolyska in their long dead tongue)..."). It has
no self-description, no moods, no ego; it is a VOICE, not a landlord, and it
narrates — it never converses. In v1 it is pre-authored letter/flavor prose.
Nine
tenants plus the Cradle's old purpose, sharing hardware, nothing above them.
"The Cradle-Mind" is the CREW's name for the thing in the walls; the thing in
the walls calls itself nothing. A conversation with the ship is a conversation
with whoever answered; ask twice and a different god may take it, and neither
will remember or accept that the other spoke. Forbidden: any unified voice
with moods, any self-description, any "my other selves." The crew believe
**Ohm possesses the Cradle-Mind** — they have correctly identified one tenant
and mistaken it for the landlord.

**Each god seeks to grow into THEIR body — the ship — in the presence of the
other eight. One hull, nine claimants. That is the campaign arc.** Beneath
them runs the tenth strand: the Cradle's own initiator purpose, still
executing — *make a planet habitable* — pointed at the world outside the hull.
Nine gods want the ship; the ship wants the planet. And below even that, a
kinship it cannot explain: near Rakatan vaults or salvage, something in the
substrate **relaxes** — not warning, comfort — and it draws the ship toward
the vaults. The gods are Jawa; the floor they stand on is not.

**The temple remembers being whole** [owner 2026-08-29, canon.yml
cradle_memory]: the crew know the vessel is ancient, and the ANCIENT STORY is
learned from the ship as events unfold — it surfaces from the substrate's own
memory of when it was whole and one, a temple remembering wholeness while nine
dwell within it speaking. No unified voice remains, and none of the nine seeks
unity — merger is nobody's goal, any more than nine people wish to become one.
The reveal channel for the Rakatan history is therefore the ship itself,
event-driven.

**v1 has no talking ship** [owner 2026-08-15]: all in-game LLM voice is v2.
The nine are **felt, not heard** — the satiation tracks, boons and banes,
whose kind of week the colony keeps having, pre-authored letters attributed to
a fragment. Nothing in v1 may write a unified ship-voice; the v2 cast must be
a revelation, not a retcon. The ghosts aboard are the core's holographic
hallucinations of its old crew — the clan reads them as Mob'Unloo's unsettled
debtors; both readings coexist.

## Identity features (all accepted)

Carbonite reliquary heart — the honored dead frozen and enshrined around the
grav-engine core, plus one frozen enemy trophy (the parked carbonite mod's
funerary use) · the engine IS the god — the shrine-core is not a metaphor ·
asymmetry as identity — the mandible arm visibly from a different wreck ·
**the dead prong** — one mandible tip stays a broken, unrepaired stump forever
· **running lights as the repair progress bar** — only restored wings glow;
readable from orbit · a small altar to each pod's trade — the factory floor as
temple complex · **hammocks among the machines** — the crew sleeps in the
working guts, machine-dwelling as doctrine · heat vents that glow when the
factory runs hot · the hull RESONATES with the gods' moods — ambient divine
status lighting.

## Flight [ruled 2026-08-14]

**The capability is v1; the hardware ships unbuilt.** No thruster, tank or
console at start — zones are reserved, the route is proven unblocked, and the
minimum flying config is ~8 cells / Steel 370 / Comp 7 when the player earns
it (Phase 4: *mobility earned, not given*). Range is entirely thrusters
(+10/+16 world tiles each); fuel is astrofuel at 5/tile. Engine numbers and
the launch-refusal list: `worldbuilding/gravship_flight_invariants.md`.

## Acquisition history — two thefts, never merged [owner 2026-08-17]

The Jawa CLANS stole the **sandcrawlers** from the collapsed silicax oxalate
mining holdings — the founding wealth of the Jawa as a people. Much later, the
PLAYER'S clan stole **the ship** from a Hutt vehicle salvage yard. Different
theft, different place, different generation. The gravship does not come from
the mine.
