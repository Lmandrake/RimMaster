# The Nine, Reviewed — BENCH critical assessment of the pantheon + matrix

_BENCH sitting with the owner, 2026-08-30. Subject: the nine gods' canon of record
and the nine matrix pages, both in `design/Jawa/divine_satiation_engine.md`
(matrix shipped nine-of-nine 2026-08-30). Register: **findings + options, not
canon** — nothing here is decided until the owner rules on a finding by name.
Findings are NAMED (F1–F19 with slugs) so rulings can land piecewise
("do SIGNATURE and POTLATCH, park MOOD")._

**Verdict:** the design is already unusually strong. The three-sign structure
(please / starve / perilous), the four pressure-clocks, and the reign format
(deeds/boons/demands/taboos/curses) are genuinely novel — no shipped RimWorld
religion mod has "the god you must court is the exposure that kills you." The
weaknesses are not in the theology; they are in **legibility** (will the player
*know* a god did it?), **mechanical invisibility of canonical feuds** (several
great rivalries exist only in prose), and **consequences that close doors
instead of opening them**. The three biggest leverage points: **SIGNATURE**
(every divine act attributable at a glance), **EXIT-VERBS** (every curse names
its own price), and **GEOGRAPHY** (the tidally-locked planet *is* the pantheon,
and nobody has said so yet).

---

## I. Philosophical — do these gods fit this species?

### F1 — FOLK-DEPTH: the gods live in the engine but not yet in the hands
A religion is visible in habit before it is visible in crisis. Ishko has folk
practice (never-nude, mate in darkness, the grave as hiding); Zizzik has one
(name unspoken near the engine). But Oomo, Mob'Unloo, Rekko, Ta'Baa have
*mechanics* without *gestures* — nothing a Jawa does eight times a day that
costs nothing and means everything. Real scavenger-faith would be thick with
apotropaic habit.
- **Option A — a folk-practice line per god in canon** (pure lore, zero
  mechanics): touch the doorframe before stepping out (Ishko); spit one drop on
  a new machine (Oomo greeting Ohm); never hand an object directly — set it
  down and let the other take it (Mob'Unloo: every transfer is a transaction);
  keep one broken thing unrepaired in every room (a decoy for Zizzik). Cost: an
  afternoon of writing. Buys: the Narrator can *reference* these, which makes
  his letters feel like they come from inside a culture.
- **Option B — make three of them micro-mechanics** (idle-job animations, tiny
  mood ticks). Trade-off: real presence on screen, but each is mod surface area
  and none carries strategy. Do A now; promote to B only the ones the
  Narrator's letters make players ask about.

### F2 — REALITY-LEAK: gods that are demonstrably real stop being a religion
The alters genuinely run the ship; the lights genuinely announce the front;
judgement genuinely lands. Aboard the Kolyska, faith is *fact* — wonderful ("a
cult with a god who answers back") but it risks flattening into an instrument
panel, the exact thing the Oracle re-scope pulled away from. The fix is to
control **where the certainty ends**: provably real *aboard the ship*; away
from the hull, the clan carries only belief.
- **Option A — attenuation by distance:** matrix effects run full-strength on
  the ship's map; half-voiced for caravans and outposts (Narrator letters only,
  no boons/curses). Away-teams are *outside the gods' reach* — theologically
  correct (the gods live in the hull) and mechanically interesting: sinning on
  a raid map is safer, which is exactly how scavengers would rationalize
  raiding. Trade-off: one more rule to learn; but only one, and it makes the
  ship *holy ground* by contrast. Also gives Ta'Baa's caravans and Ishko's
  raid-and-return a diegetic texture: you leave the gods' sight to do the
  unsighted work.
- **Option B — no attenuation; gods planet-wide.** Simpler; sacrifices the
  sacred/profane geography and makes the ship less special.
- Recommendation: A.

### F3 — SPECIES-FIT: excellent, with one seam to acknowledge rather than sand off
Oomo's "dominance is safety, hold slaves" sits oddly against a species whose
survival strategy is being beneath notice — Ishko and Ta'Baa both say extra
bodies are un-hideable weight. Canon already stages this as a feud, which is
right. One addition: give the seam a *name* the crew uses — the pantheon's
argument about slaves is the Jawa arguing about what kind of small they are
(small-and-hidden vs. small-but-growing). One sentence in §2.0d locks it as
intended tension rather than reading as inconsistency.

### F4 — NINE is over the head-budget — fine IF they arrive one at a time
RimWorld players hold ~5 systems. The front system narrows *active* rules to
one god — good — but the ambient §8b vector runs all nine from minute one. The
onboarding answer is the religion's own logic: **you meet a god the first time
you matter to him.**
- **Option A — discovery onboarding:** all nine meters start VEILED. The first
  act that moves a god past a threshold triggers his *first manifestation*
  (Narrator letter with his signature; meter unveils). The pantheon is a puzzle
  the colony solves by living. The cheapest source of shock→curiosity in the
  whole design — scrap a repairable turret on day 2 and get *"Something in the
  scrap-heap grieves. Rekko of the Second Hand has taken notice of you."*
- **Option B — all nine visible from the start with a codex.** Safe, wiki-like,
  wastes the mystery.
- Recommendation: A, strongly. Constraint: first manifestations must precede
  first curses — a god introduces himself before he bills you.

## II. Intellectual structure — pairings and triads

### F5 — THE 3×3: the pantheon has a latent triadic architecture nobody has named
Canon has six dualisms and one triad (the fall-triad). But the nine are:

| Triad | Members | The question they three-way answer |
|---|---|---|
| **The Body** | Oomo · Ohm · Rekko | *What shall the clan's hands be?* — eggs, droids, or the mended second-hand thing |
| **The Path** | Ishko · Ta'Baa · Mob'Unloo | *How do we survive the stranger?* — hide from him, leave him behind, or price him |
| **The Fall** | Zizzik · Sh'kaar · Ozzik | *Why did we fall?* — the accident, the inevitability, or our own pride |

The Body triad is literally the canon chamber-war (eggs vs. droids, plus
Rekko's hand). The Path triad is the three postures toward the outside. The
Fall triad is already canon. Six good gods in two triads of virtue; the three
you cannot safely befriend in the third. Costs *nothing* (a lore frame); buys
onboarding structure (introduce by triad), UI legibility (three rows of three
in the hologram room), ritual structure, and a repeatable "not this, nor this,
but that" template: within any triad, any situation is a three-cornered
argument.
- **Caution:** the triads must not flatten the sign-structure (the 6/2/1
  asymmetry of good/starve/complex is load-bearing) and must stay descriptive —
  the moment a mechanic *requires* triad symmetry, kill it. Present it as how
  the *crew* diagram their gods (scratched on a bulkhead), not as engine
  architecture.

### F6 — DILEMMA EVENTS: make the trinary playable, not just readable
RimWorld already supports choice-letters. Build **divine dilemma events** —
situations framed as three gods arguing, each dialog option carrying a god's
name, his blessing, and his rival's grievance. Worked example, Path triad: *a
wounded outlander crawls to the perimeter.* **Ishko:** turn him away unseen (he
can tell no one what he never saw) — Ishko pleased, Oomo slighted. **Ta'Baa:**
strike camp early; the problem is a place, leave the place — Ta'Baa pleased,
Mob'Unloo mutters at value abandoned. **Mob'Unloo:** save him and *bill* him —
ransom to his faction — Mob'Unloo delighted, Ishko nervous. Every option is
playable, every option is a theology; the player is never punished for
choosing — only *charged differently*.
- **Trade-off:** authored events are content cost and must not fire so often
  they become a quiz. Ten to fifteen excellent ones, rare, beats fifty
  mediocre.

### F7 — INVISIBLE FEUDS: several canonical rivalries never touch the machine
Ohm⇄Zizzik is live (see-saw). Ishko⇄Sh'kaar is live (light). Ozzik→evils is
live (amplifier). But **Oomo⇄Mob'Unloo** (waters vs. ledger, the lovers'
quarrel) is mechanically *nothing* — both gods gain from the same acts, so
their canonical distrust never appears in play. **Rekko⇄Ozzik**, named "the
sharpest internal war in the pantheon," is realized only as one neutrality rule
(restore ≠ transcend).
- **Option A — contested credit:** when one act pleases two rival gods (a
  marriage: Oomo + Mob'Unloo), occasionally the god with the higher mood
  *claims* it — full credit to him, a sliver of grievance to the other, and a
  flavored micro-event (the lovers bicker over the match's *terms*: the two
  gods quarreling through them, exactly as canon says). Cheap, ambient, makes
  rivalry felt.
- **Option B — Rekko⇄Ozzik as a standing dial:** track restore-vs-build-new as
  a ratio; the ship's own aesthetic drifts with it (Narrator describes the
  hull's character: patched-and-scarred vs. gleaming-and-new), and whichever of
  the two is losing grows louder in council. The sharpest war becomes *visible
  in the ship's body* — right, since the war is about the Body — and it feeds
  the endgame (which program wins the hull).
- Both compatible; B is the higher value.

### F8 — MOOD: eighteen hidden numbers is nine too many
Satiation is earned and legible. Mood is a per-god hidden random walk whose
stated purpose is capriciousness — but the front system, the banked clocks, and
curse rolls already provide variance. A hidden die that turns "I did everything
right" into a curse is the classic frustration generator: the player cannot
distinguish *divine personality* from *unfair RNG* unless mood is readable.
- **Option A — cut Mood entirely;** keep satiation + clocks. Simplest, most
  legible; loses the "gods are persons" texture.
- **Option B — Mood becomes weather you can read but not control:** keep the
  walk, but surface it *only* through the ambient gesture layer (the hum sours,
  a door hesitates, the lamps flicker in his palette) and the Narrator's
  adjectives. Never a number. The player learns to read the ship the way a
  sailor reads the sky.
- **Option C — Mood only for the front god:** one live temperament,
  theatrically prominent; the other eight sleep.
- Recommendation: **B with C's emphasis** (the front god's mood gets the
  loudest tells). Trade-off: B needs the gesture layer built early — but the
  2026-08-29 sketch already rules gestures in, so Mood becomes the *reason* the
  gesture layer exists rather than decoration on top of it.

## III. Gameplay — choice, consequence, surprise, frustration

### F9 — SIGNATURE: attribution is the hinge of the entire fantasy
"Unmasked: a raid arrives already knowing your positions — no warning letter"
is a superb curse *only if the player knows it was Ishko.* Otherwise it is
indistinguishable from Randy being Randy, and the edifice collapses into
RimWorld-with-extra-debuffs. Every divine act needs a fingerprint that lands
**before** the reading: per-god letter livery (icon, color, phrase cadence —
the epithets are already perfect for this), per-god light palette (ruled in),
and where possible the event itself carries the mark (Sh'kaar's raids arrive
*burning*; Zizzik's cascade always starts from a machine; Ishko's blessing is
an *absence* the Narrator points at — "the raid that did not come"). Rule to
adopt matrix-wide: **no unsigned act.** Even "no warning letter" curses get a
post-hoc signature line ("Only afterward do you understand whose silence that
was"). Cheap — authoring discipline, not mechanism — and the single
highest-leverage item in this review.

### F10 — EXIT-VERBS: curses must open doors, not just close them
Audit the matrix: Oomo's L curse ends "*until a feast breaks it*" and Ta'Baa's
ends "*until something FLIES*" — perfect, because the curse *names its own
price* and the player's next move is a plan, not a sulk. But most S/M curses
are flat stat-downs (Dry Mouth, Heavy Feet, Bad Faith, Loose Screws) — pure
closure. Adopt as a format rule: **every M and L curse carries an exit-verb** —
the placation act that lifts it, stated in the curse letter, priced in the
offended god's own currency (The Ledger Closes… *until a debt is collected*;
Everything Breaks… *until the reign's finest fix is enshrined*; The Hands
Refuse… *until a droid is honored*). S curses may stay ambient. Trade-off:
exit-verbs make curses solvable, which softens dread — so price the exits high
enough to hurt, and let the L tier's exit sometimes be genuinely awful
(Sh'kaar's exit is always someone's death; that is the point of him).

### F11 — THE BANK must be visible, and spendable
Zizzik's banked waking is the boldest mechanic in the doc and the likeliest
rage-quit: punishing months of *competent play* with an unannounced cascade
teaches "skill is a trap," which no game survives. Two amendments make it the
best mechanic instead:
- **Visible debt:** the slumber has tells that scale with the bank — the
  rattle-you-can-never-locate grows more frequent in the soundscape, the
  Narrator's dread lines sharpen, the crew whisper. A horror-movie meter the
  player *feels* rising. Never a number; always a mood.
- **The controlled waking:** a rite that spends the bank *on the player's
  schedule* — choose when, take a shaped M-sized disaster (category known,
  details not) instead of the unshaped L. Deliberate offerings already exist on
  his page; this is their crown, and it converts "manage *when*, not *whether*"
  from doc-prose into an actual decision — the colony girding itself,
  evacuating the nursery, and *pulling the cord*. A campaign-story generator by
  itself.
- Trade-off: a spendable bank is an optimizable bank; keep the controlled
  waking's cost real (it still hurts, it feeds Sh'kaar a little, and Zizzik
  remembers being managed — a small permanent grievance).

### F12 — DOUBLE-BILLING: two gods and the storyteller must not all charge for one sin
Sh'kaar's SEARING ("next raid massive, burning, early"), Ozzik's Exposure
("raid points calculate higher"), and Ishko's visibility curses all touch raid
math — *on top of* RimWorld's own wealth-based scaling, which the
anti-exponential pillar already fights. Stacked naively, a proud fought-out
colony gets billed four times and experiences it as the game cheating. Clean
fix (and a "world IS different" win — see F18): **replace, don't stack** — the
mod takes ownership of threat-point modulation into one number the gods
contest.

### F13 — OZZIK needs skill expression, not just a tax
Canon promises "rising *competently* is the road out" — but as specced, high
Ozzik is a flat upward bias on the evils. There is no competent way to be
proud; there is only *less pride*. The trap needs pressure-release valves so
mastery, not abstinence, is the redemptive path:
- **Option A — THE UNBURDENING (potlatch):** a rite converting wealth into
  standing — give away or ritually destroy riches, and the pride-meter vents.
  Anthropologically perfect for scavengers (wealth that cannot flee with you
  was never wealth), a natural late-game economy sink, and it splits the
  pantheon deliciously (Mob'Unloo *hates* it; Rekko demands the destroyed goods
  be truly unrepairable; Ta'Baa loves the lightened hold).
- **Option B — DEDICATION:** finish a great work *in another god's name* — the
  act happens, satiation routes to the dedicatee, Ozzik takes only a sliver
  (and a sliver of insult). Lets an ambitious player steer the pantheon like
  ballast. Trade-off: risks becoming a rote laundering trick — cap dedications
  per reign.
- **Option C — the competence check made literal:** the exposure bias scales
  not with Ozzik's level alone but with **Ozzik minus Ishko** — pride covered
  by discipline is survivable pride; the exposed number is the gap. Elegant
  (rise boldly IF you also hide well — raid-and-return doctrine as arithmetic)
  and it gives the player a real strategy: every ambition spike must be
  pre-paid in stillness. **The favorite single mechanical proposal of this
  review.**
- Recommendation: A + C together; B optional.
- **Also: the ion-over-explosives armory doctrine fell out of the matrix
  entirely** — one of the most concrete hooks (a religion that dictates your
  weapon loadout is felt every single fight) and no page carries it. Restore as
  Ozzik taboo-S (explosives) / deed+ (ion captures), and give Ta'Baa his
  canon-promised love of the escape-bomb as a deed+.

### F14 — REIGN-PARKING: the pantheon must not have a comfort setting
The counter-forces (Ta'Baa's clock, Zizzik's bank, skip-decay) probably prevent
"keep Ishko in front forever," but *probably* is not a design. Two additions:
- **The jealous watchers:** while one god fronts, the most-starved rival
  accrues grievance; past a threshold he forces a **challenge** — a mid-reign
  event where the player either yields the front (peaceful transition, small
  tribute) or holds it (the challenger's next curse tier is pre-armed). The
  eight silent observers stop being wallpaper and become a court, with
  politics.
- **Decadence:** a god who fronts too long grows *sated* — boons thin, demands
  inflate. Even a beloved reign has natural senescence.
- Trade-off: both push rotation; tune so a player *can* fight to keep a beloved
  front at escalating cost — forced rotation with no counterplay is its own
  frustration.

### F15 — FIRST-CONTACT CHAINS: author the nine openings deliberately
The shock→curiosity→delight→realization→strategy chain should be a *specced
artifact*, one page per god — the scripted-feeling (but emergent) first arc of
each relationship. Example, Zizzik's: **shock** — a pawn goes berserk and
wrecks the fabricator; before anyone is drafted the ship's lights *pulse,
warmly* (what?). **Curiosity** — the Narrator: "Somewhere below the deckplates,
something is delighted." **Delight** — a day later, an inspiration fires
(Creative Sparks; his S boon as a thank-you). **Realization** — this religion
*wants my colony a little broken*. **Strategy** — stop insta-suppressing breaks
during his stirrings; keep a decoy machine unrepaired; start planning *when* he
wakes. Writing all nine chains is the cheapest way to guarantee the fantasy
actually happens in the first ten hours instead of remaining latent in the
systems.

## IV. "This isn't normal RimWorld anymore" — the world-redefinition layer

The current design redefines the *ship* and the *event stream* but leaves the
planet, the interface, and time itself speaking vanilla.

### F16 — GEOGRAPHY IS THEOLOGY, and the fixed map makes it free
The world is tidally locked (which is also why the description doc's "sun that
never sets" vs. the canon block's "twin desert suns" contradiction must finally
be ruled — flag re-raised). Tidal lock means: **Sh'kaar owns half the planet.
Ishko owns the other half. The terminator is the eternal battlefield of the
pantheon's oldest hatred — and the Jawa live in the contested dark.** The
planet is not a backdrop for the religion; it is the religion's *diagram*, and
since Ash'karr is hand-authored and frozen it can be authored that way on
purpose: Oomo's country is the water tiles, Rekko's the ruin-fields,
Mob'Unloo's the trade roads, Ta'Baa's the open sand between, Zizzik's the
broken places. The engine already reads tiles for Oomo and Ishko — generalize:
**every tile has a divine reading, and the judgement-at-landing opens with
"whose country is this."** Landing-site selection becomes a theological act; a
pilgrimage sunward is a literal walk into the god of inevitability's mouth.
Cost: modest (a tile→god annotation pass over a map already being authored,
plus judgement text). Payoff: the player looks at the world map and sees a
pantheon. Nobody has ever shipped that.

### F17 — THE INTERFACE SPEAKS THE FAITH
Cheap, compounding reskins that make the player *think in* the theology because
the game refuses to speak anything else:
- **Time:** the date line becomes "Third day of Rekko's reign." Jawa do not
  count days; they count reigns. (Trivial patch, permanent immersion.)
- **Letters:** a Narrator rewrite layer re-titles vanilla events through the
  current front's lens — "Psychic drone" arrives as "*Zizzik scratches at
  minds*"; "Eclipse" as "*Ishko's hour — the Searing One is humiliated*." Same
  game, re-described, until the description *is* the game. Possibly the highest
  value-per-line-of-code item in the whole spec.
- **Inspect panes:** salvageable wrecks read "a sleeping hand (Rekko)" — sacred
  tags visible where the player already looks.
- Trade-off: reskin fatigue is real if it is *everything* — reserve the
  treatment for what the faith genuinely has opinions about; neutral things
  stay neutral (the gods do not care about your horseshoe pin, and their
  silence about it is characterization too).

### F18 — ONE DIAL TO MAKE IT ALL LEGIBLE: VISIBILITY
Ishko's entire boon/curse column leans on a "detection clock" that does not
exist in vanilla — so build it, and build it *big*: **Colony Visibility as a
first-class, player-facing stat** that half the pantheon fights over (Ozzik and
broadcasts raise it, Ishko and blackout lower it, Sh'kaar's meter multiplies
it, Ta'Baa's launch resets it) and that *owns* threat-point modulation (solving
F12's double-billing in the same stroke). One dial, nine hands on it. The
mechanical spine of "we are hiders" — the stat vanilla RimWorld conspicuously
lacks and this campaign conspicuously is *about*.

### F19 — THE COSMOLOGY HAS A TENTH POSITION, and an ending hiding in it
The 2026-08-30 Narrator ruling quietly completed the cosmology: **the One
(dead, narrating) · the Nine (alive, arguing) · the Clan (choosing)**. The
pantheon is a shattered monotheism and the Narrator is the ghost of the whole.
The endgame is currently "which alter wins the Body" — but the humble bloc's
theology implies a third answer the win-path map does not yet honor: **no one
wins; the argument kept honest *is* the god.** "We are always losing
gracefully" is already scripture — make graceful loss a victory lane: the
balance-keeper ending, where the clan that never let any voice conquer the hull
achieves what the Rakatan machine never could and the Empire never would — a
*plural* mind at peace with remaining plural. The anti-exponential pillar as
eschatology, thematically the truest ending, and a destination for the player
who refuses every trap. Trade-off: a "balance" win condition needs sharp
criteria or it reads as "do nothing long enough" — define it as *surviving all
four clocks through their full cycles* (every god fronted at least once, no
reign-breaker unanswered, Zizzik woken deliberately N times…): mastery of the
whole wheel, not avoidance of it.

## V. Canon & matrix hygiene (short, actionable)

1. **§4/§4c/§4d of the engine doc still carry Ohm-as-Oracle /
   Ohm-believes-he-is-the-ship with no supersession pointers** to the
   2026-08-30 re-scope living 500 lines below. One line into each section. Same
   for §5c's "voices from the speakers" (now Narrator-voiced — or is the Tiki
   Room chorus preserved? needs a ruling; recommendation: preserve it, Narrator
   as compère).
2. **The restraining-bolt / ship-rebellion arc is orphaned** by the re-scope —
   it was built on Ohm-as-ship and is too good to lose. Cleanest re-homing:
   rebellion becomes a *pantheon* phenomenon — a starved front-god seizing
   actuators (F14's challenge mechanic at its violent extreme), with the bolts
   as the clan's desperate answer to any god, not just Ohm.
3. **Twin suns vs. sun-that-never-sets** — the standing flagged contradiction;
   F16 makes it load-bearing, so it is ripe to rule.
4. **§19.5 borderline items** in the matrix, for a deliberate yes/no: Ohm-L
   (machines self-repair = free components?), Oomo-L (twins/kin joining — pawns
   are material), Mob'Unloo-L (the fence). Recommendation: rule all three legal
   (opportunity-shaped, rare) — but ruled, not drifted into.
5. **Weapon doctrine (ion/explosives) missing from the matrix** — see F13;
   restore to Ozzik's and Ta'Baa's pages.
6. **Precept layer vs. matrix layer:** decide which owns what — permanent law
   (never-nude, sacred scrap) lives in the ideoligion precepts; *rotating* law
   lives in reigns. Stated once, it prevents a whole class of future
   double-spec. (Per the ideoligion constraints, the deity layer cannot live in
   vanilla ideo XML at all — `deityCount 0` — so the matrix mod owns the gods
   outright, as the plan already assumes.)

## VI. What to pull forward into the near-spec, ranked

1. **SIGNATURE (F9)** + **EXIT-VERBS (F10)** — format rules; adopt now, they
   retrofit the shipped matrix in one editing pass.
2. **VISIBILITY dial (F18)** — the mechanical spine; solves F12 too.
3. **Ozzik−Ishko exposure arithmetic + the Unburdening (F13)** — turns the trap
   into strategy.
4. **Zizzik's visible bank + controlled waking (F11)** — turns the scariest
   mechanic into the best one.
5. **Discovery onboarding + nine first-contact chains (F4, F15)** — where the
   shock→…→strategy chains are actually manufactured.
6. **Divine dilemma events + the 3×3 frame (F5, F6)** — the trinary made
   playable.
7. **Divine geography (F16)** + **interface reskins (F17)** — the "world IS the
   religion" layer.
8. **Jealous watchers / challenges (F14)**, **contested credit / Rekko⇄Ozzik
   dial (F7)**, **Mood-as-weather (F8)**, **attenuation (F2)**, **balance-keeper
   ending (F19)** — the deepening tier.
